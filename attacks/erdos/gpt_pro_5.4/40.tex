\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#40 --- Round 5}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-5 OBJECTIVE}
\textbf{Path (C): obstruction / correction by ruling out the natural reduction from the remaining $B_2[\lambda]$-core to the Sidon problem.}

Round~4 reduced the unresolved subpolynomial sector of Problem~\#40 to the existence of an
infinite $B_2[\lambda]$ set whose counting function is $N^{1/2-o(1)}$ in the strong
``for all sufficiently large $N$'' sense. The most promising next step is therefore to test
whether this remaining $B_2[\lambda]$-problem can be collapsed to the Sidon problem
(Problem~\#39) by a structural theorem.

In this round I show that two of the most natural reduction principles are \emph{false}:
\begin{itemize}
\item bounded-representation sets need \emph{not} be finite unions of Sidon sets;
\item even a strong \emph{local} Sidon-richness property (every finite subset contains a large
Sidon subset) still does \emph{not} force such a decomposition.
\end{itemize}
Thus the Round~4 exact core is genuinely broader than the Sidon case, and any eventual
reduction to Problem~\#39 would require a new global density-preserving theorem of a different kind.

\subsection*{2) ROUND-4 FOUNDATION USED}
I rely on the following Round~4 results without re-proving them.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Round~4 exact subpolynomial reformulation:} Problem~\#40 fails for some
subpolynomial $g(N)\to\infty$ if and only if there exists an integer $\lambda\ge 1$ and an
infinite set $A\subset\mathbb N$ with $A\in B_2[\lambda]$ and
\[
|A\cap[1,N]|\gg_{\varepsilon} N^{1/2-\varepsilon}
\qquad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
\item \textbf{Round~4 one-way link:} a positive answer to Problem~\#39 implies a negative answer
to Problem~\#40 for some subpolynomial $g$.
\item \textbf{Round~4 gap:} the converse direction remained open; in particular, it was unclear
whether the Round~4 $B_2[\lambda]$-core was essentially equivalent to the Sidon problem.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-5)}
The new ingredient is a recent theorem of Reiher--R\"odl--Schacht on
\emph{Ramsey-type generalised Sidon sets}. Specialised to $k=2$, it yields the following.

\begin{theorem}[External theorem, $k=2$ case]\label{thm:RRS}
For every integer $\ell\ge 2$ there exists an infinite set $X\subset\mathbb N$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $X$ is a $B_2[\ell]$ set, i.e. every integer has at most $\ell$ nondecreasing
representations $n=x_1+x_2$ with $x_1\le x_2$ and $x_1,x_2\in X$;
\item[(ii)] for every finite colouring $X=X_1\cup\cdots\cup X_r$ there exists a colour class
$X_q$ for which some integer has exactly $\ell$ nondecreasing representations as a sum of two
members of $X_q$;
\item[(iii)] every finite subset $Y\subset X$ contains a Sidon subset $Z\subset Y$ with
\[
|Z|\ge \frac14 |Y|.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:RRS} is new relative to the previous rounds. It gives a bounded-representation
set that is \emph{locally very Sidon-rich}, yet whose finite colourings always contain a colour
class with repeated two-sums.

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-5)}
The exact gap left after Round~4 was:
\begin{quote}
Can the remaining $B_2[\lambda]$-core be reduced to Problem~\#39 by converting bounded-representation
sets into Sidon sets?
\end{quote}

Round~5 attacks this by testing two precise reduction principles.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P1)] For each fixed $\lambda\ge 2$, every $B_2[\lambda]$ set is a finite union of Sidon sets.
\item[(P2)] There exists $\delta>0$ such that every set $X\subset\mathbb N$ with the local
property
\[
\forall\text{ finite }Y\subset X\ \exists\text{ Sidon }Z\subset Y\text{ with }|Z|\ge \delta |Y|
\]
is a finite union of Sidon sets.
\end{enumerate}

If either principle were true, then the Round~4 $B_2[\lambda]$-core would be much closer to
Problem~\#39. I will prove that both are false, with (P2) already false for $\delta=1/4$.
This rules out an entire class of natural local-to-global reduction strategies.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-5)}
\paragraph{5.1. Notation.}
For $S\subset\mathbb N$ let
\[
\rho_{S,2}(n):=|\{(a,b)\in S^2:a\le b,\ a+b=n\}|.
\]
Thus $S$ is Sidon exactly when $\sup_n \rho_{S,2}(n)\le 1$, and $S\in B_2[\ell]$ exactly when
$\sup_n \rho_{S,2}(n)\le \ell$.

\paragraph{5.2. A structural obstruction.}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mainobstruction}
For every integer $\ell\ge 2$ there exists an infinite set $X\subset\mathbb N$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $X\in B_2[\ell]$;
\item[(b)] $X$ is \emph{not} a finite union of Sidon sets;
\item[(c)] every finite subset $Y\subset X$ contains a Sidon subset $Z\subset Y$ with
$|Z|\ge |Y|/4$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\ell\ge 2$, and let $X$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:RRS}.
Property (a) is exactly part (i) of Theorem~\ref{thm:RRS}, while property (c) is exactly part (iii).

It remains to prove (b). Suppose for contradiction that
\[
X=S_1\cup\cdots\cup S_r
\]
for some finite family of Sidon sets $S_1,\dots,S_r$.
Since this is only a covering, not necessarily a partition, define a colouring
$c:X\to [r]$ by choosing for each $x\in X$ one index $c(x)$ with $x\in S_{c(x)}$.
Let
\[
X_i:=\{x\in X:c(x)=i\}\subset S_i
\qquad (1\le i\le r).
\]
Each colour class $X_i$ is a subset of the Sidon set $S_i$, hence itself Sidon. Therefore
\[
\sup_n \rho_{X_i,2}(n)\le 1
\qquad\text{for every }i=1,\dots,r.
\]
But Theorem~\ref{thm:RRS}(ii) says that for \emph{every} finite colouring of $X$ there is a colour
class, say $X_q$, for which some integer has exactly $\ell$ nondecreasing two-term
representations inside $X_q$. Equivalently,
\[
\sup_n \rho_{X_q,2}(n)\ge \ell\ge 2,
\]
contradicting that $X_q$ is Sidon.
This contradiction proves (b).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{5.3. Two false reduction principles.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:mainobstruction} immediately yields the following.

\begin{corollary}[Failure of finite Sidon decomposition]\label{cor:P1false}
For every integer $\lambda\ge 2$, the following statement is false:
\begin{quote}
Every $B_2[\lambda]$ set is a finite union of Sidon sets.
\end{quote}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\ell=\lambda$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:mainobstruction}. Then $X\in B_2[\lambda]$ but
$X$ is not a finite union of Sidon sets.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Failure of the Alon--Erd\H{o}s local-to-global principle]\label{cor:P2false}
The following statement is false, already with $\delta=1/4$:
\begin{quote}
If $X\subset\mathbb N$ is such that every finite subset $Y\subset X$ contains a Sidon subset
$Z\subset Y$ with $|Z|\ge \delta |Y|$, then $X$ is a finite union of Sidon sets.
\end{quote}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Again use Proposition~\ref{prop:mainobstruction}. The set $X$ there satisfies the stated local
property with $\delta=1/4$, but is not a finite union of Sidon sets.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{5.4. Consequence for Problem~\#40.}
Round~4 identified the unresolved core of Problem~\#40 as the possible existence of a dense
$B_2[\lambda]$ set with counting function $N^{1/2-o(1)}$. Corollaries~\ref{cor:P1false} and
\ref{cor:P2false} show that this core cannot be collapsed to Problem~\#39 by either of the two
most obvious mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] decomposing bounded-representation sets into finitely many Sidon pieces;
\item[(ii)] proving only a local positive-density Sidon extraction statement for finite subsets.
\end{enumerate}

This yields the following corrected structural formulation.

\begin{corollary}[Minimal corrected obstruction statement]\label{cor:corrected}
The remaining open sector of Problem~\#40 is a genuinely \emph{generalised Sidon} problem.
More precisely, any reduction of the Round~4 exact core to Problem~\#39 must use a theorem
strictly stronger than both false principles above.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:P1false}, finite Sidon decomposition fails even inside the class $B_2[\lambda]$.
By Corollary~\ref{cor:P2false}, even strong local Sidon-richness does not force such a
global decomposition. Therefore neither principle can justify replacing the $B_2[\lambda]$-core
from Round~4 by the Sidon problem.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Compatibility of notation:} The external theorem uses $\rho_{X,2}(n)$ to count
nondecreasing representations, which is exactly the standard $B_2[\ell]$ convention used in the
previous rounds after converting from ordered counts.

\item \emph{Colouring vs covering:} In Proposition~\ref{prop:mainobstruction}(b), the sets
$S_1,\dots,S_r$ may overlap. Choosing for each $x\in X$ a single colour $c(x)$ produces colour
classes $X_i\subset S_i$, which is enough because subsets of Sidon sets are Sidon.

\item \emph{Local property is genuinely strong:} Corollary~\ref{cor:P2false} does not merely say
that some finite subsets of $X$ contain large Sidon subsets; it says \emph{every} finite subset does,
with a fixed proportion $1/4$.

\item \emph{No density conclusion is being smuggled in:} Theorem~\ref{thm:RRS} says nothing about
$|X\cap[1,N]|$, so Round~5 does \emph{not} solve the remaining density question of Problem~\#40.
It only rules out two natural ways of reducing that density question to the Sidon case.

\item \emph{Could there still be some deeper equivalence with Problem~\#39?} Yes. Round~5 does not
exclude all possible future reductions. It excludes only reductions based on finite Sidon
decomposition or on the local positive-density extraction principle above.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{7) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).}

Round~5 gives a substantive structural advance beyond Round~4:
\begin{quote}
The unresolved subpolynomial sector of Problem~\#40 is \emph{not} presently reducible to the
Sidon problem by the two most natural local-to-global mechanisms. In fact, for every
$\ell\ge 2$ there exists an infinite $B_2[\ell]$ set that is locally Sidon-rich (every finite subset
contains a Sidon subset of size at least one quarter) but is nevertheless not a finite union
of Sidon sets.
\end{quote}
Thus the remaining core of Problem~\#40 must be treated as a genuinely generalised Sidon
problem, not merely as Problem~\#39 in disguise.
