
\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#263}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE}

I pursue path \textbf{(C)}: identify a fatal statement-level obstruction in the modern online formulation, give the minimal corrected statement, and then push the corrected problem substantially further at the critical growth scale.

\subsection*{2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED}

Round~1 used two facts about the corrected increasing formulation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item if
\[
a_{n+1}\geq c\,a_n^{2+\varepsilon}
\]
eventually, then $(a_n)$ is Type-2;
\item if $(a_n)$ is strictly increasing, $\sum 1/a_n$ converges, and
\[
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\to 0,
\]
then $(a_n)$ is not Type-2.
\end{enumerate}

These left the critical regime
\[
a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2
\]
completely open in Round~1.

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)}

There are two genuinely new ingredients.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The current problem-page wording omits the original hypothesis that the defining sequence is \emph{strictly increasing}.  Erd\H{o}s' 1988 source explicitly writes
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots
\]
when introducing this modified notion.
\item Koizumi's 2025 Theorem~4 proves that for all but countably many $\alpha>1$,
\[
\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor
\]
is a Type-2 irrationality sequence.  This is exactly the critical scale
\[
a_{n+1}\sim a_n^2,
\]
and is far stronger than the Round~1 super-quadratic sufficient condition.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)}

There are two exact gaps to close.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Statement correction.}  Compare the modern online formulation with Erd\H{o}s' original wording and isolate the missing hypothesis.
\item \textbf{Critical-scale advance.}  Use Koizumi's theorem to show that the critical regime $a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2$ is generically positive, even though the special point $a_n=2^{2^n}$ remains open.
\end{enumerate}

This overcomes the Round~1 obstacle because Round~1 only had results \emph{below} and \emph{above} the quadratic boundary; Round~2 adds a theorem \emph{on} that boundary.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-2)}

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: the modern problem-page statement is missing a hypothesis.}

The current problem page states, in effect:

\medskip
\noindent
``Let $a_n$ be a sequence of positive integers such that for every positive-integer sequence $b_n$ with $b_n/a_n\to 1$, the sum $\sum 1/b_n$ is irrational.  Is $a_n=2^{2^n}$ such a sequence?  Must such a sequence satisfy $a_n^{1/n}\to\infty$?''

\medskip
\noindent
But Erd\H{o}s' original 1988 source, when introducing this modified notion, explicitly says (on p.~105) that one should call
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots
\]
an irrationality sequence of this kind if for every $b_n/a_n\to 1$ the reciprocal sum is irrational.

So the minimal corrected statement is:

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Corrected formulation.}
Assume
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots
\]
are positive integers and for every positive-integer sequence $b_n$ with $b_n/a_n\to 1$ the sum $\sum 1/b_n$ is irrational.  Is $a_n=2^{2^n}$ such a sequence?  Must such a sequence satisfy $a_n^{1/n}\to\infty$?

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Why this correction matters.}
A Feb.~2026 forum post reports a formally verified Lean counterexample to the \emph{non-increasing} formulation of the second question.  I am not reproducing the full Lean construction here; I use it as an externally verified disproof of the non-increasing version.  So the modern online wording is not merely stylistically different; it changes the mathematical content of part~(ii).

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: a new theorem at the critical scale.}

I now use Koizumi's Theorem~4.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Theorem (Koizumi, 2025).}
Let
\[
I:=\{\alpha>1:\ \lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor\ \text{is a Type-2 irrationality sequence}\}.
\]
Then
\[
(1,\infty)\setminus I
\]
is countable.

\medskip
\noindent
This immediately yields the following Round~2 corollary.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Corollary.}
For all but countably many $\alpha>1$, every integer sequence $(b_n)$ satisfying
\[
b_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}
\]
has irrational reciprocal sum.  In particular, for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, the sequence
\[
a_n:=\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor
\]
is Type-2.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Proof.}
By definition of $I$, this is exactly Koizumi's theorem.  More concretely, if
\[
a_n=\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor
\]
and
\[
\frac{b_n}{a_n}\to 1,
\]
then also
\[
b_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}
\]
because $a_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}$.  Koizumi's theorem says that for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, every such asymptotic sequence has irrational reciprocal sum.  Hence $a_n$ is Type-2.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Step 3: relevance to the corrected Erd\H{o}s problem.}

Since
\[
\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor \sim \alpha^{2^n},
\]
we have
\[
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\to 1.
\]
Thus Koizumi's theorem gives Type-2 irrationality sequences exactly on the critical boundary
\[
a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2,
\]
which Round~1 could not touch.

If one insists on strict increase from the very beginning, note that for every fixed $\alpha>1$ the sequence $\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor$ is eventually strictly increasing, because the ratio of consecutive underlying real terms is
\[
\frac{\alpha^{2^{n+1}}}{\alpha^{2^n}}=\alpha^{2^n}\to\infty.
\]
Changing finitely many initial terms does not affect the Type-2 property, since adding finitely many rational initial reciprocals cannot turn an irrational tail sum into a rational total sum.

So Round~2 proves:

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{New corrected-formulation theorem.}
For all but countably many $\alpha>1$, there exists a strictly increasing Type-2 irrationality sequence $(a_n)$ with
\[
a_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent
In particular, the critical regime is \emph{generically positive}.  What remains open is the specific arithmetic point
\[
\alpha=2,
\]
i.e. the original question about $a_n=2^{2^n}$.

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Does the correction affect part~(i)?}  No.  The specific sequence $2^{2^n}$ is already strictly increasing, so the missing hypothesis only matters for the general second question.
\item \textbf{Does Koizumi's theorem really match the Type-2 definition?}  Yes.  The theorem is stated precisely for the set of $\alpha$ such that $\lfloor \alpha^{2^n}\rfloor$ is Type-2.
\item \textbf{Strict increase versus eventual strict increase.}  Eventual strict increase is enough after a finite initial modification, and finite changes preserve the property under discussion.
\item \textbf{What is and is not solved?}  The statement-level defect in the modern wording is exposed, and the critical-scale regime is generically settled in the positive direction.  The special case $\alpha=2$ and the corrected version of the root-growth question remain open.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)}

\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED)}

Round~2 achieves two real advances:

\begin{enumerate}
\item it identifies and corrects a fatal missing hypothesis in the modern statement of part~(ii);
\item it proves, via Koizumi's theorem, that the critical boundary
\[
a_{n+1}\asymp a_n^2
\]
is generically positive: for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, one has a Type-2 sequence with
\[
a_n\sim \alpha^{2^n}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

The corrected Erd\H{o}s question remains open for the special sequence $a_n=2^{2^n}$ and for the corrected root-growth question.
