\section{Problem 15 --- Round 4}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-4 OBJECTIVE}

\textbf{(C) Obstruction / correction.}
I continue the \emph{obstruction/correction} track from Round~3.
Round~3 reduced the original Erd\H{o}s series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
to the parity-weighted series
\[
\sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m}
\]
and then to an exact Abel-type criterion involving the discrepancy
\[
A(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x}(-1)^{\pi(m)}.
\]
The natural Round~4 gap is therefore no longer the prime-indexed series itself, but the following sharper question:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Exactly when can a discrepancy bound of the form}
\[
A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
\emph{force convergence of the weighted series?}
\end{quote}

The most promising Round~4 direction is to settle this question \emph{at the level of generic sign sequences}. I will prove a sharp threshold theorem showing that, within the entire class of ``discrepancy-only'' arguments, the integral condition
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty
\]
is exactly the right scale: it is sufficient for convergence, and if it fails then one can explicitly build equidistributed sign sequences with discrepancy
\[
O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
for which the analogous weighted series diverges.

This is a genuine Round~4 advance beyond Round~3: it does not solve the prime problem, but it rules out an entire class of proof strategies in a near-optimal way and gives the minimal corrected statement for what a discrepancy-only method could possibly prove.

\subsection*{2) Round-3 FOUNDATION USED}

I use the following Round~3 results as fixed input.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Round~3 Theorem~6.2 (exact discrepancy criterion for the prime problem).}
Let
\[
A(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x}(-1)^{\pi(m)}.
\]
Then the original Erd\H{o}s series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
converges if and only if the parity series
\[
\sum_{m=3}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m}
\]
converges, and this in turn is equivalent to convergence of the discrepancy-weighted series/integral
\[
\sum_{m=3}^\infty A(m)\left(\frac{1}{m\log m}-\frac{1}{(m+1)\log(m+1)}\right)
\]
or equivalently
\[
\int_3^\infty A(t)\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)dt.
\]

\item \textbf{Round~3 Corollary~6.3 (sufficient discrepancy criterion).}
If
\[
A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
for some scale $L(x)\to\infty$ with
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty,
\]
then the original Erd\H{o}s series converges.

\item \textbf{Round~3 Proposition~6.4 (one explicit model obstruction).}
There exists an equidistributed sign sequence with discrepancy
\[
O\!\left(\frac{x}{\log\log x\,\log\log\log x}\right)
\]
for which the weighted series
\[
\sum \frac{\sigma(m)}{m\log m}
\]
diverges.
\end{enumerate}

I will not re-prove these Round~3 results. Round~4 begins from them and sharpens them.

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-4)}

The new ingredient is a \emph{sharp threshold theorem for discrepancy-only methods}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item I extend the Round~3 Abel criterion from the specific prime-parity sequence $(-1)^{\pi(m)}$ to \emph{arbitrary} sign sequences $\sigma(m)\in\{\pm1\}$.

\item I prove a converse construction: whenever a scale $L(x)$ is too weak in the sense that
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}=\infty,
\]
there exists an explicit equidistributed sign sequence with discrepancy
\[
O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
whose weighted series diverges.

\item Combining the two, I obtain the minimal corrected statement:
\begin{quote}
For discrepancy-only proofs, the integral threshold
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty
\]
is optimal (up to mild monotonicity assumptions on $x/L(x)$).
\end{quote}
\end{enumerate}

This is strictly stronger than Round~3, which only supplied one sufficient criterion and one special counterexample scale.

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-4)}

After Round~3, the exact unresolved structural question was:

\begin{quote}
\emph{How sharp is the discrepancy criterion? In particular, which bounds on}
\[
A(x)=\sum_{3\le m\le x}(-1)^{\pi(m)}
\]
\emph{could ever suffice if one tries to prove convergence using only Abel summation and discrepancy estimates?}
\end{quote}

To close that gap, I will:

\begin{enumerate}
\item prove a generic Abel-summation theorem for arbitrary sign sequences;
\item prove a generic converse construction showing that the Round~3 sufficient condition is essentially optimal;
\item apply the result back to the prime problem via Round~3 Theorem~6.2, thereby ruling out an entire class of proof strategies.
\end{enumerate}

This directly overcomes the Round~3 obstacle: instead of asking for a single ad hoc example, it identifies the exact integrability threshold that controls all discrepancy-only approaches.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-4)}

\subsubsection*{5.1. A generic Abel identity for weighted sign series}

Let
\[
w_n:=\frac{1}{n\log n}\qquad (n\ge 3),
\]
and let $\sigma_n$ be any real sequence. Define its partial sums by
\[
B(N):=\sum_{n=3}^N \sigma_n.
\]

\textbf{Lemma 7.1 (generic Abel identity).}
For every integer $N\ge 3$,
\[
\sum_{n=3}^N \sigma_n w_n
=
B(N)w_N + \sum_{n=3}^{N-1} B(n)(w_n-w_{n+1}).
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
This is the standard summation-by-parts identity applied to $a_n=\sigma_n$ and $b_n=w_n$:
\[
\sum_{n=3}^N a_nb_n = A(N)b_N + \sum_{n=3}^{N-1} A(n)(b_n-b_{n+1}),
\]
where $A(n)=\sum_{j=3}^n a_j=B(n)$. Substituting $a_n=\sigma_n$ and $b_n=w_n$ gives the claim. \qed

\medskip

\textbf{Lemma 7.2 (kernel asymptotics).}
There exist absolute constants $0<c<C<\infty$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$,
\[
\frac{c}{n^2\log n}
\le
w_n-w_{n+1}
\le
\frac{C}{n^2\log n}.
\]
More precisely,
\[
w_n-w_{n+1}
=
\int_n^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)dt.
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
The function $w(t)=1/(t\log t)$ is differentiable for $t>1$, and
\[
-w'(t)=\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}.
\]
Hence
\[
w_n-w_{n+1} = \int_n^{n+1} -w'(t)\,dt
= \int_n^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log t}+\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}\right)dt.
\]
Since on $[n,n+1]$ one has $t\asymp n$ and $\log t\asymp \log n$, the stated two-sided bound follows. \qed

\subsubsection*{5.2. Generic sufficiency at a given discrepancy scale}

The next statement extends Round~3 Corollary~6.3 from the prime-parity sequence to arbitrary sign sequences.

\textbf{Proposition 7.3 (generic discrepancy-only sufficiency).}
Let $L:[3,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be eventually increasing with $L(x)\to\infty$. Let $\sigma_n\in\{\pm1\}$ and write
\[
B(N):=\sum_{n=3}^N \sigma_n.
\]
Assume
\[
B(N)=O\!\left(\frac{N}{L(N)}\right)
\qquad (N\to\infty)
\]
and
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty.
\]
Then the series
\[
\sum_{n=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}
\]
converges.

\textbf{Proof.}
By Lemma~7.1,
\[
\sum_{n=3}^N \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}
=
\frac{B(N)}{N\log N}
+
\sum_{n=3}^{N-1} B(n)(w_n-w_{n+1}).
\]
The boundary term tends to zero because
\[
\left|\frac{B(N)}{N\log N}\right|
\ll \frac{1}{L(N)\log N}
\to 0.
\]
Also, by Lemma~7.2 and the hypothesis on $B(n)$,
\[
|B(n)|(w_n-w_{n+1})
\ll
\frac{n/L(n)}{n^2\log n}
=
\frac{1}{n\log n\,L(n)}.
\]
Because $L$ is eventually increasing, the function $1/(x\log x\,L(x))$ is eventually decreasing, so the integral test yields
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3}\frac{1}{n\log n\,L(n)}<\infty.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3}|B(n)|(w_n-w_{n+1})<\infty,
\]
so the Abel-transformed series converges absolutely. Therefore
\[
\sum_{n=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}
\]
converges. \qed

\subsubsection*{5.3. A sharp converse model construction}

I now prove the key new theorem of Round~4. It subsumes the special Round~3 counterexample and shows that the preceding sufficiency criterion is essentially best possible for discrepancy-only arguments.

\textbf{Theorem 7.4 (sharpness of discrepancy-only criteria for generic sign sequences).}
Let $L:[3,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be eventually increasing with $L(x)\to\infty$, and set
\[
D(x):=\frac{x}{L(x)}.
\]
Assume in addition that $D$ is eventually nondecreasing. If
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)} = \infty,
\]
then there exists a sign sequence $\sigma_n\in\{\pm1\}$ such that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] its discrepancy satisfies
\[
\sum_{n=3}^N \sigma_n = 2D(N)+O(1)
\qquad (N\to\infty),
\]
and in particular
\[
\sum_{n=3}^N \sigma_n = O\!\left(\frac{N}{L(N)}\right);
\]

\item[(ii)] the sequence is equidistributed (because $D(N)=o(N)$);

\item[(iii)] the weighted series diverges to $+\infty$:
\[
\sum_{n=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}=+\infty.
\]
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proof.}
Because $L(x)\to\infty$ and $L$ is eventually increasing, there exists an odd integer $N_0\ge 3$ such that for all $x\ge N_0-2$,
\[
L(x)>3.
\]
For such $x$ we have
\[
D(x+2)-D(x)
=
\frac{x+2}{L(x+2)}-\frac{x}{L(x)}
\le
\frac{x+2}{L(x)}-\frac{x}{L(x)}
=
\frac{2}{L(x)}<1.
\]
Since $D$ is assumed eventually nondecreasing, it follows that for all sufficiently large $x$,
\[
0\le D(x+2)-D(x)<1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Now define a set $Y$ of odd integers by
\[
Y:=\left\{n\ge N_0: n\text{ odd and }\lfloor D(n)\rfloor>\lfloor D(n-2)\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Because of \eqref{7.1}, each floor difference is either $0$ or $1$.
Let
\[
C(X):=\#\{n\in Y: n\le X\}.
\]
If $X^*$ denotes the largest odd integer $\le X$, then by telescoping,
\[
C(X)
=
\sum_{\substack{N_0\le n\le X^*\\ n\text{ odd}}}
\bigl(\lfloor D(n)\rfloor-\lfloor D(n-2)\rfloor\bigr)
=
\lfloor D(X^*)\rfloor-\lfloor D(N_0-2)\rfloor.
\]
Since $0\le D(X)-D(X^*)<1$ for large $X$, we obtain
\[
C(X)=D(X)+O(1).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Define the sign sequence by flipping the alternating sequence on $Y$:
\[
\sigma_n:=(-1)^n + 2\mathbf 1_Y(n).
\]
Because $Y$ consists only of odd integers, $\sigma_n\in\{\pm1\}$ for every $n$.
Its discrepancy is
\[
B(N):=\sum_{n=3}^N \sigma_n
=
\sum_{n=3}^N (-1)^n + 2C(N).
\]
The alternating partial sums are bounded, so by \eqref{7.2},
\[
B(N)=2D(N)+O(1).
\]
This proves (i). Since $D(N)=N/L(N)=o(N)$, we also have
\[
B(N)=o(N),
\]
which is exactly equidistribution of the two signs; thus (ii) holds.

It remains to prove (iii). Write
\[
\sum_{n=3}^N \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}
=
\sum_{n=3}^N \frac{(-1)^n}{n\log n}
+
2\sum_{\substack{n\in Y\\ n\le N}} \frac{1}{n\log n}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The first term converges by the alternating series test, so it suffices to show that
\[
\sum_{n\in Y} \frac{1}{n\log n}=\infty.
\]
By summation by parts,
\[
\sum_{\substack{n\in Y\\ n\le N}} w_n
=
C(N)w_N + \sum_{m=N_0}^{N-1} C(m)(w_m-w_{m+1}).
\tag{7.4}
\]
The boundary term tends to zero because, by \eqref{7.2},
\[
C(N)w_N
\ll
\frac{D(N)}{N\log N}
=
\frac{1}{L(N)\log N}
\to 0.
\]
Also, again by \eqref{7.2},
\[
\sum_{m=N_0}^{N-1} C(m)(w_m-w_{m+1})
=
\sum_{m=N_0}^{N-1} D(m)(w_m-w_{m+1}) + O\!\left(\sum_{m=N_0}^{\infty}(w_m-w_{m+1})\right).
\tag{7.5}
\]
The error term is finite because the latter series telescopes.
Thus it is enough to prove that
\[
\sum_{m\ge N_0} D(m)(w_m-w_{m+1})=\infty.
\tag{7.6}
\]
By Lemma~7.2,
\[
D(m)(w_m-w_{m+1}) \asymp \frac{D(m)}{m^2\log m}
=
\frac{1}{m\log m\,L(m)}.
\]
Because $L$ is eventually increasing, the latter sequence is eventually decreasing, so the integral test applies. Since by hypothesis
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}=\infty,
\]
we conclude that
\[
\sum_{m\ge N_0} \frac{1}{m\log m\,L(m)}=\infty,
\]
and therefore \eqref{7.6} holds. Returning to \eqref{7.4} and \eqref{7.5}, we obtain
\[
\sum_{n\in Y} \frac{1}{n\log n}=\infty.
\]
Now \eqref{7.3} shows that
\[
\sum_{n=3}^\infty \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}=+\infty,
\]
proving (iii). \qed

\subsubsection*{5.4. Consequence for the original Erd\H{o}s problem}

The generic theorem above does not by itself solve the prime problem, but it gives a sharp obstruction for an entire proof strategy.

\textbf{Corollary 7.5 (optimality of discrepancy-only methods for Problem~15).}
Let
\[
A(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x} (-1)^{\pi(m)}.
\]
Suppose one seeks to prove convergence of
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
using only a bound of the form
\[
A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
for some eventually increasing scale $L(x)\to\infty$ with $x/L(x)$ eventually nondecreasing.
Then:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] if
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty,
\]
then such a discrepancy bound \emph{would} suffice to prove convergence (by Round~3 Theorem~6.2 and Proposition~7.3);

\item[(b)] if
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}=\infty,
\]
then no argument based solely on that generic discrepancy scale can be sharp in full generality, because Theorem~7.4 produces equidistributed model sign sequences with the same discrepancy scale for which the analogous weighted series diverges.
\end{enumerate}

Hence the threshold
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty
\]
is the minimal corrected statement for what a discrepancy-only strategy can hope to prove.

\medskip

In particular, Round~3's special model obstruction at the scale
\[
L(x)=\log\log x\,\log\log\log x
\]
is now seen to be a single instance of a much more general sharp theorem.

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

I now try to break the new Round~4 arguments.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Does Theorem~7.4 really produce a \emph{set}, not a multiset?}
Yes.
Because $0\le D(x+2)-D(x)<1$ for large $x$, the floor difference
\[
\lfloor D(n)\rfloor-\lfloor D(n-2)\rfloor
\]
is always either $0$ or $1$.
So at each odd index we either place one point or none; no multiplicities occur.

\item \textbf{Is the count $C(X)=D(X)+O(1)$ really valid?}
Yes.
The exact telescoping identity gives
\[
C(X)=\lfloor D(X^*)\rfloor-\lfloor D(N_0-2)\rfloor,
\]
where $X^*$ is the largest odd integer $\le X$.
Since $D$ changes by less than $1$ across any interval of length $2$, replacing $X^*$ by $X$ changes $D$ by $O(1)$.
Thus $C(X)=D(X)+O(1)$.

\item \textbf{Could the divergence proof fail because the comparison with $1/(m\log mL(m))$ is too weak?}
No.
Lemma~7.2 gives the two-sided estimate
\[
D(m)(w_m-w_{m+1})\asymp \frac{1}{m\log m\,L(m)}.
\]
Since $L$ is eventually increasing, the comparison sequence is eventually decreasing, so the integral test is legitimate.

\item \textbf{Is equidistribution automatic?}
Yes.
The discrepancy of the constructed sign sequence is
\[
B(N)=2D(N)+O(1)=O\!\left(\frac{N}{L(N)}\right),
\]
and because $L(N)\to\infty$, this is $o(N)$.
That is exactly the statement that the two signs occur with asymptotic density $1/2$.

\item \textbf{Did Round~4 improperly discard Round~3's prime-specific structure?}
No.
Round~4 does not replace the prime problem by an unrelated model; rather, it uses Round~3 Theorem~6.2 to identify the prime problem with one special weighted sign sequence, and then proves a general theorem showing what can and cannot follow from discrepancy information alone.
This is precisely the kind of obstruction analysis requested in path~(C).

\item \textbf{What mild regularity is still assumed?}
The converse model theorem assumes that $L$ is eventually increasing and that $D(x)=x/L(x)$ is eventually nondecreasing.
This covers the natural scales that arise in practice (for instance powers of $\log\log x$, powers of $\log\log\log x$, and their products).
I do not claim here to have removed every monotonicity assumption in complete generality.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{7) FINAL}

\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED)}

Round~4 proves a substantially sharper obstruction theorem than Round~3.

\begin{enumerate}
\item It generalizes the Round~3 Abel criterion from the prime-parity sequence to arbitrary sign sequences.

\item It proves a sharp converse construction: for every eventually increasing scale $L(x)$ with $x/L(x)$ eventually nondecreasing and
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}=\infty,
\]
there exists an equidistributed sign sequence with discrepancy
\[
O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
whose weighted series
\[
\sum \frac{\sigma_n}{n\log n}
\]
diverges.

\item Consequently, for Problem~15, any attempt to prove convergence using only a discrepancy estimate of the form
\[
A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
is optimal exactly at the integral threshold
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty.
\]
This is the minimal corrected statement for the discrepancy-only strategy.
\end{enumerate}

What remains open is the genuinely prime-specific question: does the actual discrepancy
\[
A(x)=\sum_{3\le m\le x}(-1)^{\pi(m)}
\]
exhibit enough cancellation, beyond generic discrepancy considerations, to make the Abel kernel converge unconditionally?
