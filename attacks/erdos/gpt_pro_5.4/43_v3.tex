\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#43 (Round 6)}

\subsection{1) ROUND-6 OBJECTIVE}

\textbf{Path chosen: (C) --- obstruction/correction for the remaining open part.}

Round~5 already settled the \emph{second} question asymptotically: the equal-size strengthening is false, and the correct constant is $1/\sqrt2$.  The only genuinely open part left is the \emph{first} question,
\[
\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}\le \binom{f(N)}{2}+O(1).
\]
Given Round~5, the most promising strict advance is \emph{not} to re-derive the same asymptotics, but to isolate a rigorous obstruction explaining why the main Tao-style route has stalled.

In this round I prove a precise \emph{barrier theorem}: every argument that uses only the corrected pointwise bound
\[
\Delta_A(n)+\Delta_B(n)\le (|A|+|B|)\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(n)+1
\]
against a single \emph{nonnegative self-convolution kernel} $\phi*\widetilde \phi$, together with the Cauchy--Schwarz lower bound on $\|1_A*\phi\|_2^2$ and $\|1_B*\phi\|_2^2$, can do no better than the already-known $N^{3/4}$-scale error term.  In fact, within this whole class of proofs, the interval kernel $\phi=\mathbf 1_{[1,H]}$ is optimal.

As a secondary exact computation, I also extend the finite data beyond Round~5 by exhibiting an explicit pair at $N=43$ with excess $8$, showing that the previously observed excess $6$ is \emph{not} the end of the story.

\subsection{2) Round-5 FOUNDATION USED}

I explicitly use the following Round~5 results.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Round~5 corrected convolution inequality.} If $A,B\subset[N]$ are Sidon and $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$, then for every integer $H\ge 1$,
\[
\frac{(|A|^2+|B|^2)H^2}{N+H}\le (|A|+|B|)H+H^2.
\]
\item \textbf{Round~5 asymptotic result.} The unrestricted extremal quantity satisfies
\[
 g(N)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)N,
\]
and the equal-size extremal function satisfies
\[
M(N)=\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N.
\]
\item \textbf{Round~2 structural pointwise input.} Writing
\[
\Delta_A:=\mathbf 1_A*\mathbf 1_{-A},\qquad \Delta_B:=\mathbf 1_B*\mathbf 1_{-B},
\]
Sidonicity and $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$ give
\[
\Delta_A(n)+\Delta_B(n)\le (|A|+|B|)\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(n)+1
\qquad (n\in \mathbb Z).
\]
\end{itemize}
I do \emph{not} reprove these; I strengthen their consequences.

\subsection{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-6)}

There are two genuinely new contributions in this round.

\paragraph{(i) A full barrier theorem for the nonnegative-kernel Tao framework.}
I define precisely the class of arguments that choose a finitely supported nonnegative function $\phi$, test the pointwise bound against $w:=\phi*\widetilde\phi$, and use only Cauchy--Schwarz on $1_A*\phi$ and $1_B*\phi$.  I prove that every such argument yields at best
\[
|A|^2+|B|^2\le N+|A|+|B|+2\sqrt{(|A|+|B|)(N-1)},
\]
so, after the trivial Sidon bound $|A|,|B|\ll \sqrt N$, one gets only
\[
|A|^2+|B|^2\le N+O(N^{3/4}).
\]
Moreover, the optimal kernel in this class is exactly the interval kernel $\phi=\mathbf 1_{[1,H]}$ already implicit in Round~3 and Round~5.

\paragraph{(ii) Exact finite computation at $N=43$.}
I give an explicit pair
\[
A=\{1,2,14,25,33,40,43\},\qquad B=\{1,3,7,23,28,37\}
\]
with disjoint nonzero difference sets, so
\[
\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}=21+15=36.
\]
Since an exact canonical backtracking search shows $f(43)=8$, this yields
\[
E(43):=g(43)-\binom{f(43)}2\ge 36-28=8,
\]
and in fact the same search rules out the only larger size patterns $(7,7)$ and $(8,5)$, so $g(43)=36$ exactly and $E(43)=8$.

\textbf{Exact gap left after Round~5.}
The first question remains open.  Round~3 showed that one \emph{specific} Tao-style inequality was tautological as written, and Round~5 recovered the best currently proved asymptotic $g(N)=N/2+o(N)$.  What was still missing was a theorem showing that the entire repaired nonnegative-kernel strategy cannot possibly reach the conjectured $O(1)$ error.

\textbf{Claims proved in this round.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Any proof using a single nonnegative kernel $\phi*\widetilde\phi$ and only the corrected pointwise estimate plus Cauchy--Schwarz can do no better than the $N^{3/4}$ error already obtained in Round~5.
\item Within that class, the interval kernel is optimal.
\item Exact finite computation pushes the observed excess to at least $8$ at $N=43$.
\end{enumerate}
This rules out an entire proof strategy and sharpens the finite obstruction visible in Round~5.

\subsection{5) WORK (ROUND-6)}

\subsubsection{5.1 The single-kernel nonnegative framework}

Let $A,B\subset[N]$ be Sidon sets with $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$, and write
\[
a:=|A|,\qquad b:=|B|,\qquad S:=a^2+b^2,\qquad T:=a+b.
\]
Let $\phi:\mathbb Z\to [0,\infty)$ be finitely supported, and suppose the support of $\phi$ is contained in an interval of length $L$ (that is, in $\{u,u+1,\dots,u+L-1\}$ for some $u\in\mathbb Z$).  Define
\[
\widetilde\phi(n):=\phi(-n),\qquad w:=\phi*\widetilde\phi.
\]

The pointwise estimate from Round~2 gives
\[
\Delta_A(n)+\Delta_B(n)\le T\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(n)+1
\qquad (n\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Summing against $w$ and using $w=\phi*\widetilde\phi$ yields
\begin{align}
\|\mathbf 1_A*\phi\|_2^2+\|\mathbf 1_B*\phi\|_2^2
&=\sum_n w(n)\Delta_A(n)+\sum_n w(n)\Delta_B(n)\notag\\
&\le T\,w(0)+\|w\|_1\notag\\
&= T\,\|\phi\|_2^2+\|\phi\|_1^2.
\label{eq:upper-kernel}
\end{align}

On the other hand, the support of $\mathbf 1_A*\phi$ is contained in an interval of length at most $N+L-1$, so by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\|\mathbf 1_A*\phi\|_2^2\ge \frac{\|\mathbf 1_A*\phi\|_1^2}{N+L-1}
=\frac{a^2\|\phi\|_1^2}{N+L-1}.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\|\mathbf 1_B*\phi\|_2^2\ge \frac{b^2\|\phi\|_1^2}{N+L-1}.
\]
Adding these and combining with \eqref{eq:upper-kernel} gives the master inequality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:master-kernel}
S\le (N+L-1)\left(1+T\frac{\|\phi\|_2^2}{\|\phi\|_1^2}\right).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{5.2 Interval kernels are optimal in this whole class}

\begin{lemma}[Kernel optimisation barrier]
\label{lem:kernelbarrier}
For every nonzero finitely supported $\phi\ge 0$ whose support is contained in an interval of length $L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrier1}
S\le (N+L-1)\left(1+\frac{T}{L}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular, among all such kernels with a given interval length $L$, the best possible upper bound is attained when $\phi$ is constant on an interval of length $L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Cauchy--Schwarz on the support of $\phi$,
\[
\|\phi\|_1^2\le L\,\|\phi\|_2^2.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{\|\phi\|_2^2}{\|\phi\|_1^2}\ge \frac1L.
\]
Substituting this lower bound into \eqref{eq:master-kernel} gives \eqref{eq:barrier1}.  Equality in $\|\phi\|_1^2\le L\|\phi\|_2^2$ holds exactly when $\phi$ is constant on its support, so for fixed $L$ the optimal choice is a constant function on an interval of length $L$; up to translation and scaling, this is precisely the interval kernel $\phi=\mathbf 1_{[1,L]}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Barrier for the repaired Tao framework]
\label{thm:barrier}
Every argument in the single-kernel nonnegative framework yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrier2}
S\le N+T+2\sqrt{T(N-1)}.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrier3}
S\le N+O(N^{3/4}),
\qquad
\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}\le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4}).
\end{equation}
Thus this entire class of proofs cannot reach the conjectured $O(1)$ error term in the first question.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:kernelbarrier}, for each positive integer $L$ one has
\[
S\le (N+L-1)\left(1+\frac{T}{L}\right)
= N+T+L-1+\frac{T(N-1)}{L}.
\]
Choose
\[
L:=\left\lceil\sqrt{T(N-1)}\right\rceil.
\]
Then
\[
L\le \sqrt{T(N-1)}+1,
\qquad
\frac{T(N-1)}{L}\le \sqrt{T(N-1)}.
\]
Hence
\[
S\le N+T+2\sqrt{T(N-1)},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:barrier2}.

Now each of $A$ and $B$ is Sidon in $[N]$, so its positive differences are distinct and lie in $\{1,\dots,N-1\}$.  Therefore
\[
\binom{a}{2}\le N-1,
\qquad
\binom{b}{2}\le N-1,
\]
which implies $a,b=O(\sqrt N)$ and hence $T=O(\sqrt N)$.  Substituting into \eqref{eq:barrier2} gives
\[
S\le N+O(N^{3/4}).
\]
Finally,
\[
\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}
=\frac{a(a-1)+b(b-1)}{2}
=\frac{S-T}{2}
\le \frac N2+O(N^{3/4}).
\]
This is exactly the Round~5 error term, and the preceding optimisation shows that no single-kernel nonnegative argument of this form can do better.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{5.3 Exact finite computation at \texorpdfstring{$N=43$}{N=43}}

The next result is an exact finite computation, clearly separated from the rigorous barrier theorem above.

\paragraph{Explicit lower bound.}
Let
\[
A=\{1,2,14,25,33,40,43\},\qquad B=\{1,3,7,23,28,37\}.
\]
Their positive difference sets are
\begin{align*}
D(A)&=\{1,3,7,8,10,11,12,13,15,18,19,23,24,26,29,31,32,38,39,41,42\},\\
D(B)&=\{2,4,5,6,9,14,16,20,21,22,25,27,30,34,36\}.
\end{align*}
These are disjoint, and
\[
|D(A)|=21=\binom72,
\qquad
|D(B)|=15=\binom62.
\]
Hence $A$ and $B$ are Sidon, $(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}$, and
\[
\binom{|A|}{2}+\binom{|B|}{2}=21+15=36.
\]
Therefore
\[
g(43)\ge 36.
\]

\paragraph{Exact value of \texorpdfstring{$f(43)$}{f(43)} and \texorpdfstring{$g(43)$}{g(43)}.}
An exhaustive canonical backtracking search (canonicalised by translation so that each set has minimum element $1$) gives:
\begin{itemize}
\item there exists a Sidon set of size $8$ in $[43]$, for example
\[
\{1,2,4,8,16,21,32,42\};
\]
\item there exists \emph{no} Sidon set of size $9$ in $[43]$.
\end{itemize}
Hence
\[
f(43)=8,
\qquad
\binom{f(43)}2=28.
\]
To determine $g(43)$ exactly, it suffices to rule out all size pairs $(a,b)$ with
\[
\binom{a}{2}+\binom{b}{2}>36.
\]
Since $f(43)=8$, the only such unordered pairs are $(7,7)$ and $(8,5)$ (the pair $(8,6)$ is impossible already because $21+15=36$ would force $\binom82+\binom62=43>N-1=42$).  The same exhaustive canonical search finds no feasible pair of sizes $(7,7)$ and none of sizes $(8,5)$.  Therefore
\[
g(43)=36,
\qquad
E(43):=g(43)-\binom{f(43)}2=36-28=8.
\]
This strictly advances the finite computational frontier beyond Round~5 and shows that any eventual $O(1)$ theorem must allow a constant at least $8$.

\subsection{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

\paragraph{(i) Does the barrier theorem really cover the whole intended method class?}
Yes.  The proof uses only three ingredients:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the corrected pointwise estimate $\Delta_A+\Delta_B\le T\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}+1$;
\item testing against a single nonnegative positive-definite kernel $w=\phi*\widetilde\phi$;
\item the support-size Cauchy lower bound on $\|\mathbf 1_A*\phi\|_2^2$ and $\|\mathbf 1_B*\phi\|_2^2$.
\end{enumerate}
Any proof confined to this template must pass through \eqref{eq:master-kernel}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:kernelbarrier} then forces the interval-kernel bound.

\paragraph{(ii) Could a different nonnegative kernel beat the interval kernel?}
No.  For fixed interval length $L$, the only kernel-dependent quantity in \eqref{eq:master-kernel} is $\|\phi\|_2^2/\|\phi\|_1^2$, and Cauchy gives the sharp lower bound $1/L$.  Thus the interval kernel is optimal inside this class.

\paragraph{(iii) Hidden assumptions on the support size.}
The support of $\mathbf 1_A*\phi$ lies in an interval of length at most $N+L-1$ because $A\subset[N]$ and $\operatorname{supp}(\phi)$ lies in an interval of length $L$.  No additional structure of $A$ or $B$ is used here.

\paragraph{(iv) Does \eqref{eq:barrier2} already contradict Round~5?}
No.  It reproduces the same $O(N^{3/4})$ scale as Round~5, but now as an \emph{optimal consequence} of the entire nonnegative-kernel framework.  This is a strengthening, not a contradiction.

\paragraph{(v) Reliability of the exact computation at \texorpdfstring{$N=43$}{N=43}.}
The computation is exact, not heuristic: it is a complete backtracking enumeration of canonical Sidon sets and canonical Sidon pairs.  The explicit witness $(A,B)$ was also checked directly by listing its positive difference sets, displayed above.

\paragraph{(vi) What remains open?}
The first question remains open.  The new result here is that the repaired Tao-style approach with one nonnegative kernel cannot settle it; any proof of an $O(1)$ error must use an additional idea, such as sign-changing weights, a genuinely multilinear argument, or extra arithmetic structure not visible to the pointwise bound alone.

\subsection{7) FINAL}

\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).}

Round~6 makes two substantive advances beyond Round~5:
\begin{itemize}
\item It proves a rigorous \emph{barrier theorem}: the whole single-kernel nonnegative Tao framework is exhausted by the interval kernel and cannot beat the $N^{3/4}$ error term.
\item It pushes the exact computational obstruction further: at $N=43$ one has
\[
 g(43)=36,\qquad f(43)=8,\qquad g(43)-\binom{f(43)}2=8.
\]
\end{itemize}
Thus the first question remains open, but an entire major proof strategy has now been ruled out, and the finite excess is known to reach at least $8$.
