\subsection*{1) ROUND-5 OBJECTIVE}

\noindent\textbf{Path chosen: (C) obstruction/correction.}

\medskip
Round~4 repaired the erroneous Round~3 certificate and produced a corrected explicit family
\[
\{N_\nu\}_{\nu\ge 11}\subseteq A,
\qquad
A:=\{\text{odd }n\ge 1:\ n\neq p+2^k+2^\ell\ \forall\text{ primes }p,\ k,\ell\ge 0\}.
\]
However, Round~4 only showed sparsity for that \emph{specific} corrected family. The most promising Round~5 direction is to prove a \emph{class-wide obstruction theorem}: any construction of the same fixed-data CRT/Fermat-product type can produce at most polylogarithmically many integers up to $X$, and in particular any bounded-branch version is forced to have only $O(\log\log X)$ output up to $X$.

This is strict progress beyond Round~4 because it rules out an \emph{entire class} of proof strategies, not just the single family already constructed there. \end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-5)}

The new ingredient is a general level-counting theorem for \emph{fixed-data CRT/Fermat constructions}.

\medskip
Fix a divisor $G$ of one Fermat number $F_t=2^{2^t}+1$, and define
\[
M_\nu:=\frac{2^{2^\nu}-1}{G}\qquad (\nu>t).
\]
Any Crocker-style construction at level $\nu$ first forces divisibility by $M_\nu$, then imposes a fixed finite set of extra congruences modulo an auxiliary modulus $Q$ independent of $\nu$, and finally selects one or several representatives below $QM_\nu$.

The new theorem shows that for \emph{any} such construction,
\[
\left|\bigcup_{\nu>t}E_\nu\cap [1,X]\right|
\le
\sum_{\nu\le \log_2\log_2(GX+1)} |E_\nu|,
\]
where $E_\nu$ is the set of selected representatives at level $\nu$. Consequently:

\begin{itemize}
\item bounded branching ($|E_\nu|\le R$) gives at most $O(\log\log X)$ terms up to $X$;
\item polynomial branching ($|E_\nu|\ll \nu^C$) gives at most $O((\log\log X)^{C+1})$ terms up to $X$.
\end{itemize}

So the Round~4 family is not merely sparse; it is quantitatively \emph{optimal up to constants} among one-representative-per-level constructions of this whole type.

\subsubsection*{5.2. A general level-counting theorem}

\noindent\textbf{Theorem 5.2 (fixed-data CRT/Fermat counting bound).}
Fix an integer $t\ge 0$, a divisor $G$ of the Fermat number
\[
F_t:=2^{2^t}+1,
\]
and a positive integer $Q$.
For every integer $\nu>t$, set
\[
M_\nu:=\frac{2^{2^\nu}-1}{G}.
\]
Let $E_\nu$ be any finite set of positive integers such that every $n\in E_\nu$ satisfies
\[
M_\nu\mid n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n<QM_\nu.
\]
Then for every $X\ge 1$,
\[
\left|\left(\bigcup_{\nu>t}E_\nu\right)\cap [1,X]\right|
\le
\sum_{t<\nu\le \lfloor \log_2\log_2(GX+1)\rfloor} |E_\nu|.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Take any $n\in E_\nu\cap [1,X]$.
Because $M_\nu\mid n$ and $n>0$, we have
\[
M_\nu\le n\le X.
\]
By the definition of $M_\nu$ this means
\[
\frac{2^{2^\nu}-1}{G}\le X,
\]
hence
\[
2^{2^\nu}\le GX+1.
\]
Taking $\log_2$ twice gives
\[
\nu\le \log_2\log_2(GX+1).
\]
Therefore only indices
\[
t<\nu\le \lfloor \log_2\log_2(GX+1)\rfloor
\]
can contribute elements to $\bigcup_{\nu>t}E_\nu\cap[1,X]$.
Summing the trivial bound $|E_\nu\cap [1,X]|\le |E_\nu|$ over those $\nu$ yields the claim. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark 5.3.}
The theorem applies precisely to constructions that, at each level $\nu$, first force divisibility by the growing modulus $M_\nu$ and then choose finitely many representatives below a fixed multiple $QM_\nu$.
This includes all variants of the corrected Crocker--CRT scheme in which the auxiliary congruence conditions are imposed modulo a fixed modulus independent of $\nu$.

\subsubsection*{5.3. Consequences for bounded and polynomial branching}

\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.4 (bounded branching).}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~5.2 and suppose that
\[
|E_\nu|\le R\qquad (\nu>t)
\]
for some fixed constant $R$.
Then
\[
\left|\left(\bigcup_{\nu>t}E_\nu\right)\cap [1,X]\right|
\le
R\max\!\left(0,\lfloor \log_2\log_2(GX+1)\rfloor-t\right)
=
O(\log\log X).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
This is immediate from Theorem~5.2 by bounding each summand by $R$. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.5 (polynomial branching).}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~5.2 and suppose that for some constants $C\ge 0$ and $K>0$ one has
\[
|E_\nu|\le K\nu^C\qquad (\nu>t).
\]
Then
\[
\left|\left(\bigcup_{\nu>t}E_\nu\right)\cap [1,X]\right|
=
O\!\big((\log\log X)^{C+1}\big).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
By Theorem~5.2,
\[
\left|\left(\bigcup_{\nu>t}E_\nu\right)\cap [1,X]\right|
\le
K\sum_{\nu\le \log_2\log_2(GX+1)} \nu^C.
\]
The sum on the right is $O((\log\log X)^{C+1})$. \hfill$\square$

\subsubsection*{5.4. Application to the corrected Round-4 family}

\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.6 (optimality of the corrected family in the one-per-level regime).}
For the corrected Round~4 family $\{N_\nu\}_{\nu\ge 11}$, one has
\[
\#\{\nu\ge 11:N_\nu\le X\}
=
\Theta(\log\log X).
\]
More precisely, Theorem~5.2 gives the upper bound
\[
\#\{\nu\ge 11:N_\nu\le X\}=O(\log\log X),
\]
and Round~4 already proved the matching lower bound
\[
\#\{\nu\ge 11:N_\nu\le X\}\gg \log\log X.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Apply Theorem~5.2 with
\[
t=10,\qquad G=\frac{2^{1024}+1}{45592577},\qquad Q=L,
\]
where $L=16\cdot 8191\prod_{i=1}^{28}p_i$ is the fixed auxiliary modulus from Round~4, and with
\[
E_\nu:=\{N_\nu\}.
\]
Then $|E_\nu|=1$, $M_\nu\mid N_\nu$, and $N_\nu<LM_\nu\cdot L=QM_\nu$ by the Round~4 CRT construction, so Theorem~5.2 yields the $O(\log\log X)$ upper bound.
The matching lower bound is exactly the Round~4 counting theorem. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.7 (class-wide obstruction).}
No bounded-branch fixed-data CRT/Fermat construction of the above type can prove any lower bound of the form
\[
\gg X^\delta\qquad (\delta>0),
\]
and in particular no such construction can prove that $A$ has positive upper density.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Corollary~5.4 gives at most $O(\log\log X)$ integers up to $X$.
Since $\log\log X=o(X^\delta)$ for every $\delta>0$, such a construction cannot yield an $X^\delta$ lower bound, and therefore cannot establish positive upper density. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.8 (stronger obstruction under polynomial branching).}
Even if one allows polynomially many admissible CRT representatives at level $\nu$, the total output up to $X$ is still only
\[
O\!\big((\log\log X)^{C+1}\big),
\]
hence still has density $0$ and still falls far short of Pan's bound $|A\cap[1,X]|\gg_\varepsilon X^{1-\varepsilon}$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The first statement is Corollary~5.5.
The second follows because $(\log\log X)^{C+1}=o(X^{1-\varepsilon})$ for every fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$. \hfill$\square$

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

I check the new argument for hidden weaknesses.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Does Theorem~5.2 really apply to the corrected family?}
Yes.
Round~4 already established $M_\nu\mid N_\nu$ and $N_\nu<LM_\nu\cdot L$, where $L$ is fixed independently of $\nu$.
Thus $E_\nu=\{N_\nu\}$ satisfies the hypotheses with $Q=L$.

\item \textbf{Could duplicates across different levels invalidate the counting bound?}
No.
Theorem~5.2 is an upper bound on the size of a union, and duplicates can only decrease the size of the union.
In the actual corrected family, Round~4 already showed strict growth, so there are no duplicates anyway.

\item \textbf{Is the theorem too narrow to say anything useful?}
It does not rule out all possible covering-system or sieve strategies.
It rules out the \emph{entire fixed-data CRT/Fermat class} in which the auxiliary modulus is fixed and one selects only finitely many representatives below $QM_\nu$ at each level.
That is strictly stronger than Round~4, which treated only one specific family.

\item \textbf{Could $M_\nu$ fail to be an integer in the abstract theorem?}
No.
Since $G\mid F_t$ and
\[
2^{2^\nu}-1=\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}F_j
\]
for every $\nu>t$, one has $G\mid (2^{2^\nu}-1)$, so $M_\nu$ is indeed integral.

\item \textbf{Did I overstate the comparison with Pan?}
No.
Corollary~5.8 says only that this construction class cannot recover a bound as large as Pan's $X^{1-\varepsilon}$ lower bound.
It does not claim any contradiction with Pan; it only isolates the severe quantitative limitation of the CRT/Fermat framework.
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\medskip
