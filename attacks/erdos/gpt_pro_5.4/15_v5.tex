\section{Problem 15 --- Round 5}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-5 OBJECTIVE}

\textbf{(C) Obstruction / correction.}
I continue the \emph{obstruction/correction} track from Round~4.
Round~4 isolated the parity-discrepancy function
\[
A(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x} (-1)^{\pi(m)}
\]
as the exact obstruction, and proved that discrepancy-only arguments are sharp at the integral threshold
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}.
\]
The natural next step is to push this structure \emph{back to the prime gaps themselves}, since the original problem statement also highlights auxiliary alternating series built from the gaps
\[
g_n:=p_{n+1}-p_n.
\]

The most promising Round~5 direction is therefore to prove that the original Erd\H{o}s series is \emph{exactly equivalent} to a much cleaner weighted alternating prime-gap series, and then to derive an exact Abel-type criterion in terms of the cumulative even--odd gap imbalance
\[
G_n:=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k.
\]
This produces a genuinely new and more prime-specific obstruction:
\begin{quote}
convergence can only fail through a long-range imbalance between the \emph{total lengths} of the even-indexed and odd-indexed prime gaps.
\end{quote}

This is strict progress beyond Round~4: Round~4 identified the correct discrepancy threshold in the integer variable $m$; Round~5 identifies the exact corresponding obstruction in the prime-gap variable $n$.

\subsection*{2) Round-4 FOUNDATION USED}

I use the following earlier results as fixed input.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Round~2 Proposition~5.6 (block approximation).}
For
\[
\Sigma_n:=\sum_{m=p_n}^{p_{n+1}-1}\frac{1}{m\log m},
\]
there exist real numbers $e_n^{(0)}$ with
\[
\sum_{n\ge 2}|e_n^{(0)}|<\infty
\]
such that
\[
\Sigma_n = \frac{n g_n}{p_n p_{n+1}} + e_n^{(0)}.
\]

\item \textbf{Round~2 Lemma~5.5 (gap-weighted error summability).}
The series
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3} g_n\frac{\log\log n}{n\log^3 n}
\]
converges.

\item \textbf{Round~2 / Round~3 Said--Tao equivalence.}
There exists a constant $C_0\in\mathbb R$ such that for the Erd\H{o}s partial sums
\[
S(N):=\sum_{n=1}^N (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
and the parity partial sums
\[
T(y):=\sum_{3\le m\le y}\frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m},
\]
one has
\[
S(N)=\frac12 T(N\log N)+C_0+o(1).
\]
Equivalently, the original Erd\H{o}s series converges if and only if the parity series converges.

\item \textbf{Round~4 sharp discrepancy-only obstruction.}
Bounds of the form
\[
A(x)=O\!\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)
\]
are sufficient in the generic setting exactly when
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty.
\]
I will not re-prove this; I use it only as contextual motivation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-5)}

The new ingredient is a \emph{prime-gap reformulation} of the obstruction.

\begin{enumerate}
\item I strengthen the Round~2 block approximation from
\[
\Sigma_n = \frac{n g_n}{p_n p_{n+1}} + e_n^{(0)}
\]
to the cleaner form
\[
\Sigma_n = \frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n} + e_n,
\qquad \sum |e_n|<\infty.
\]
Thus the parity series is equivalent to the weighted alternating gap series
\[
\sum_{n\ge 2} (-1)^n \frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}.
\]

\item I then apply Abel summation to that series and show that the controlling quantity is the cumulative even--odd gap imbalance
\[
G_n:=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k.
\]
This gives an exact criterion:
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n} \text{ converges}
\iff
\sum_{n=2}^\infty G_n\left(\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}-\frac{1}{(n+1)\log^2(n+1)}\right)
\text{ converges}.
\]

\item Because the new kernel is positive and explicit, I can derive a clean \emph{one-sided divergence criterion}: if $G_n$ has an eventual sign and is as large as a non-integrable threshold, then the original Erd\H{o}s series must diverge in that sign.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-5)}

After Round~4, the exact remaining structural gap was:
\begin{quote}
\emph{What does the discrepancy obstruction look like directly in terms of prime gaps?}
\end{quote}

To close that gap, I will:
\begin{enumerate}
\item identify the parity discrepancy at prime endpoints with the alternating partial sums of the gaps;
\item prove that the original Erd\H{o}s series is equivalent to a weighted alternating gap series;
\item Abel-sum that weighted gap series to obtain an exact criterion in terms of $G_n$;
\item extract a prime-gap sufficient criterion and a prime-gap one-sided divergence criterion.
\end{enumerate}

This directly overcomes the Round~4 obstacle by expressing the analytic obstruction in the most natural prime-specific language available.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-5)}

\subsubsection*{5.1. The prime-endpoint discrepancy is the alternating gap sum}

Define
\[
G_n:=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k
\qquad (n\ge 2),
\]
where $g_k=p_{k+1}-p_k$.
Also define, as in Round~3,
\[
A(x):=\sum_{3\le m\le x} (-1)^{\pi(m)}.
\]

\textbf{Lemma 8.1.}
For every integer $n\ge 2$,
\[
A(p_{n+1}-1)=G_n.
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
For each $k\ge 2$, one has
\[
\pi(m)=k \qquad \text{for all } m\in [p_k,p_{k+1}-1].
\]
Therefore
\[
(-1)^{\pi(m)}=(-1)^k \qquad (p_k\le m<p_{k+1}).
\]
Summing over the disjoint blocks $[p_k,p_{k+1}-1]$ for $2\le k\le n$ gives
\[
A(p_{n+1}-1)
=
\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k \#\bigl([p_k,p_{k+1}-1]\cap\mathbb Z\bigr)
=
\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k (p_{k+1}-p_k)
=
\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k
=G_n.
\]
This is exactly the claimed identity. \qed

\medskip

Thus $G_n$ is the parity discrepancy sampled at the prime endpoints. Equivalently,
\[
G_n
=
\sum_{\substack{2\le k\le n \\ k\text{ even}}} g_k
-
\sum_{\substack{2\le k\le n \\ k\text{ odd}}} g_k,
\]
so $G_n$ is the total even-gap length minus the total odd-gap length up to index $n$.

\subsubsection*{5.2. A cleaner block approximation}

The next lemma is the key strengthening beyond Round~2.

\textbf{Lemma 8.2 (clean weighted-gap block approximation).}
There exist real numbers $e_n$ for $n\ge 2$ with
\[
\sum_{n\ge 2}|e_n|<\infty
\]
such that for every $n\ge 2$,
\[
\Sigma_n:=\sum_{m=p_n}^{p_{n+1}-1}\frac{1}{m\log m}
=
\frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}+e_n.
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
By Round~2 Proposition~5.6, there are numbers $e_n^{(0)}$ with
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3}|e_n^{(0)}|<\infty
\]
such that
\[
\Sigma_n = \frac{n g_n}{p_n p_{n+1}} + e_n^{(0)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
By the same prime number theorem level expansion used in Round~2,
\[
\frac{n}{p_n p_{n+1}}
=
\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}
\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)\right),
\]
hence
\[
\frac{n}{p_n p_{n+1}} - \frac{1}{n\log^2 n}
=
O\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{n\log^3 n}\right).
\tag{8.2}
\]
Multiplying \eqref{8.2} by $g_n$ and summing over $n$, Round~2 Lemma~5.5 gives
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3} g_n\left|\frac{n}{p_n p_{n+1}} - \frac{1}{n\log^2 n}\right| < \infty.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Define
\[
e_n := e_n^{(0)} + g_n\left(\frac{n}{p_n p_{n+1}} - \frac{1}{n\log^2 n}\right).
\]
Then \eqref{8.1} becomes
\[
\Sigma_n = \frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}+e_n,
\]
and \eqref{8.3} together with $\sum |e_n^{(0)}|<\infty$ shows that
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3}|e_n|<\infty.
\]
By redefining finitely many initial values of $e_n$, we may extend the identity to every $n\ge 2$ while preserving absolute summability. This proves the lemma. \qed

\subsubsection*{5.3. Equivalence with a weighted alternating prime-gap series}

Define
\[
U(N):=\sum_{n=2}^N (-1)^n \frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}.
\]

\textbf{Theorem 8.3 (prime-gap weighted equivalence).}
There exists a constant $C_1\in\mathbb R$ such that
\[
S(N):=\sum_{n=1}^N (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
=
\frac12 U(N)+C_1+o(1)
\qquad (N\to\infty).
\]
In particular,
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\text{ converges}
\iff
\sum_{n=2}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}
\text{ converges}.
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
By Round~2 / Round~3, it suffices to compare the parity series
\[
T(y):=\sum_{3\le m\le y}\frac{(-1)^{\pi(m)}}{m\log m}
\]
with $U(N)$.
For integer $N\ge 2$, grouping the terms of $T(p_{N+1}-1)$ into prime-gap blocks gives
\[
T(p_{N+1}-1)=\sum_{n=2}^N (-1)^n \Sigma_n.
\tag{8.4}
\]
By Lemma~8.2,
\[
T(p_{N+1}-1)
=
\sum_{n=2}^N (-1)^n \frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}
+
\sum_{n=2}^N (-1)^n e_n.
\]
Because $\sum |e_n|<\infty$, the second sum converges to some finite constant $C_2$, and therefore
\[
T(p_{N+1}-1)=U(N)+C_2+o(1).
\tag{8.5}
\]
Round~2 already established that
\[
T(N\log N)=T(p_{N+1}-1)+o(1),
\tag{8.6}
\]
and also that there exists $C_0\in\mathbb R$ with
\[
S(N)=\frac12 T(N\log N)+C_0+o(1).
\tag{8.7}
\]
Combining \eqref{8.5}, \eqref{8.6}, and \eqref{8.7} yields
\[
S(N)=\frac12 U(N)+C_1+o(1)
\]
for some constant $C_1\in\mathbb R$.
The convergence equivalence follows immediately. \qed

\subsubsection*{5.4. Exact Abel criterion in terms of cumulative even--odd gap imbalance}

Let
\[
w_n:=\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}
\qquad (n\ge 2).
\]
This is positive, decreasing, and tends to $0$.

\textbf{Theorem 8.4 (exact prime-gap criterion).}
Let
\[
G_n:=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k.
\]
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the Erd\H{o}s series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
converges;

\item[(ii)] the weighted alternating gap series
\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}
\]
converges;

\item[(iii)] the Abel-transformed series
\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty G_n\left(\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}-\frac{1}{(n+1)\log^2(n+1)}\right)
\]
converges.
\end{enumerate}

Moreover,
\[
\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}-\frac{1}{(n+1)\log^2(n+1)}
=
\int_n^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}+\frac{2}{t^2\log^3 t}\right)dt
\asymp \frac{1}{n^2\log^2 n}.
\]

\textbf{Proof.}
The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is exactly Theorem~8.3.
For (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii), apply Abel summation to
\[
U(N)=\sum_{n=2}^N (-1)^n g_n w_n.
\]
Since the partial sums of the coefficients $(-1)^n g_n$ are precisely $G_n$, one gets
\[
U(N)=G_N w_N + \sum_{n=2}^{N-1} G_n(w_n-w_{n+1}).
\tag{8.8}
\]
Now
\[
|G_N|
\le
\sum_{k=2}^N g_k
=
p_{N+1}-3,
\]
so
\[
|G_N w_N|
\le
\frac{p_{N+1}-3}{N\log^2 N}
\ll
\frac{N\log N}{N\log^2 N}
=
\frac{1}{\log N}
\to 0
\]
by the prime number theorem estimate $p_{N+1}\asymp N\log N$.
Hence the partial sums $U(N)$ converge if and only if the partial sums of
\[
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} G_n(w_n-w_{n+1})
\]
converge. This proves (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii).

Finally,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{t\log^2 t}\right)
=
-\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}-\frac{2}{t^2\log^3 t},
\]
so
\[
w_n-w_{n+1}
=
\int_n^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}+\frac{2}{t^2\log^3 t}\right)dt.
\]
Since $t\asymp n$ and $\log t\asymp \log n$ on $[n,n+1]$, the last quantity is $\asymp 1/(n^2\log^2 n)$. \qed

\medskip

Theorem~8.4 is the new prime-gap analog of the Round~3 exact discrepancy criterion.
It shows that the real obstruction is the long-range behavior of the cumulative even--odd gap imbalance $G_n$.

\subsubsection*{5.5. Prime-gap sufficient and one-sided divergence criteria}

The positivity of the new kernel makes the obstruction especially transparent.

\textbf{Corollary 8.5 (prime-gap sufficient criterion).}
Let $L:[3,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be eventually increasing with $L(x)\to\infty$.
Assume
\[
G_n=O\!\left(\frac{n\log n}{L(n)}\right)
\qquad (n\to\infty)
\]
and
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}<\infty.
\]
Then the original Erd\H{o}s series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
converges.

\textbf{Proof.}
By Theorem~8.4 it suffices to show that
\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty G_n(w_n-w_{n+1})
\]
converges absolutely.
Using $w_n-w_{n+1}\asymp 1/(n^2\log^2 n)$,
\[
|G_n|(w_n-w_{n+1})
\ll
\frac{n\log n}{L(n)}\cdot \frac{1}{n^2\log^2 n}
=
\frac{1}{n\log n\,L(n)}.
\]
Because $L$ is eventually increasing, the integral test applies, and the hypothesis implies
\[
\sum_{n\ge 3}\frac{1}{n\log n\,L(n)}<\infty.
\]
Therefore the Abel-transformed series converges absolutely, so the original Erd\H{o}s series converges. \qed

\medskip

\textbf{Corollary 8.6 (one-sided prime-gap divergence criterion).}
Let $L:[3,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be eventually increasing.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If there exists $c>0$ such that
\[
G_n\ge c\,\frac{n\log n}{L(n)}
\qquad \text{for all sufficiently large } n,
\]
and
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}=\infty,
\]
then
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
diverges to $+\infty$.

\item[(b)] If there exists $c>0$ such that
\[
G_n\le -c\,\frac{n\log n}{L(n)}
\qquad \text{for all sufficiently large } n,
\]
and
\[
\int_3^\infty \frac{dt}{t\log t\,L(t)}=\infty,
\]
then the same series diverges to $-\infty$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proof.}
By Theorem~8.4,
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}
\]
has the same convergence behavior as
\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty G_n(w_n-w_{n+1}),
\qquad w_n-w_{n+1}>0.
\]
If $G_n\ge c n\log n/L(n)$ eventually, then for large $n$,
\[
G_n(w_n-w_{n+1})
\gg
\frac{n\log n}{L(n)}\cdot \frac{1}{n^2\log^2 n}
=
\frac{1}{n\log n\,L(n)}.
\]
Since the latter comparison series diverges by the integral test, the Abel-transformed series diverges to $+\infty$; hence the original Erd\H{o}s series also diverges to $+\infty$.
The proof of (b) is identical with all signs reversed. \qed

\medskip

Corollary~8.6 isolates a single sharp obstruction in prime-gap language:
\begin{quote}
if the cumulative even--odd gap imbalance $G_n$ keeps one sign and is as large as a non-integrable threshold, then the Erd\H{o}s series cannot converge.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

I now try to break the new Round~5 work.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Does Lemma~8.2 use anything beyond Round~2?}
No.
It is exactly a strengthening of Round~2 Proposition~5.6 using the already-established Round~2 asymptotic
\[
\frac{n}{p_n p_{n+1}} = \frac{1}{n\log^2 n}\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)\right)
\]
and the already-proved Round~2 Lemma~5.5.
No new prime-distribution theorem is imported.

\item \textbf{Could Theorem~8.3 fail because of the switch from $T(p_{N+1}-1)$ to $T(N\log N)$?}
No.
That replacement was already proved in Round~2, and contributes only an $o(1)$ term.
The rest is an absolutely convergent error, hence only changes the limiting constant.

\item \textbf{Is the Abel transformation in Theorem~8.4 legitimate?}
Yes.
It is a finite identity for each $N$.
The only extra point to check is the boundary term $G_Nw_N\to0$, and that follows from the trivial estimate
\[
|G_N|\le \sum_{k=2}^N g_k = p_{N+1}-3
\]
together with $p_{N+1}\asymp N\log N$.

\item \textbf{Is the kernel really positive and of the claimed size?}
Yes.
Since $w_n=1/(n\log^2 n)$ is decreasing,
\[
w_n-w_{n+1}>0.
\]
The exact integral formula
\[
w_n-w_{n+1}
=
\int_n^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{t^2\log^2 t}+\frac{2}{t^2\log^3 t}\right)dt
\]
immediately gives
\[
w_n-w_{n+1}\asymp \frac{1}{n^2\log^2 n}.
\]

\item \textbf{Do the new corollaries really strengthen Round~4 rather than merely restate it?}
Yes.
Round~4 worked with the integer-variable discrepancy
\[
A(x)=\sum_{3\le m\le x}(-1)^{\pi(m)}.
\]
Round~5 identifies the corresponding prime-gap quantity
\[
G_n=A(p_{n+1}-1)=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k,
\]
and proves that the entire convergence problem is equivalent to a weighted Abel criterion directly in terms of $G_n$.
This is a prime-specific structural reduction that was not available in Round~4.

\item \textbf{Boundary issues at small $n$.}
All logarithmic weights are only used for sufficiently large $n$.
Any finite number of initial terms is absorbed into the harmless constants $C_0,C_1,C_2$.
No argument depends on the exact normalization at $n=1,2$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{7) FINAL}

\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED)}

Round~5 makes a substantive prime-specific advance beyond Round~4.

\begin{enumerate}
\item It proves that the original Erd\H{o}s series is equivalent to the cleaner weighted alternating prime-gap series
\[
\sum_{n=2}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{g_n}{n\log^2 n}.
\]

\item It derives an exact Abel criterion in terms of the cumulative even--odd gap imbalance
\[
G_n:=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k g_k,
\]
namely
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n\frac{n}{p_n}\text{ converges}
\iff
\sum_{n=2}^\infty G_n\left(\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}-\frac{1}{(n+1)\log^2(n+1)}\right)
\text{ converges}.
\]

\item It yields a sharp one-sided prime-gap obstruction: if $G_n$ keeps one sign and is as large as a non-integrable threshold $n\log n/L(n)$, then the Erd\H{o}s series must diverge in that sign.
\end{enumerate}

What remains open is now extremely crisp:
\begin{quote}
Does the actual prime-gap imbalance
\[
G_n=\sum_{k=2}^n (-1)^k(p_{k+1}-p_k)
\]
exhibit enough sign change and cancellation, against the positive kernel
\[
\frac{1}{n\log^2 n}-\frac{1}{(n+1)\log^2(n+1)},
\]
to force convergence?
\end{quote}

This is the Round~5 minimal corrected formulation of the obstruction in prime-gap language.
