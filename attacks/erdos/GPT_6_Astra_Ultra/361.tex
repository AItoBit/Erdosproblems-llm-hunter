\section{WORK: A SMALL BLOCK PLUS A LARGE BLOCK}
Let $n/2<m<n$. Choose $t\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ with $t(t+1)/2<n-m$, and put
\[
A=[1,t]\cup[\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,m].
\]
A subset with at least two members of the upper block has sum greater than $n$. One with at most one such member has sum at most $m+t(t+1)/2<n$. Thus $n$ is not a subset sum, and
\[
G(n,m)\ge m-\lfloor n/2\rfloor+t.
\]
For fixed $1/2<c<1$ and $m=\lfloor cn\rfloor$, the maximal admissible $t$ satisfies $t=\sqrt{2(1-c)n}+O(1)$. Therefore
\[
G_c(n)\ge(c-1/2)n+\sqrt{2(1-c)n}+O(1).
\]
Taking the larger of this and the supplied modular construction gives two complementary lower bounds: the small block supplies a square-root correction uniformly, whereas small nondivisors of $n$ can supply a linear gain. This construction is also discussed in the official comments and is not claimed as new.
\section{ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
The inequality on $t$ is strict; equality could create a forbidden sum using $m$. The two blocks are disjoint by the imposed upper bound on $t$, automatic for sufficiently large $n$ at fixed $c$. These are existence bounds, not matching upper bounds. In particular, a dependence of a chosen construction on $q(n)$ does not prove the true optimum has that dependence. The general extremal value for $c<1$ remains undetermined.
