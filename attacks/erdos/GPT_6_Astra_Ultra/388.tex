\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#388: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
Classify the equal products of two disjoint consecutive integer blocks with lengths $k_1,k_2>3$; are there finitely many solutions when all parameters vary?

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{388.tex}.
The source correctly separates fixed lengths from the global question, although its cited theorems need hypotheses beyond the overbroad phrase ``fixed positive integers. I avoid relying on that phrasing and give an exact reduction and exhaustive check of the four-term later block.
\subsection{2) WORK}
For $k_2=4$ put
\[
P=\prod_{i=1}^{k_1}(m_1+i).
\]
The identity
\[
(m_2+1)(m_2+2)(m_2+3)(m_2+4)=(m_2^2+5m_2+5)^2-1
\]
shows that a nonnegative integer $m_2$ exists exactly when $P+1=z^2$ for an integer $z\ge5$, and $4z+5=w^2$ with an odd integer $w\ge5$. Then $m_2=(w-5)/2$. The disjointness test $m_2\ge m_1+k_1$ is imposed afterward. Both square tests use exact integer square roots, so there is no numerical tolerance.

The saved script checks every $0\le m_1\le10000$ and $5\le k_1\le12$. It finds exactly
\[
(m_1,k_1,m_2,k_2)=(7,7,62,4),
\]
corresponding to
\[
8\cdot9\cdot10\cdot11\cdot12\cdot13\cdot14
=63\cdot64\cdot65\cdot66=17297280.
\]
The reduction is complete for this bounded parameter box because every four-term later product satisfies the displayed identity and every admissible pair of squares reconstructs its unique $m_2$.

A finite search cannot establish global uniqueness or finiteness, and the nonexistence of other short examples is not used as a theorem beyond the box.
