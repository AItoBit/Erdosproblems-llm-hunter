\subsection*{2) EXACT VERIFICATION}
Represent each source coordinate as $(x_i,t_i\sqrt{2002})$, with rational
$x_i,t_i$. Exact rational arithmetic gives the 21 distances, in lexicographic
pair order,
\[
\begin{split}
&22270,22098,16637,9248,8908,8636,21488,11397,15138,20698,13746,\\
&10795,14450,13430,20066,7395,11135,11049,5780,5916,10744.
\end{split}
\]
All 35 determinants with rows $(x_i,t_i,1)$ are nonzero. All 35 determinants
with rows $(x_i,t_i,x_i^2+2002t_i^2,1)$ are nonzero. Multiplying the second
column by $\sqrt{2002}$ recovers the geometric determinants, so these exact
checks certify both general-position conditions. The companion script
\texttt{verification/root\_checks.py} contains the rational calculation.

\subsection*{3) WORK: COMPLETE FINITE EXTENSION PARAMETRIZATION}
More generally, normalize three old points to
$O=(0,0)$, $B=(d,0)$, $C=(u,v\sqrt k)$ with $d>0$, $v\ne0$, and
$d,u,v\in\mathbb Q$. Any new point $P=(x,t\sqrt k)$ at integer distances
$a,b,c$ from them must satisfy
\[
 x=\frac{a^2-b^2+d^2}{2d},\qquad
 t=\frac{a^2+u^2+kv^2-c^2-2ux}{2kv}. \tag{1}
\]
These follow by subtracting the three squared-distance equations.
Conversely, a triple $(a,b,c)$ of positive integers and (1) gives a valid
point at those three distances precisely when $x^2+kt^2=a^2$.
Distances to the remaining old points are then tested by checking whether
their rational squared distances are integer squares. The two determinant
tests above enforce general position after adjoining $P$.

Thus all extensions with $a,b,c\le D$ are covered by at most $D^3$ exact
tests. This is completeness for a specified bounded search, not a heuristic
coordinate grid. It also avoids assuming the new point has rational
Cartesian coordinates when the characteristic is nonsquare.
