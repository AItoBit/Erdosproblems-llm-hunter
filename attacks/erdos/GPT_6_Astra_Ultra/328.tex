\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#328: lowering the convolution bound by partitioning}
\noindent Elementary counterexample to the stated universal assertion, 24 September 2026.

\subsection*{1) Statement and convention}
Suppose $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and $C>0$ satisfy
\[r_A(n)=(1_A*1_A)(n)=|\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b=n\}|\le C
\]
for every $n$. Must there be a finite partition $A=A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_t$, with $t$ depending only on $C$, such that $r_{A_i}(n)<C$ for every $i,n$? Convolution counts ordered pairs; this convention is essential to the example below.

\subsection*{2) A bounded-representation set}
Take $C=2$ and $A=\{3^j:j\ge0\}$. If $i\ne j$, the sum $3^i+3^j$ has base-three digits 1 in exactly positions $i,j$. If $i=j$, the sum is $2\cdot3^i$, with a single digit 2. These expansions have no carries and are unique. Hence every sum of two elements of $A$ has a unique unordered representation. Its number of ordered representations is two when the elements differ and one when they agree. In particular $r_A(n)\le2$ for all $n$.

\subsection*{3) Every admissible part would be a singleton}
If a part $B\subseteq A$ contains distinct $a,b$, then both $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ contribute to $r_B(a+b)$, so $r_B(a+b)\ge2$. Therefore $r_B(n)<2$ for every $n$ forces $|B|\le1$. A finite union of such parts cannot cover infinite $A$.

Even the finite version has no bound depending only on $C$: the set $\{3^0,\ldots,3^{m-1}\}$ satisfies the same hypothesis with $C=2$ but requires at least $m$ parts. Thus the asserted uniform partition theorem fails in both the infinite and finite settings.

\subsection*{4) Scope of the resolution}
\textbf{SOLVED in the negative for the literal universal assertion.} The counterexample verifies every hypothesis and excludes every finite partition, even when its size is allowed to depend on $A$. No assertion for an individually prescribed $C>2$ is proved here. The source records the much stronger Ne\v{s}et\v{r}il--R\"odl result covering the substantive larger bounds; that theorem is not reconstructed or presented as an elementary consequence of this example. This writeup resolves the question as worded, which permits $C=2$.
