\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 397}

\paragraph{Statement.}
Are there only finitely many identities
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{2a_i}{a_i}
 =
 \prod_{j=1}^{s}\binom{2b_j}{b_j},
\]
where all the indices on both sides are distinct?

\paragraph{Result and attribution.}
No. The current problem page records the following parametric construction of
Somani, obtained with ChatGPT. Here is a direct verification, rather than a
claim of a new construction.

Write \(B_t=\binom{2t}{t}\). For every integer \(a\geq2\), put
\(c=8a^2+8a+1\). Then
\[
 B_a B_{2a+2}B_c=B_{a+1}B_{2a}B_{c+1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \frac{B_{t+1}}{B_t}=\frac{2(2t+1)}{t+1},
\]
and consequently
\[
 \frac{B_aB_{2a+2}}{B_{a+1}B_{2a}}
 =\frac{(4a+1)(4a+3)}{(2a+1)^2}
 =\frac{2(2c+1)}{c+1}
 =\frac{B_{c+1}}{B_c}.
\]
This proves (1). The six indices are distinct: for \(a\geq2\),
\[
 a<a+1<2a<2a+2<c<c+1.
\]
The largest index \(c+1\) increases strictly with \(a\), so these give
infinitely many different identities. For example, \(a=2\) gives
\(B_2B_6B_{49}=B_3B_4B_{50}\).
