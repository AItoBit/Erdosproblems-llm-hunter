\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
With $\omega(m)$ equal to the number of different primes dividing $m$, the problem defines
\[F(n)=\max_k\left(\omega(n+k)\frac{\log\log k}{\log k}\right)\]
and asks whether $F(n)\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$.

\subsection*{SOURCE CONVENTIONS AND OPEN DETAILS}
The displayed official definition does not state the range of k or justify attainment of the maximum. At minimum k must avoid the logarithmic singularities. The intended divergence formulation can be stated as: for every M, every sufficiently large n has some integer k>=3 with omega(n+k) log(log k)/log k > M. No claim about attainment is needed for that formulation.
