\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#339: replacing repeated summands by distinct ones}
\noindent Partial proof, including both order-two assertions, 24 September 2026.

\subsection*{1) Statement}
For $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and a fixed positive integer $r$, write $rA$ for sums of exactly $r$ members, allowing repetitions, and $r^{\wedge}A$ for sums of exactly $r$ pairwise distinct members. If $A$ is a basis of order $r$, must $r^{\wedge}A$ have positive lower density? The companion question asks whether positive upper density of $rA$ implies positive upper density of $r^{\wedge}A$.

\subsection*{2) A uniform finite inequality for order two}
Assume $A$ is nonempty and fix $a\in A$. Put
\[T(N)=|(A+A)\cap[1,N]|,\qquad D(N)=|2^{\wedge}A\cap[1,N]|,\qquad x=A(\lfloor N/2\rfloor).
\]
Every sum counted by $T(N)$ but not by $D(N)$ must be a diagonal $2b$, so
\[T(N)\le D(N)+x.
\]
For $N\ge2a$, the distinct sums $a+b$, with $b\in A\cap[1,\lfloor N/2\rfloor]$ and $b\ne a$, are all counted by $D(N)$. Thus $D(N)\ge x-1$, and consequently
\[D(N)\ge\frac{T(N)-1}{2}\qquad(N\ge2a).\tag{1}\]
If $A$ is an asymptotic basis of order two, then $T(N)=N-O(1)$; (1) proves lower density at least $1/2$ for $2^{\wedge}A$. If $A+A$ has upper density $\delta>0$, (1) instead gives upper density at least $\delta/2$. This handles both order-two questions without any regularity assumption on the counting function of $A$. Order one is immediate.

\subsection*{3) A sparse-set regime for arbitrary fixed order}
For general $r\ge2$, a sum in $rA\setminus r^{\wedge}A$ has an ordered representation in which two of the $r$ positions agree. For a specified pair of positions, the number of such ordered tuples using elements of $A\cap[1,N]$ is at most $A(N)^{r-1}$. A union bound over the pairs gives
\[|(rA\setminus r^{\wedge}A)\cap[1,N]|\le\binom r2 A(N)^{r-1}.\tag{2}\]
The count deliberately allows several repeated pairs and representations of the same sum; overcounting is harmless for this upper bound.

If $A(N)=o(N^{1/(r-1)})$, (2) is $o(N)$. In this regime the lower and upper densities of $rA$ and $r^{\wedge}A$ agree whenever interpreted through liminf and limsup. In particular a basis of order $r$ has $r^{\wedge}A$ of density one, and the positive-upper-density variant also follows.

\subsection*{4) A dense-set regime and the remaining gap}
If $A$ itself has positive upper density, choose $r-1$ fixed distinct anchors in $A$. Their sum plus every other member of $A$ belongs to $r^{\wedge}A$, so that restricted sumset has positive upper density. The identical argument gives positive lower density when $A$ has positive lower density. Translation and deletion of finitely many anchors do not change either density.

\textbf{UNRESOLVED for general $r\ge3$ in this attempt.} Equations (1) and (2) prove the stated special cases, but an intermediate-size or irregular set need satisfy neither the sparse condition nor the density condition needed for the appropriate conclusion. Counting repeated tuples in (2) can then be of order $N$ or larger. The general theorem requires a different way to replace repeated summands, not simply the assertion that repeats are rare.
