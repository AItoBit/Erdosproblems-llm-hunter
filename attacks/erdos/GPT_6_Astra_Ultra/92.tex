\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 92: many equidistant neighbors at every point}

\subsection{1) Statement and the source of a counterexample}
For a finite planar set $A$, put
\[
 g(A)=\min_{x\in A}\max_{r>0}
       |\{y\in A\setminus\{x\}:|x-y|=r\}|,
 \qquad f(n)=\max_{|A|=n}g(A).
\]
Is $f(n)\leq n^{o(1)}$, or even
$f(n)\leq n^{O(1/\log\log n)}$? Both assertions are false.
We give the complete graph-theoretic deduction from the established
negative resolution of Problem 90. This is a reconstruction of the
known implication, not a new counterexample construction.

The explicit external input is the theorem of OpenAI's
\emph{Planar Point Sets with Many Unit Distances}: there is a
fixed $\delta>0$ and sets $P_j$ of $N_j\to\infty$ planar points
whose unit-distance graphs have at least $N_j^{1+\delta}$ edges.
The geometric part of a reconstruction is also given in the
companion attempt for Problem 90; its arithmetic existence input
is not reproved here.

\subsection{2) Converting many edges into a minimum-degree bound}
Let a finite graph have $N$ vertices and $E>0$ edges. Repeatedly
delete a vertex whose current degree is less than $E/N$, where
$E/N$ remains the original fixed threshold. If all vertices were
deleted, count each edge at the moment its first endpoint is removed.
The resulting count would be strictly less than
$N(E/N)=E$, a contradiction. A nonempty induced subgraph therefore
remains, with minimum degree at least $E/N$.

Apply this to the unit-distance graph on $P_j$. The remaining
vertices form a planar set $Q_j$ of order $M_j\leq N_j$, with
\[
 \deg_{Q_j}(x)\geq E_j/N_j\geq N_j^\delta
       \quad\hbox{for every }x\in Q_j.
\]
All these neighbors have the same radius $1$ at every vertex, a
stronger requirement than allowing a different radius at each point.
Since degree is at most $M_j-1$, we also have
\[
 M_j\geq N_j^\delta+1\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Finally, $M_j\leq N_j$ implies
\[
 f(M_j)\geq g(Q_j)\geq N_j^\delta\geq M_j^\delta.
\]
Thus the counterexamples occur at unbounded orders, not merely in
a bounded core repeatedly extracted from larger sets.

\subsection{3) Negating both asymptotic assertions}
The estimate $f(n)\leq n^{o(1)}$ would imply, for example,
$f(n)\leq n^{\delta/2}$ for all sufficiently large $n$, contradicting
the preceding subsequence. If instead some constant $C$ gave
$f(n)\leq n^{C/\log\log n}$ eventually, choose $M_j$ so large
that $C/\log\log M_j<\delta$. The same subsequence contradicts
that bound as well. No monotonicity of $f$ is needed.

\subsection{4) An elementary upper-bound consistency check}
For an $n$-point set with $g(A)=t$, select at each center a circle
containing at least $t$ other points. The $n$ circles are distinct
because their centers are distinct. Let $r_p$ count how many selected
circles contain $p\in A$, and put $I=\sum_p r_p\geq nt$.
Two distinct circles meet at most twice, so double counting gives
\[
 \sum_{p\in A}\binom{r_p}{2}\leq2\binom n2=n(n-1).
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz the left side is at least
$(I^2/n-I)/2$. Writing $z=I/n\geq t$ yields
$z^2-z\leq2(n-1)$, and hence
\[
 f(n)\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{8n-7}}2.
\]
This weak upper bound is compatible with a fixed positive lower
exponent. It does not restore either conjectured subpolynomial bound.
