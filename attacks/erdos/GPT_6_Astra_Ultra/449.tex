\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 449}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Let \(r(n)\) count pairs of divisors \(d_1<d_2<2d_1\) of \(n\).
Must \(r(n)<\epsilon\tau(n)\) hold for almost every integer, for
every \(\epsilon>0\)?
No. For every fixed \(K>0\), we construct a set of positive natural
density on which \(r(n)>K\tau(n)\).

\paragraph{Build a finite collection of tightly grouped divisors.}
Choose a large integer \(s\), and set \(\lambda=2^{1/s}\).
There is an interval \([X,\lambda X)\) containing at least \(s\)
distinct primes. Indeed, if every interval
\([\lambda^j,\lambda^{j+1})\), \(j\geq0\), contained at most \(s-1\)
primes, their reciprocal sum would be bounded by
\((s-1)\sum_{j\geq0}\lambda^{-j}<\infty\), contradicting Euler's
divergence of the prime reciprocal sum.
Choose \(s\) such primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_s\) and let \(M=p_1\cdots p_s\).

Divisors formed from exactly \(j\) of these primes lie in
\([X^j,\lambda^jX^j)\). Any two distinct such divisors have ratio
less than \(2\), because \(\lambda^j\leq2\).
Thus, using \(\tau(M)=2^s\) and Vandermonde's identity,
\[
 \frac{r(M)}{\tau(M)}
 \geq\frac1{2^s}\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{\binom sj}{2}
 =\frac{\binom{2s}{s}-2^s}{2^{s+1}}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The right side tends to infinity. For example,
\(\binom{2s}{s}\geq4^s/(2s+1)\), since it is the largest of the
\(2s+1\) binomial coefficients with total \(4^s\).
Choose \(s\) so that (1) exceeds the prescribed \(K\).

\paragraph{Transfer to a positive-density set of integers.}
Consider
\[
 \mathcal A_M=\{Mv:v\geq1,\ \gcd(v,M)=1\}.
\]
It has natural density
\[
 \frac1M\prod_{p\mid M}(1-1/p)>0
 \tag{2}
\]
by periodicity or the Chinese remainder theorem.
For each divisor \(d\mid v\) and each close pair \(a<b<2a\)
of divisors of \(M\), the pair \(ad<bd<2ad\) is counted by \(r(Mv)\).
All these pairs are distinct, since coprimality makes the
\(M\)-part and \(v\)-part of every divisor unique. Hence
\[
 r(Mv)\geq r(M)\tau(v),\qquad
 \tau(Mv)=\tau(M)\tau(v).
\]
Therefore \(r(n)/\tau(n)\geq r(M)/\tau(M)>K\) throughout
\(\mathcal A_M\), proving the stronger claimed disproof.

\paragraph{Counting convention and attribution.}
The pairs here are counted once, with \(d_1<d_2\).
If divisors are grouped into dyadic bins of sizes \(a_j\), the safe
second-moment inequality is
\[
 2r(n)+\tau(n)\geq\sum_j a_j^2
 \geq\frac{\tau(n)^2}{\tau^+(n)}.
\]
The factor two comes from unordered pairs. The proof above does not
need a distributional theorem about \(\tau^+\).
The known negative answer and a deduction credited to Kevin Ford are
recorded at \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/449},
checked 24 September 2026. No novelty is claimed.
The only external input above is Euler's divergence theorem.

\paragraph{Completion estimate.}
A positive-density counterexample set is proved for every prescribed ratio \(K\).
\textbf{COMPLETION: 100\%}
