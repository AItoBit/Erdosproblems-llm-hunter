\section{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
\textbf{Erd\H{o}s \#139; reconstruction, 2026-09-23.}
For a fixed integer $k\ge2$, let $r_k(N)$ be the largest size of a subset
of $[N]$ containing no $k$-term arithmetic progression with nonzero common
difference. Prove $r_k(N)=o(N)$ as $N\to\infty$.

\section{WORK}
We use the following form of the hypergraph removal lemma. For fixed
$r$ and a fixed $r$-uniform hypergraph $F$, a hypergraph on $v$ vertices
with $o(v^{|V(F)|})$ labelled copies of $F$ can be made $F$-free by
deleting $o(v^r)$ edges. Equivalently, for every $\epsilon>0$ there is
$\delta>0$ such that at most $\delta v^{|V(F)|}$ copies suffice for
an $\epsilon v^r$ deletion bound, once $v$ is large enough.
This theorem follows from the work of Gowers and independently
Nagle--R\"odl--Schacht--Skokan; see the primary reference below.

For $k=2$, any two distinct elements form a progression, so $r_2(N)=1$.
Fix $k\ge3$ and a progression-free $A\subseteq[N]$. By Bertrand's
postulate choose a prime $p$ with $2N<p<4N$, taking $N$ large enough
that $p>k$. Work over $\mathbb F_p$ and form $k$ vertex classes
$V_1,\ldots,V_k$, each a copy of $\mathbb F_p$.
A $(k-1)$-tuple omitting class $i$ is an edge exactly when
\[
 \psi_i(x_1,\ldots,\widehat{x_i},\ldots,x_k)
 :=\sum_{j\ne i}(j-i)x_j\in A.                         \tag{1}
\]
A copy of the complete $(k-1)$-uniform hypergraph on $k$ vertices must
use all $k$ classes. Indeed, every pair of its vertices lies in some
edge and hence cannot lie in the same class.

For a transversal tuple $(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ put
$u=\sum_jjx_j$ and $d=\sum_jx_j$. Its $k$ edge labels are
\[
 a_i=\psi_i=u-id\quad(1\le i\le k).                     \tag{2}
\]
If all these labels belong to $A$, regard them as integers in $[N]$.
Every integer second difference $a_i-2a_{i+1}+a_{i+2}$ is zero modulo
$p$ and has absolute value less than $2N<p$. It is therefore exactly
zero. Thus the labels form an ordinary integer arithmetic progression.
Since $A$ is progression-free, it is constant, so $d=0$ in the field.
Conversely, $d=0$ and $u=a\in A$ give a complete copy.

The linear forms $\sum_jx_j$ and $\sum_jjx_j$ are independent over
$\mathbb F_p$, so each pair of prescribed values $(0,a)$ has exactly
$p^{k-2}$ preimages. The number of complete transversal copies is
therefore exactly
\[
 |A|p^{k-2}\le Np^{k-2}=O(p^{k-1})=o(p^k).              \tag{3}
\]
More importantly, these copies are edge-disjoint. Once a face omitting
$i$ is fixed, there is exactly one possible missing coordinate with
$\sum_jx_j=0$, namely $x_i=-\sum_{j\ne i}x_j$. There cannot be a
second complete copy containing that face, by (2) and progression
freeness. This also shows that every edge in (1) belongs to exactly
one complete copy.

Apply removal with $r=k-1$ and $F$ the complete $(k-1)$-uniform
hypergraph on $k$ vertices. There are $kp$ vertices, and (3) gives
$o((kp)^k)$ labelled copies (the fixed labelling factor is harmless).
We can destroy all copies by removing $o(p^{k-1})$ edges. But their
edge-disjointness requires at least one different deleted edge for
each of the $|A|p^{k-2}$ copies. Hence
\[
 |A|p^{k-2}=o(p^{k-1}),\qquad |A|=o(p)=o(N).
\]
The constants in the removal statement depend only on fixed $k$, so
the conclusion is uniform over $A$ and proves $r_k(N)=o(N)$.
\hfill$\square$

\section{VERIFICATION}
The prime modulus prevents degeneracy of the two linear forms; the
inequality $p>2N$ prevents wraparound progressions from being mistaken
for ordinary ones. The script checks the encoding and the exact count
of edge-disjoint complete copies in small $k=3,4$ examples. The deep
removal theorem, rather than those finite tests, supplies the
asymptotic implication.
