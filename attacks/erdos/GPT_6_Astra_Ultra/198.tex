\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#198}
\subsection*{1. FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
If $A\subset\mathbb N$ is Sidon, must its complement contain an infinite arithmetic progression? We use the strong Sidon convention: $a+b=c+d$, with all entries in $A$ and repetitions allowed, implies equality of the two unordered pairs.
\subsection*{2. LITERATURE AND CONSTRUCTION}
The negative answer is attributed to Baumgartner. The current problem page credits AlphaProof with the explicit factorial example used here. We verify that construction, with no novelty claim:
\[
 a_n=(n+1)!+n\quad(n\ge0),\qquad A=\{a_n:n\ge0\}.
\]
\subsection*{3. A SEPARATION LEMMA}
For $n\ge0$,
\[
 a_{n+1}-2a_n=n(n+1)!+1-n>0.
\]
The inequality holds at $n=0$ directly; at $n\ge1$, $(n+1)!\ge2$ makes it immediate. Thus $a_{n+1}>2a_n$ and all entries are positive.

Any positive sequence with this property is Sidon. To prove it, suppose $a_i+a_j=a_r+a_s$. If the largest occurring index appears on both sides, cancel it and use strict increase. Otherwise let the largest index be $t$, occurring on just one side. Its side is at least $a_t$, whereas the other side is at most $2a_{t-1}<a_t$, a contradiction. This includes the case where the largest summand is repeated.
\subsection*{4. MEETING EVERY PROGRESSION}
Fix integers $b\ge1,d\ge1$. For every sufficiently large index $n\equiv b\pmod d$, we have $d\mid(n+1)!$ and hence
\[
 a_n\equiv b\pmod d.
\]
Also $a_n\ge b$, so $a_n\in\{b+jd:j\ge0\}$. There are infinitely many such indices. Thus $A$ meets every infinite positive-integer arithmetic progression infinitely often, and no such progression lies in its complement.

An abstract version further explains why sparsity is no obstacle: enumerate all infinite progressions $P_1,P_2,\ldots$ and choose $b_j\in P_j$ with $b_j>2b_{j-1}$. The same separation lemma gives a Sidon transversal. Larger prescribed finite lower thresholds may be imposed at every step as well.
\subsection*{5. FINAL AND VERIFICATION}
\textbf{SOLVED: negative answer, by a fully verified known construction.}
Both the repeated-summand Sidon convention and the one-sided progression condition are addressed explicitly. There is no remaining gap or external theorem beyond elementary factorial divisibility.
