\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1175 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
For every uncountable cardinal $\kappa$, is there a cardinal $\lambda$ such that each graph of chromatic number exactly $\lambda$ contains a triangle-free subgraph of chromatic number exactly $\kappa$?

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

Subgraph means an arbitrary subgraph, not necessarily induced. The quantifier allows lambda larger than kappa; a consistency obstruction at lambda=kappa=aleph_1 alone does not settle it.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
The usual finite chromatic escalation has a precise ceiling here. Starting with
a triangle-free graph $H$ with vertices $v_i$, form a new graph with the original
vertices, copies $u_i$, and a new vertex $w$. Retain the original edges, join
$u_i$ to every original neighbor of $v_i$, and join $w$ to every $u_i$.
This graph remains triangle-free: copies are independent, a triangle through
$w$ is impossible, and a triangle through one copy would give a triangle in $H$.

If $\chi(H)=k<\infty$, the new graph has chromatic number $k+1$.
The upper bound colors $u_i$ like $v_i$ and gives $w$ a new color.
For the lower bound, suppose there were $k$ colors and call the color of $w$
color $c$. Recolor each original vertex of color $c$ by its copy's color.
No copy has color $c$; and on an original edge only one endpoint could originally
have color $c$, while its copy is adjacent to the other endpoint. This would
give a proper $(k-1)$-coloring of $H$, a contradiction.

Iterating from $K_2$ and taking the disjoint union produces a countable
triangle-free graph of chromatic number exactly $\aleph_0$. Any infinite complete
host contains this graph as a subgraph. But the construction stays countable,
so it cannot supply the uncountable chromatic target in the problem. Also an
arbitrary high-chromatic host need not contain a large complete graph.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
An uncountable construction and a theorem for arbitrary hosts are both missing.
Finite chromatic escalation inside complete hosts establishes neither of those
requirements; the parameter $\lambda$ remains unchosen for the full problem.
