\section{WORK: MULTIPLICITY-SENSITIVE ANCHOR BOUND}
Put $L=v-u$. Whenever $Q>L$, the interval has a unique multiple $t$ of $Q$: two such multiples would differ by at least $Q$. If the total exponent of $Q$ in the interval product is $r$, this unique term satisfies $Q^r\mid t$. Hence
\[
L<Q\le v^{1/r}.
\]
Thus a block with exponent at least three satisfies a cube-root bound, and a sequence of such blocks with $r\to\infty$ satisfies $L=v^{o(1)}$. The last statement follows by fixing $\varepsilon>0$ and eventually having $1/r<\varepsilon$.
The supplied anchor theorem obtains $Q>L$ by a Sylvester--Schur step. The displayed argument above is explicitly conditional on that inequality; it verifies the subsequent valuation step without treating the imported theorem as an in-file proof. In particular it shows that, within that framework, the hardest unresolved case has bounded multiplicity, especially exponent two.
\section{ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Total exponent need not belong to one term unless $Q>L$ is first proved. The anchor is a multiple of $Q^r$, not necessarily $Q^r$ itself. Conversely, if every interval term is $Q$-smooth and a term is divisible by $Q^2$ with $Q$ prime, then the largest prime factor really is $Q$ and the interval is bad. This converse requires the full smoothness condition; a square anchor alone proves nothing. No unbounded family of smooth blocks has been constructed here. For fixed $r=2$, the power $1/r$ does not tend to zero, so this refinement does not settle the original subpower assertion.
