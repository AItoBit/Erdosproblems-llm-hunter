\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 237: many representations as a prime plus a set element}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
Let $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ satisfy $|A\cap[1,N]|\gg\log N$.
If $f_A(n)=\#\{a\in A:n-a\text{ is prime}\}$, must
$\limsup_n f_A(n)=\infty$? The answer is yes, even for every
infinite $A$. We reconstruct the reduction of Chen and Ding
to the Maynard--Tao theorem. That sieve theorem is an explicit
external input.

\subsection{2) The external prime-tuple theorem}
We use the following qualitative form: for each $m\geq1$ there
exists an integer $k\geq m$ such that, for every admissible set
of $k$ distinct integer shifts $h_1,\ldots,h_k$, infinitely many
integers $t$ have at least $m$ primes among $t+h_1,\ldots,t+h_k$.
Admissible means that, for every prime $p$, the shifts do not
occupy every residue class modulo $p$. The theorem applies to
fixed shifts, and the threshold $k$ depends only on $m$.

\subsection{3) Extracting an admissible tuple from any infinite set}
Fix $m$ and a corresponding $k$, and put $P=\prod_{p\leq k}p$.
One residue class modulo $P$ contains infinitely many elements of
$A$. Choose distinct $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ in that class. Take
$T=\max_i a_i$ and $h_i=T-a_i$.

For any prime $p\leq k$, all the $h_i$ have the same residue
modulo $p$, and so miss at least one class. For $p>k$, at most
$k<p$ residue classes are occupied. Thus the tuple is admissible.
For infinitely many $t$, at least $m$ numbers
$t+T-a_i$ are prime. Setting $n=t+T$ gives $f_A(n)\geq m$.
These $n$ are unbounded. Since $m$ was arbitrary,
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}f_A(n)=\infty.
\]
The original growth hypothesis is used only to ensure that $A$
is infinite. The choices of $a_i$ may depend on $m$, which is
consistent with the asserted limsup.

\subsection{4) Attribution and dependencies}
This is a reconstruction of the known solution, not a new sieve
proof. The admissibility and quantifier arguments are supplied
in full; the deep theorem about simultaneous prime shifts is used
as stated.
