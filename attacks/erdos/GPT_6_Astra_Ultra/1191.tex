\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1191 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
Write $A(x)=|A\cap[1,x]|$ for an infinite Sidon subset $A$ of the positive integers; Sidon means that $a+b=c+d$ forces equality of the unordered pairs. Must every such $A$ satisfy
\[\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)\sqrt{\log x}}{\sqrt x}=0?\]
Conversely, is there an infinite Sidon set $A$ and a constant $c>0$ such that
\[\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)(\log x)^c}{\sqrt x}>0?\]

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
For a finite truncation $A\cap[1,X]$ of size $m$, all its positive differences
are distinct. If $a-b=c-d>0$, then $a+d=c+b$; the Sidon property forces
the same unordered summand pair, and the positive-difference condition excludes
the reversed degeneracy. Thus $(a,b)=(c,d)$. Consequently
\[\binom m2\le\lfloor X\rfloor-1,\qquad A(X)\le\sqrt{2X}+1.\tag{1}\]
This is the basic square-root scale, but has no logarithmic saving.

There are elementary infinite examples for the sparse side: $A=\{4^j:j\ge0\}$
is Sidon because the base-four expansion of a sum has either two digits equal
to one or one digit equal to two, with no carries. Its counting function is
$\lfloor\log_4 X\rfloor+1$, so it satisfies the first displayed liminf assertion
and fails the positive lower liminf in the second for every fixed exponent $c$.
This proves those facts for this one family only.

The first question is universal over all infinite Sidon sets, whereas the
second seeks one much denser example. Neither can be settled by taking a large
finite Sidon subset separately for each $X$: choices at different scales must
be nested compatibly in a single infinite set.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Produce the required logarithmic improvement for arbitrary infinite sets,
or construct one infinite set maintaining nearly square-root density at every
large scale. The finite difference count and sparse explicit example do neither.
