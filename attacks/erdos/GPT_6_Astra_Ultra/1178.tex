\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1178 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
For integers $r,e\geq3$, let $\mathcal F_{r,d,e}$ comprise the $r$-uniform hypergraphs having $d$ vertices and $e$ edges. Write $\operatorname{ex}_r(n,\mathcal F)$ for the largest number of edges in an $n$-vertex $r$-uniform hypergraph containing no member of $\mathcal F$ as a subhypergraph. Define
\[d_r(e)=\min\{d:\operatorname{ex}_r(n,\mathcal F_{r,d,e})=o(n^2)\text{ as }n\to\infty\}.\]
The Brown--Erd\H{o}s--S\'os conjecture asks whether $d_r(e)=(r-2)e+3$ for every $r,e\geq3$.

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
An elementary packing gives a useful lower obstruction. A linear $r$-uniform
hypergraph is one in which two different edges meet in at most one vertex.
A greedy packing of $r$-sets on $n$ vertices can select at least
\[\frac{\binom nr}{\binom r2\binom{n-2}{r-2}}
=\frac{\binom n2}{\binom r2^2}\]
edges up to rounding: each selected edge rules out at most
$\binom r2\binom{n-2}{r-2}$ candidates sharing a pair with it.
Thus linear examples with $\gg_r n^2$ edges exist.

Any $e$ edges in a linear hypergraph have union size at least
\[re-\binom e2.\tag{1}\]
To verify this without an unjustified full inclusion-exclusion truncation,
sum vertex multiplicities $m_v$. The overcount $\sum_v(m_v-1)$ is at most
$\sum_v\binom{m_v}{2}$, which is at most $\binom e2$ by linearity.
Hence, whenever the right side is positive, no collection of $e$ edges fits
on fewer vertices, and
\[d_r(e)\ge re-\binom e2.\tag{2}\]
For $e=3$, this is exactly the conjectured necessary lower bound $3r-3$.
It does not prove the matching upper bound. A crude general upper bound is
$d_r(e)\le re$, because any $e$ edges occupy at most $re$ vertices; when
$n\ge re$, a hypergraph avoiding every $re$-vertex $e$-edge configuration
therefore has at most $e-1$ edges.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Prove the sharp subquadratic upper bound at $(r-2)e+3$ vertices and the
matching lower obstruction for general $e$. The elementary linear packing
only reaches the stated weaker range.
