\subsection{2) WORK}
\paragraph{Repair of the measure-theoretic step.}
Let $\mu$ be a finite singular Borel measure on $\mathbb R$, supported on a Lebesgue-null Borel set $S$. For positive integers $M,j$ define
\[
E_{M,j}=\{x\in S:\mu((x-h,x])\le Mh\text{ for every }0<h<1/j\}.
\]
I prove $\mu(E_{M,j})=0$. Choose an open set $O\supset S$ with $|O|<\eta<1/j$. Each component $(a,b)$ of $O$ has length below $1/j$. For $x\in E_{M,j}\cap(a,b)$,
\[
\mu((a,x])\le M(x-a)\le M(b-a).
\]
Take a sequence of such $x$ increasing to the supremum of the set in this component; if a maximum exists use it. Continuity from below shows
$\mu(E_{M,j}\cap(a,b))\le M(b-a)$. Summing over the countably many components gives $\mu(E_{M,j})\le M\eta$. Let $\eta\downarrow0$.
Every point with finite left upper density belongs to some $E_{M,j}$, apart from the $\mu$-null complement of $S$. Consequently the left upper density is infinite $\mu$-almost everywhere. Reflection gives the right-hand result. This proves the intended conclusion without the claimed one-sided Besicovitch selection.

\paragraph{Why the stated selection rule is unsafe.}
The family $I_x=(x-1,x]$, $0<x<1$, cannot have a bounded-overlap subfamily covering all $x\in(0,1)$. Such a subfamily needs right endpoints approaching $1$ and hence infinitely many intervals containing $1/2$. This refutes the general endpoint-based selection assertion as it was stated. It does not refute the density theorem, repaired above.

For continuous $G$ with singular $dG$, finite positive differentiability points are therefore $dG$-null. This conclusion still permits an exceptional point.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The sets can be defined using rational $h$ by continuity from below of interval masses, ensuring measurability. Finite-density points cannot be atoms. The component proof handles its supremum without assuming it belongs to $E_{M,j}$. Singularity and residual irregularity do not turn a null exceptional set into the empty set; endpoints also require separate analysis.
