\subsection*{2) FINITE PREFIXES DO NOT OBSTRUCT A GROWTH EXPONENT}
Here is an independent extension statement. Let $F\subseteq[1,K]$ be any finite Sidon set, and let $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ be any infinite Sidon set. Put
\[
C=2K+1,\qquad M=6K+3,\qquad B=F\cup\{Ma+C:a\in A\}.
\]
Then $B$ is Sidon. Modulo $M$, sums from two elements of $F$ lie in $[2,2K]$; mixed sums lie in $[2K+2,3K+1]$; sums from two elements of the new tail have residue $4K+2$. These residue ranges are disjoint and all lie below $M$, so collisions cannot mix the three types. Within $F$ use its Sidon property. Within the tail, divide an equality by $M$ and use the Sidon property of $A$. Two mixed sums satisfy
\[
f_1-f_2=M(a_2-a_1),
\]
and $|f_1-f_2|<M$ forces both differences to vanish. This also handles repeated summands in the usual Sidon convention.
If $A(x)=x^{c+o(1)}$, then
\[
B(x)=|F|+A((x-C)/M)=x^{c+o(1)}.
\]
Thus every available exponent can be realized while preserving any prescribed finite Sidon prefix.
\subsection*{3) CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SUPPLIED APPROACH}
The extension theorem separates finite repairs from the real asymptotic difficulty: finitely many exceptional indices, or a prescribed initial segment, cannot prevent an exponent already achieved by some infinite Sidon sequence. What remains difficult is controlling infinitely many potential collisions while increasing that exponent. This lemma does not supply such control and does not approach exponent $1/2$ by itself.
