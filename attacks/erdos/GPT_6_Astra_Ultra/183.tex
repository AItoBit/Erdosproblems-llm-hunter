\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 183: super-exponential multicolour triangle Ramsey growth}

\subsection{1) Statement, attribution, and plan}
Let $R_k=R(3;k)$ be the least $n$ for which every $k$-colouring of
the edges of $K_n$ contains a monochromatic triangle. The question asks
for $\lim_{k\to\infty}R_k^{1/k}$. Its value is now known to be $+\infty$.
We reconstruct the coordinate-cover and palette recursion from Chapter 9
of the cited 2026 manuscript. We use parameters sufficient for the
qualitative limit, rather than its stronger explicit lower bound.
All construction steps needed for this limit are proved below; the
construction is attributed to that source and is not claimed as new.

\subsection{2) A pair of functions covering all pairs of words}
Fix an integer $H\geq3$, and set
$m=\lceil2H\log H\rceil$, $s=m(m+1)+1$.
There is an $s$-by-$H^m$ matrix $D$ with entries in $[H]$, whose columns
are indexed by $z\in[H]^m$, such that every $m+1$ columns contain all
$H$ symbols in at least one row. To prove this, choose the entries
independently and uniformly. For any fixed $m+1$ columns, a row fails
with probability at most
\[
 H(1-1/H)^{m+1}<H\exp(-m/H)\leq H^{-1}.
\]
The probability all $s$ rows fail is at most $H^{-s}$. There are at
most $H^{m(m+1)}$ choices of columns. The union bound makes the
probability of any failure at most $H^{-1}<1$, proving existence.

This yields maps $f,g:[H]^s\to[H]^s$ such that, for any $x,y$,
\[
 x_d=f(y)_d\quad\hbox{or}\quad y_d=g(x)_d
 \quad\hbox{for some }d\in[s].                         \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, let $\bar x=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$ and set $g(x)_r=D_{r,\bar x}$.
For fixed $y$, the exceptional columns
\[
 E_y=\{z:D_{r,z}\ne y_r\text{ for every }r\}
\]
number at most $m$, by the matrix property. List them in a fixed order
as $z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(\rho)}$ and put
$f(y)_d=z^{(d)}_d$ for $d\leq\rho$, with arbitrary values thereafter.
If $\bar x$ is not exceptional, the second equality in (1) holds.
Otherwise $\bar x=z^{(d)}$ for some $d\leq\rho\leq m$, and the first
holds at that coordinate. This proves (1).

\subsection{3) Many mutually separated palettes}
Let the integer $t\geq s$ tend to infinity, keeping $H,m,s$ fixed.
For each $1\leq j\leq H$, choose a maximal family $\mathcal P_j$ of
$t$-subsets of $[jt]$ whose distinct members satisfy
$|P\setminus Q|=|Q\setminus P|\geq s$. Write $B_j=|\mathcal P_j|$.
Maximality says every $t$-subset is within difference less than $s$
of a chosen one. A ball of this radius has size
$\sum_{d=0}^{s-1}\binom td\binom{(j-1)t}d$, hence
\[
 B_j\geq
 \frac{\binom{jt}t}{\sum_{d=0}^{s-1}\binom td\binom{(j-1)t}d}
 \geq\frac{j^t}{s(jt^2)^{s-1}}.                         \tag{2}
\]
For the second inequality, each factor $(jt-r)/(t-r)$ in the binomial
product is at least $j$, and each summand in the denominator is at most
$(jt^2)^{s-1}$. Also $B_j\geq1$. A palette records colours omitted
inside its associated block.

\subsection{4) The recursive colouring and its invariant}
We construct, for $0\leq j\leq H$, a triangle-free colouring on
$n_j=\prod_{r=1}^j B_r$ vertices, using the $jt$ colours in $[jt]$.
The stronger invariant is that the graph of edges of each one colour
has a proper vertex-colouring with at most $j+1$ labels.
For $j=0$, take one vertex and no edges.

At stage $j$, for each $P\in\mathcal P_j$ make a block $V_P$ carrying
the previous construction, with its $(j-1)t$ colours relabelled by
$[jt]\setminus P$. A colour is called active on this block when it
is outside $P$, and missing when it belongs to $P$. For each active
colour $c$, fix a proper $j$-label vertex-colouring
$\ell^P_c:V_P\to[j]$ of its internal edges.

Order the palettes. For $P<Q$, choose distinct
$a_1,\ldots,a_s\in Q\setminus P$ and distinct
$b_1,\ldots,b_s\in P\setminus Q$. For $u\in V_P$, $v\in V_Q$, set
\[
 x(u)=(\ell^P_{a_d}(u))_{d=1}^s,\qquad
 y(v)=(\ell^Q_{b_d}(v))_{d=1}^s.
\]
These are words in $[H]^s$, since $j\leq H$. If some coordinate has
$x(u)_d=f(y(v))_d$, colour $uv$ with $a_d$, choosing the first such
coordinate. If none does, (1) provides a coordinate with
$y(v)_d=g(x(u))_d$; colour $uv$ with its $b_d$, again using the first.
Every cross-edge colour lies in $P\mathbin\triangle Q$. Moreover,
when the colour is active on one endpoint's block, its proper vertex
label at that endpoint is fixed by the opposite endpoint.

There is no monochromatic triangle. Inside a block this follows by
induction. For a triangle with two vertices in one block, suppose the
two cross edges share colour $c$. If $c$ is missing on that block,
its internal edge cannot have colour $c$. If $c$ is active, the
cross-edge rule gives the two vertices the same $\ell_c$ label, so
their internal edge again cannot have colour $c$. For a triangle in
three blocks, colour $c$ on all cross edges would require its membership
in the three palettes to differ on every pair. Three binary membership
values cannot have that property.

Finally, for a fixed colour $c$, use the internal labels in $[j]$ on
blocks where $c$ is active, and label $j+1$ on all blocks where it is
missing. Internal $c$-edges are properly labelled by induction. A
cross edge of colour $c$ joins one active block to one missing block,
so its labels differ too. This proves the required $(j+1)$-label
invariant and completes the induction.

\subsection{5) Growth and the passage from a subsequence to a limit}
At stage $H$ we have $k=Ht$ colours and $n_H=\prod_{j=1}^H B_j$
vertices with no monochromatic triangle. From (2),
\[
 \log n_H\geq t\log(H!)-O_H(\log t).
\]
Consequently, for each fixed $H$,
\[
 \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(R_{Ht}-1)}{Ht}
 \geq\frac{\log(H!)}H.                                 \tag{3}
\]
These subsequences have arbitrarily large exponential bases, but one
more argument ensures the limit over every colour count.

Put $N_k=R_k-1$. The usual block product gives
$N_{a+b}\geq N_aN_b$: replace each vertex of a triangle-free
$a$-colouring by a copy of a triangle-free $b$-colouring, using disjoint
colour sets internally and between blocks. A triangle in one block is
handled internally; one in three blocks projects to the outer colouring;
one in two blocks uses both colour sets.
Thus $u_k=\log N_k$ is superadditive and nonnegative. For fixed $r$,
write $k=qr+h$, $0\leq h<r$. Repeated multiplication, with $N_0=1$
as an auxiliary convention, gives $u_k\geq q u_r$. It follows that
$\liminf_k u_k/k\geq u_r/r$ for every $r$, while the limsup is at
most $\sup_r u_r/r$. Hence $u_k/k$ has a limit in $[0,\infty]$.
Equation (3) bounds that limit below by $\log(H!)/H$ for every $H$.
At least $H/2$ factors of $H!$ are at least $H/2$, so this bound tends
to infinity. Therefore
\[
 \frac{\log(R_k-1)}k\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad R_k^{1/k}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
