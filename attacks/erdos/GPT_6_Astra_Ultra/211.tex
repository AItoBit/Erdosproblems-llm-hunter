\section{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
\textbf{Erd\H{o}s \#211; reconstruction using the incidence theorem, 2026-09-23.}
Let $P$ be a set of $n$ real planar points, and suppose no line contains more than $n-k$ of them, where $1\leq k<n$. Prove that the number $L(P)$ of lines determined by pairs of points satisfies $L(P)\geq ckn$ for an absolute $c>0$.

\section{QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK}
This is the quantitative form of Beck's theorem, proved by Beck and also by Szemer\'edi--Trotter. The external input here is the Szemer\'edi--Trotter rich-line estimate, not the desired $ckn$ conclusion. The conversion from that estimate to the precise dependence on $k$ is given below.

\section{OBJECTIVE AND ATTACK PLAN}
Separate configurations having no very rich line from configurations with one. In the first case, control how many pairs lie on rich lines by a dyadic sum. In the second, count lines between a richest line and a suitably sized subset outside it.

\section{WORK}
\textbf{Declared external input.} There is an absolute constant $C\geq1$ such that for every real planar $n$-point set and every integer $r\geq2$,
\[
|\{\ell:|P\cap\ell|\geq r\}|\leq C(n^2/r^3+n/r).
\tag{1}
\]
This standard consequence of the Szemer\'edi--Trotter incidence theorem is the sole substantial geometric theorem used without proof.

Write $M=\max_\ell|P\cap\ell|$. Choose an integer $r_0\geq64C$ and put $\alpha=1/(128C)$. Suppose first that $M\leq\alpha n$. A line containing between $r$ and $2r$ points accounts for at most $2r^2$ pairs. Summing (1) over $r=r_0,2r_0,4r_0,\ldots\leq M$, the number of pairs on lines containing at least $r_0$ points is at most
\[
\sum_r2C(n^2/r+nr)
\leq4Cn^2/r_0+4CnM
\leq 3n^2/32.
\tag{2}
\]
If there are no such $r$, the sum is zero. For $n\geq2$, the total number of pairs is $\binom n2\geq n^2/4$. Thus at least $n^2/8$ pairs lie on lines with fewer than $r_0$ points. Each such line accounts for at most $r_0^2/2$ pairs, so
\[
L(P)\geq n^2/(4r_0^2)\geq kn/(4r_0^2).
\tag{3}
\]

Now suppose $M>\alpha n$. Fix a richest line $\ell_0$ and write $h=n-M\geq k$. Choose any $q=\min(h,M)$ points off $\ell_0$. For every line $\ell\ne\ell_0$ joining one of the $M$ points on $\ell_0$ to at least one chosen outside point, let $b_\ell$ be the number of chosen outside points on it. Such a line meets $\ell_0$ in exactly one point, so
\[
\sum_\ell b_\ell=Mq,\qquad
\sum_\ell\binom{b_\ell}{2}\leq\binom q2.
\tag{4}
\]
The second inequality holds because each outside pair determines only one line; that line may or may not meet $\ell_0$ at a point of $P$. Since $b-1\leq\binom b2$ for every positive integer $b$, the number of these lines is at least
\[
Mq-\binom q2\geq Mq/2.
\tag{5}
\]
If $h\leq M$, this is at least $\alpha nh/2\geq\alpha nk/2$. If $h>M$, it is at least $M^2/2>\alpha^2n^2/2\geq\alpha^2nk/2$. Combining (3)--(5), an admissible absolute constant is
\[
c=\min\{1/(4r_0^2),\alpha^2/2\}>0.
\]

\section{VERIFICATION AND SCOPE}
The dyadic calculation includes the $n/r$ term in (1), which cannot be dropped for rich lines. In the second case only $q\leq M$ outside points are used, preventing the subtraction in (5) from overwhelming the main term. All constants are independent of $n$, $k$, and the configuration. The proof is complete relative to the explicitly stated incidence theorem; it is not a new proof of that theorem.
