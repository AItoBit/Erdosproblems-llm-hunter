Lemma 1 (Every $C_4$ in $Q_n$ is a 2-face, and counts of faces/containment).
In $Q_n$:
1. Every 4-cycle is contained in a unique 2-dimensional face (specified by choosing two coordinates to vary and fixing the other $n-2$ coordinates).
2. The number of 2-faces (and hence the number of $C_4$ subgraphs) is
\[\binom{n}{2}\,2^{n-2}.\]
3. Every edge of $Q_n$ lies in exactly $n-1$ distinct 2-faces.

Proof.
(1) A length-4 cycle in the hypercube flips coordinates along its edges. Returning to the start after 4 steps means each coordinate is flipped an even number of times.
In a simple 4-cycle, exactly two coordinates are flipped twice (and all others zero times), so the cycle lies in the subcube where only those two coordinates vary, i.e. a 2-face.
Uniqueness follows because the set of coordinates flipped along the cycle is determined by the cycle.

(2) Choose an unordered pair of coordinates $\{i,j\}$ to vary ($\binom{n}{2}$ choices), and choose values of the remaining $n-2$ coordinates ($2^{n-2}$ choices). This determines a unique 2-face with 4 vertices and 4 edges, i.e. one $C_4$.

(3) Fix an edge corresponding to flipping coordinate $i$. To specify a 2-face containing it, choose a second coordinate $j\ne i$ to vary; there are $n-1$ choices, and each yields a distinct face. \qed

Lemma 2 (A $3/4$ upper bound by double counting faces).
Let $H$ be a $C_4$-free subgraph of $Q_n$ with $e(H)$ edges. Then
\[e(H)\le \frac34\, n2^{n-1}.\]

Proof.
By Lemma 1(1), a $C_4$ in $Q_n$ is exactly the event that all 4 edges of some 2-face are present.
So if $H$ is $C_4$-free, then in every 2-face, $H$ contains at most $3$ of the $4$ edges.

Count incidences between edges of $H$ and 2-faces of $Q_n$ that contain them.
By Lemma 1(3), each edge lies in exactly $n-1$ faces, so the number of incidences equals $(n-1)e(H)$.
On the other hand, by the per-face bound, each face contributes at most $3$ incidences.
There are $\binom{n}{2}2^{n-2}$ faces by Lemma 1(2), so total incidences are at most $3\binom{n}{2}2^{n-2}$.
Hence
\[(n-1)e(H)\le 3\binom{n}{2}2^{n-2} = 3\cdot \frac{n(n-1)}{2}\cdot 2^{n-2}.\]
Cancel $n-1$ to obtain $e(H)\le \frac{3n}{2}2^{n-2}=\frac34 n2^{n-1}$. \qed

So Lemma 2 is tight for small $n$, but it is far from the conjectured asymptotic $1/2$.

vertices at Hamming distance $1$. Let
\[f(n)=\max\{|E(G)|: G\subseteq Q_n\text{ and }G\text{ contains no (not-necessarily-induced) }C_4\}.\]
The conjecture is that
\[f(n)\le \left(\tfrac12+o(1)\right)n2^{n-1} \quad (n\to\infty).\]

2) QUICK LITERATURE/CONTEXT CHECK
Browsing is not available. I only record what the problem statement itself claims about known

3) ATTACK PLAN
Proof strategies:
1. Characterize $C_4$ in $Q_n$ as a 2-dimensional face, then use counting or averaging to
   bound the number of edges if all faces are missing an edge.
2. Construct explicit $C_4$-free subgraphs with many edges to obtain lower bounds.

Disproof strategies:
1. Build a $C_4$-free subgraph with density strictly above $1/2$ for all large $n$.

Chosen path: provide a clean lower bound construction ($\ge \tfrac12 e(Q_n)$) and the basic
structural lemma about 2-faces.

4) WORK
Lemma 1 (C4 = 2-face in the hypercube).
Every 4-cycle in $Q_n$ lies in a unique 2-dimensional subcube (square face), and every 2-face is a
4-cycle.

Proof.
A length-4 cycle flips exactly two coordinates, each twice, so its vertices are
$x, x\oplus e_i, x\oplus e_j, x\oplus e_i\oplus e_j$ for some distinct $i,j$. These form a 2-face.
Conversely, fixing all coordinates except $i,j$ yields exactly this 4-cycle. ∎

Lemma 2 (Half-layer construction).
Let $H\subseteq Q_n$ be the subgraph containing exactly those edges whose higher-weight endpoint
has odd Hamming weight. Then $H$ is $C_4$-free and
\[|E(H)|=\tfrac12\,|E(Q_n)|=n2^{n-2}.\]

Proof.
Any 2-face has vertices of weights $r,r+1,r+1,r+2$. Its four edges are split between the layers
$L_{r+1}$ and $L_{r+2}$, which have opposite parity. Thus $H$ contains at most two edges from any
face, so no $C_4$ appears (Lemma 1). For the edge count, the number of edges between levels
$k-1$ and $k$ is $k\binom{n}{k}$. Summing over odd $k$ gives
\[\sum_{k\text{ odd}} k\binom{n}{k} = \tfrac12\sum_{k} k\binom{n}{k} = \tfrac12 n2^{n-1}=n2^{n-2},\]
using the standard binomial identity. ∎

Corollary 3 (Elementary lower bound).
For all $n\ge 2$, $f(n)\ge n2^{n-2}=\tfrac12 e(Q_n)$.

Proof.
Immediate from Lemma 2. ∎

5) VERIFICATION
Edge cases:
- $n=1$: $Q_1$ has one edge and no $C_4$.
- $n=2$: $Q_2$ is a 4-cycle; the construction keeps exactly two edges.

Quantifier checks:
Lemma 2 yields a $C_4$-free subgraph for every $n$, so the lower bound holds uniformly.

6) FINAL
