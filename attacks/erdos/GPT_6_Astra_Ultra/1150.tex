\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1150 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
Does there exist an absolute constant $c>0$ and an integer $n_0$ such that, whenever $n\geq n_0$ and
\[
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n\epsilon_jz^j,\qquad \epsilon_j\in\{-1,1\},
\]
one has $\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|>(1+c)\sqrt n$?

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
Let $N=n+1$ and $c_k=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\epsilon_j\epsilon_{j+k}$ for $1\le k\le n$.
Orthogonality on the unit circle gives
\[
\int |P|^2=N,\qquad \int |P|^4=N^2+2\sum_{k=1}^n c_k^2,
\tag{1}
\]
where both integrals use normalized arc measure. Indeed
$|P(e^{it})|^2=N+\sum_{k=1}^n c_k(e^{ikt}+e^{-ikt})$, so (1) follows by taking
the constant Fourier coefficients. If $M=\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|$, then
$\int|P|^4\le M^2\int|P|^2$, and hence
\[M^2\ge N+\frac2N\sum_{k=1}^n c_k^2.\tag{2}\]
The parity relation $c_k\equiv N-k\pmod2$ forces at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$
of these integer correlations to have square at least one. Thus
$M^2\ge N+2\lceil n/2\rceil/N$.

This improves the bare $L^2$ bound only additively. A sufficient new input would
be a uniform bound $\sum c_k^2\ge\eta n^2$ for an absolute $\eta>0$;
(2) would then give a fixed multiplicative improvement over $\sqrt n$.
No such bound is proved here. It is a sufficient route, not an equivalence:
a large supremum can also be concentrated on a small arc without large fourth moment.

\subsection*{FINITE COMPUTATIONAL CHECK}
All sign polynomials of degrees 1 through 8 were enumerated. Expanding the square polynomial and summing its squared coefficients agrees with the fourth-moment identity, and every autocorrelation energy satisfies the parity lower bound.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Obtain a uniform multiplicative improvement, either through quadratic
autocorrelation energy or by directly locating a large value of the polynomial.
The proved parity bound vanishes in the requested relative scale.
