\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1184 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
Put $P(m)=\max\{p:p\text{ prime},\ p\mid m\}$, and set
\[f(n,k)=\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}:P(n+i)>k\}.\]
For every fixed $\alpha>1$ and every integer sequence $n=n(k)$ satisfying $n=k^{\alpha+o(1)}$, must
\[f(n,k)=(1-\rho(\alpha)+o(1))k\quad(k\to\infty)\]
hold? Here $\rho$ is the Dickman function.

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

The current statement uses rho(alpha); a superseded revision incorrectly used rho(1/alpha).

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
The large-prime contributions have an exact separation property: a prime
$p>k$ divides at most one of $n+1,\ldots,n+k$, since it would otherwise divide
a nonzero difference smaller than $p$. Let $R_i$ be the part of $n+i$ supported
on primes greater than $k$, including their full multiplicities, and put
$L=\sum_{i=1}^k\log R_i$. The number of indices with $R_i>1$ is exactly $f(n,k)$.
For each such index $\log k<\log R_i\le\log(n+k)$, whence
\[\frac{L}{\log(n+k)}\le f(n,k)\le\frac{L}{\log k}\qquad(k>1).\tag{1}\]

The weighted quantity $L$ itself can be written without factoring each integer:
\[
L=\sum_{i=1}^k\log(n+i)-
\sum_{p\le k}\log p\sum_{a\ge1}
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n+k}{p^a}\right\rfloor-
      \left\lfloor\frac n{p^a}\right\rfloor\right).\tag{2}
\]
The inner sum is finite because the floor difference vanishes once $p^a>n+k$.
Equation (2) follows by separating prime-power valuations of the full product.

For $n=k^{\alpha+o(1)}$, the two denominators in (1) differ asymptotically by
the factor $\alpha$. Thus even a precise estimate of this weighted logarithmic
mass would not by itself determine the unweighted count with Dickman coefficient.
The remaining problem is distribution of that mass among individual integers,
not an unproved independence of their large prime factors.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Control short-interval smooth-number counts uniformly for every allowed
sequence $n(k)$, beyond the weighted bounds (1)--(2). No Dickman asymptotic
in such intervals is established here.
