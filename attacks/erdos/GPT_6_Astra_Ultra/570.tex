\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 570}

\paragraph{Statement and scope.}
For fixed \(k\geq3\), is
\[
 R(C_k,H)\leq2m+\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor
 \tag{1}
\]
true for every graph \(H\) with \(m\) edges and no isolated vertices,
once \(m\) is sufficiently large depending on \(k\)?
The assertion is known to be true. We prove the full sharp matching
case for every \(k\), and the triangle-versus-tree case. The extension
to arbitrary \(H\) is unresolved in this attempt.

\paragraph{A construction showing that the proposed bound is sharp.}
Let \(t=\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor\) and \(H=mK_2\).
On \(2m-1+t\) vertices, take a set \(A\) of size \(2m-1\).
Colour edges within \(A\) blue and every other edge red.
The blue graph has matching number \(m-1\).
In a red cycle, no two successive vertices can both belong to
\(A\). Thus the number from \(A\) is at most the number from its
complement, and every red cycle has length at most \(2t<k\).
Consequently
\[
 R(C_k,mK_2)\geq2m+t. \tag{2}
\]

\paragraph{Matching upper bound via the stated matching theorem.}
We use the Tutte--Berge formula as an explicit external input:
for a finite graph \(G\) on \(N\) vertices with maximum matching
size \(\nu\), some vertex set \(S\) satisfies
\[
 2\nu=N-o(G-S)+|S|,
\]
where \(o\) counts the connected components of odd order.

Consider any colouring of \(K_N\), with \(N=2m+t\) and \(m\geq k\).
If its blue graph has no matching of size \(m\), the formula gives
\[
 o(G-S)\geq t+2+|S|.
\]
Since \(o(G-S)\leq N-|S|\), this also implies \(|S|\leq m-1\).
Hence \(G-S\) has at least \(m+t+1\geq k\) vertices and at least
\(t+2\) components. Edges joining distinct blue components are red.

If there are at least \(k\) components, choose one vertex from
each of \(k\) of them to obtain a red clique. Otherwise, choose
\(k\) vertices meeting every component, first choosing one from
each and then adding arbitrary available vertices.
The complete multipartite red subgraph on these vertices has no
part of size larger than
\[
 k-(t+2)+1=k-t-1=\lfloor k/2\rfloor.
\]
Its minimum degree is therefore at least \(\lceil k/2\rceil\).
It has a Hamilton cycle by the elementary argument below.
This gives a red \(C_k\) and proves, together with (2),
\[
 R(C_k,mK_2)=2m+\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor
 \qquad(m\geq k). \tag{3}
\]

\paragraph{The Hamilton-cycle step, proved explicitly.}
Any graph on \(k\geq3\) vertices of minimum degree at least \(k/2\)
is connected, since two components would each need at least
\(k/2+1\) vertices.
Take a longest path \(v_1,\ldots,v_a\). All neighbours of its
endpoints lie on the path. Among the indices \(1\leq i<a\),
the conditions \(v_1v_{i+1}\in E\) and \(v_iv_a\in E\)
hold together for some \(i\): the two index sets have total size
at least \(k\geq a\), exceeding the available \(a-1\) indices.
These two edges and the path form a cycle on its \(a\) vertices.
If \(a<k\), connectivity supplies an edge from this cycle to an
outside vertex, producing a longer path. Hence \(a=k\).

\paragraph{Another full case: a triangle versus a tree.}
For every tree \(T\) on \(m+1\) vertices,
\[
 R(C_3,T)=2m+1. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, a graph of minimum degree at least \(m\) contains every
such tree: root the tree and embed its vertices one at a time after
their parents. When embedding a child, at most \(m-1\) other
already used vertices can occupy neighbours of its parent's image,
so an unused neighbour exists.

It follows that a graph containing no \(T\) is \((m-1)\)-degenerate:
every nonempty subgraph must have a vertex of degree at most \(m-1\).
Repeated deletion and reverse greedy colouring make it \(m\)-colourable.
If a blue graph on \(2m+1\) vertices has no \(T\), one of these
independent colour classes has at least three vertices, giving a
red triangle. This proves the upper bound in (4).
For the lower bound, take two blue cliques of size \(m\), with
all cross edges red. Blue has no connected graph on \(m+1\)
vertices, and the red bipartite graph has no triangle.
Since a tree has \(m\) edges, (4) is exactly (1) for this case.

\paragraph{Remaining gap and attribution.}
The matching argument cannot simply be applied to an arbitrary
target: a large matching alone need not contain its adjacent edges.
The tree argument uses a triangle obstruction and does not supply
a cycle of a prescribed larger length. These are genuine limitations
of the proofs given here.

The matching proof is reconstructed from Proposition 9 of
Cambie, Freschi, Morawski, Petrova and Pokrovskiy,
\emph{Ramsey number of a cycle versus a graph of a given size},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10238}.
Their Theorem 3 settles the previously missing odd \(k\geq7\)
cases for arbitrary targets, with earlier results covering the other
cycle lengths. That general induction is not proved here.
The Tutte--Berge formula is the only external input used in (3);
the other displayed constructions and the tree case are proved in full.
Current statement: \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/570},
checked 24 September 2026. No novelty is claimed.
