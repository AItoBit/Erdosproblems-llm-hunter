\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 426}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Can an \(n\)-vertex graph have a positive constant multiple of
\(2^{\binom n2}/n!\) nonisomorphic subgraphs which each occur exactly
once? The answer is no. We reconstruct the qualitative argument of
Brada\v{c} and Christoph. A subgraph here need not be induced.
Put \(N=\binom n2\) and let \(f(H)\) denote the number in question,
divided by \(2^N/n!\). We prove uniformly that \(f(H)=o(1)\).

\paragraph{Standard external inputs.}
We use the graph-enumeration theorem \(U_n\sim2^N/n!\), where \(U_n\)
counts unlabelled \(n\)-vertex graphs (P\'olya--Wright), elementary
binomial concentration and the bound
\(\max_m\mathbb P(\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2)=m)=O(1/n)\),
and the bounded-differences inequality
\[
 \mathbb P(Z\leq\mathbb EZ-t)
 \leq\exp\left(-\frac{2t^2}{\sum_i c_i^2}\right)
 \tag{1}
\]
for a function of independent variables with coordinate influences
at most \(c_i\). These inputs are not reproved here.

\paragraph{1. Passing from isomorphism types to random embeddings.}
The identity
\[
 2^N=\sum_{F\text{ unlabelled},\,|V(F)|=n}
       \frac{n!}{|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|}
\]
and the enumeration theorem imply that the number of types with a
nontrivial automorphism is \(o(2^N/n!)\): each such type accounts for
a deficit at least \(n!/2\) in comparison with \(n!U_n\).
Also \(\sum_{k<n}U_k=o(2^N/n!)\). To see this last assertion, for
\(k\geq n/2\) use \(U_k\leq C2^{\binom k2}/k!\); consecutive terms
have ratio \(k2^{-(k-1)}\), so the sum is dominated by its last term.
For \(k<n/2\), the crude bound \(U_k\leq2^{\binom k2}\) suffices.

An asymmetric spanning subgraph has one copy in \(H\) exactly when
there is one vertex bijection embedding it into \(H\). Each asymmetric
type has \(n!\) labellings. It follows, uniformly in \(H\), that
\[
 f(H)=\mathbb P_{G\sim G(n,1/2)}
       (G\text{ has exactly one embedding into }H)+o(1).
 \tag{2}
\]

\paragraph{2. Positive probability forces only \(O(n)\) missing edges.}
Suppose the probability in (2) is at least a fixed \(\delta>0\).
Choose \(L=L(\delta)\) so that the binomial edge count lies in
\(I=[N/2-Ln,N/2+Ln]\) except with probability at most \(\delta/2\).
Consider the random graph process \(G_0\subset\cdots\subset G_N\),
adding a uniformly random unused edge each time.
Let \(X\) count the integers \(m\in I\) at which \(G_m\) has exactly
one embedding into \(H\). Conditional on its edge count, \(G(n,1/2)\)
has the distribution of \(G_m\). The binomial point-mass bound gives
\(\mathbb EX\geq c_\delta n\). Since \(X\leq2Ln+2\), there are
constants \(\alpha,\beta>0\), depending only on \(\delta\), such that
\[
 \mathbb P(X\geq\alpha n)\geq\beta.
 \tag{3}
\]

The number of embeddings is nonincreasing as edges are added, so the
indices with exactly one embedding form an interval. Stop at the first
such index in \(I\), when one exists. By conditioning on the stopped
history, (3) supplies a history for which the conditional probability
of \(X\geq\alpha n\) is at least \(\beta\).
Write \(m\) for that index and \(\pi\) for its unique embedding.
For \(h=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor-1\), the event in (3) requires that
\(\pi\) survive the next \(h\) edges. The random process after the
stopping time still adds edges uniformly, so this probability is
\[
 \frac{\binom{e(H)-m}{h}}{\binom{N-m}{h}}
 \leq\left(\frac{e(H)}N\right)^h.
 \tag{4}
\]
If the numerator has too few available edges, its value is zero.
Writing \(t=N-e(H)\), (4) is at most \(\exp(-th/N)\).
Thus \(\beta\leq\exp(-th/N)\), which implies \(t\leq C_\delta n\).

\paragraph{3. A sparse complement supplies many possible switches.}
Fix \(C\geq1\), assume the complement \(K=\overline H\) has at most
\(Cn\) edges, and let \(J\sim G(n,1/2)\).
An embedding \(G\to H\) corresponds, by inversion, to an embedding
\(K\to\overline G\); thus it suffices to exclude a unique embedding
\(K\to J\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).

At least \(n/2\) vertices of \(K\) have degree less than \(4C\).
The graph on these vertices has an independent set \(B\) of size at
least \(n/[2(4C+1)]\), by the greedy independent-set bound.
For large \(n\), start with \(T=B\), \(D=1\), so
\(|T|\geq n/(\log n)^D\).
If an outside vertex has a neighborhood in \(T\) which is not all of
\(T\), but has size at least \(n/(\log n)^{6D}\), replace \(T\) by
that neighborhood and replace \(D\) by \(6D\).
Each chosen outside vertex becomes adjacent to all later members of
\(T\); chosen vertices are distinct. Since every member of \(T\)
has degree less than \(4C\), fewer than \(4C\) refinements are possible.
The final \(D\) is bounded in terms of \(C\), \(T\) is independent,
and, writing \(d_w=|N_K(w)\cap T|\), every \(w\notin T\) has either
\[
 d_w=|T|\quad\hbox{or}\quad d_w<n/(\log n)^{6D}.
 \tag{5}
\]

Fix any bijection \(\pi:V(K)\to V(J)\). For \(u,v\in T\), call the
pair switchable if \(J\) contains all edges from \(\pi(u)\) to
\(\pi(N_K(v)\setminus N_K(u))\) and from \(\pi(v)\) to
\(\pi(N_K(u)\setminus N_K(v))\).
If \(\pi\) is already an embedding, switching its values at \(u,v\)
then gives a different embedding. Common neighbors need no new edges,
and \(u,v\) are nonadjacent in \(K\).

Let \(Z\) count switchable unordered pairs from \(T\).
Each event requires at most \(8C\) independent edges, so
\[
 \mathbb EZ\geq2^{-8C}\binom{|T|}{2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Only edges of \(J\) corresponding to pairs \(uw\), \(u\in T,w\notin T\),
can influence \(Z\). When \(w\) is adjacent to all of \(T\), such an
edge has no influence. Otherwise its influence is at most \(d_w\).
Using (5) and \(\sum_wd_w\leq2Cn\),
\[
 \sum_i c_i^2
 \leq |T|\sum_{\substack{w\notin T\\d_w<|T|}}d_w^2
 \leq\frac{2Cn^3}{(\log n)^{6D}}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Equations (1), (6), (7), and \(|T|\geq n/(\log n)^D\) give
\[
 \mathbb P(Z=0)
 \leq\exp\bigl(-c_C n(\log n)^{2D}\bigr)
 =o(1/n!),
 \tag{8}
\]
uniformly over \(\pi\). A union bound over all \(n!\) bijections says
that, with probability \(1-o(1)\), every embedding of \(K\) into \(J\)
can be switched to a second one. There is therefore no unique embedding.
If every influence in (7) is zero, \(Z\) is deterministic and positive
by (6), so the same conclusion holds without dividing by zero in (1).

\paragraph{Conclusion and attribution.}
If \(f(H)\) failed to tend to zero uniformly, some fixed positive
\(\delta\) and graphs on an unbounded sequence of orders would
contradict (2), Step 2, and Step 3. Thus the answer is negative.
This reconstructs the qualitative proof of D.~Brada\v{c} and
M.~Christoph, \emph{Unique subgraphs are rare},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16233}.
Their stronger quantitative rate is not asserted here.
Statement checked at \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/426}
on 24 September 2026. The explicit standard inputs above remain external.
