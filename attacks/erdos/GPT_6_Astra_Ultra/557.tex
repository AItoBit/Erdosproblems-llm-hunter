\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 557}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
For fixed \(k\), must every tree \(T\) on \(n\) vertices satisfy
\(R_k(T)\leq kn+O_k(1)\)? Yes. The dense Erd\H{o}s--S\'os
theorem of Reed and Stein implies, for all sufficiently large
\(n\) depending on \(k\),
\[
 R_k(T)\leq k(n-2)+3. \tag{1}
\]
We verify the exact reduction and its quantifiers, and give an
elementary construction showing why the leading coefficient \(k\)
cannot be decreased.

\paragraph{The stated external input.}
Reed and Stein, Theorem 2, prove that for every \(\gamma>0\)
there is \(N_0(\gamma)\) such that, whenever \(N\geq N_0(\gamma)\)
and \(n\geq\gamma N\), every \(N\)-vertex graph of average degree
strictly greater than \(n-2\) contains every \(n\)-vertex tree.
Its proof is an external dependency. The unrestricted Erd\H{o}s--S\'os
conjecture is not needed for this deduction.

\paragraph{Apply the density theorem to one colour.}
For \(k\geq2\), set \(N=k(n-2)+3\), and consider any colouring
of \(K_N\) with \(k\) colours. One colour has at least
\(\binom N2/k\) edges, hence average degree at least
\[
 \frac{N-1}{k}=n-2+\frac2k>n-2. \tag{2}
\]
Also \(N\leq kn\), so \(n\geq N/k\).
Choose \(\gamma=1/k\), and then choose \(n\) so large that
\(N\geq N_0(1/k)\). The external theorem applies to the spanning
graph of this colour and supplies \(T\). This proves (1), uniformly
over all trees of the specified order.
For \(k=1\), the exact value is \(R_1(T)=n\), so the original
claim is immediate.

\paragraph{Small orders and the meaning of the constant.}
Only finitely many \(n\) lie below the threshold for fixed \(k\),
and there are only finitely many trees of each such order.
Their Ramsey numbers are finite: each is bounded by the ordinary
\(k\)-colour Ramsey number of a clique of the same order.
Increasing a constant \(C_k\) to cover these finitely many values
therefore gives \(R_k(T)\leq kn+C_k\) for every \(n\).
No uniformity in a growing number of colours is asserted.

\paragraph{A star construction with the matching leading coefficient.}
Let \(N\) be the largest positive even integer with
\(N\leq k(n-2)+1\), for \(n\) sufficiently large.
The complete graph on \(N\) vertices decomposes into \(N-1\)
perfect matchings. Here is the usual explicit decomposition.
Use vertices \(\{\infty\}\cup\mathbb Z_{N-1}\); for each
\(a\in\mathbb Z_{N-1}\), pair \(\infty\) with \(a\), and pair
\(a+j\) with \(a-j\) for \(1\leq j\leq(N-2)/2\).
Each edge with two finite endpoints belongs to the unique matching
whose index is their midpoint, because \(N-1\) is odd.

Distribute these \(N-1\leq k(n-2)\) matchings among \(k\) colours,
at most \(n-2\) matchings per colour. Every vertex then has degree
at most \(n-2\) in each colour. No colour contains the star
\(K_{1,n-1}\). Consequently
\[
 R_k(K_{1,n-1})>N\geq k(n-2),
\]
which is \(kn-O(k)\). This construction establishes the claimed
sharpness of the leading coefficient.
