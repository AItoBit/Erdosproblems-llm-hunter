\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1172 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
Under the generalized continuum hypothesis, decide each ordinal partition relation
\[
\omega_3\longrightarrow(\omega_2,\omega_1+2)^2,
\qquad
\omega_3\longrightarrow(\omega_2+\omega_1,\omega_2+\omega)^2,
\]
\[
\omega_2\longrightarrow(\omega_1^{\omega+2}+2,\omega_1+2)^2.
\]
Separately, determine whether GCH is consistent with
\[
\omega_2\longrightarrow(\omega_1+\omega)^2_2,
\]
and whether it is consistent with the stronger simultaneous assertion $\omega_2\longrightarrow(\xi)^2_2$ for every $\xi<\omega_2$.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
A single bounded-degree lemma settles a restricted family of colorings in all
the displayed questions. Let $\theta$ be either $\omega_2$ or $\omega_3$ and
suppose the blue-neighbor set of every vertex has cardinality less than $\theta$.
There is a red-homogeneous set of size $\theta$: choose vertices recursively,
at stage $\alpha<\theta$ excluding the earlier vertices and their blue neighbors.
Regularity of $\theta$ makes this excluded union smaller than $\theta$, so another
vertex exists. All pairs of chosen vertices are red.

A subset of the initial ordinal $\theta$ with size $\theta$ has order type
$\theta$. It therefore contains every smaller requested target as an initial
segment. In particular, $\omega_1^{\omega+2}+2$ has cardinality $\aleph_1$ and
lies below $\omega_2$: ordinal products and the countable supremum in that
expression do not increase cardinality beyond $\aleph_1$. The targets on
$\omega_3$ likewise lie below $\omega_3$.

This restricted result does not need GCH. Its failure as a general proof is
specific: arbitrary blue neighborhoods may have full domain cardinality,
so the recursive exclusion can exhaust the domain at its very first step.
Reversing red and blue only handles a second restricted family.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Handle colorings with full-sized neighborhoods in both colors, while retaining
the exact ordinal targets and distinguishing GCH implications from consistency
questions. The bounded-degree lemma is not a forcing or consistency construction.
