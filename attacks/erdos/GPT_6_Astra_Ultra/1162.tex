\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
For the symmetric group $S_n$, seek an asymptotic formula for the number $|\operatorname{Sub}(S_n)|$ of its subgroups. Also seek a statistical description of the orders of those subgroups.

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

The question asks for an asymptotic formula for the subgroup count, not merely its leading logarithmic exponent. Its statistical clause does not specify a probability model, so none is imposed here.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
Put $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. The disjoint transpositions
$(1\ 2),(3\ 4),\ldots,(2k-1\ 2k)$ generate a subgroup
$E\cong\mathbb F_2^k$ of $S_n$. Split $E=U\oplus V$ with
$\dim U=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ and $\dim V=\lceil k/2\rceil$.
Every linear map $T:U\to V$ gives a distinct graph subgroup
$\{(u,Tu):u\in U\}$. Consequently
\[|\operatorname{Sub}(S_n)|\ge2^{\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor}
=2^{n^2/16-O(n)}.\tag{1}\]
All of these many subgroups even have the same order $2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$.

There is also a crude universal upper bound requiring no permutation-group
structure. Every subgroup of a finite group $G$ has a generating set of size
at most $\lfloor\log_2|G|\rfloor$: whenever a chosen element enlarges the subgroup
generated so far, its order at least doubles. Pad shorter generating lists with
identities. Counting all lists then yields
\[|\operatorname{Sub}(S_n)|\le(n!)^{\lfloor\log_2(n!)\rfloor}
\le2^{n^2(\log_2 n)^2}.\tag{2}\]
Neither (1) nor (2) is presented as a new or sharp literature bound. They are
independent baseline proofs identifying a large explicit family and the very
substantial precision still missing from this attempt.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Determine an asymptotic formula rather than bounds on different quadratic
logarithmic scales. Counting one elementary abelian family also does not describe
the order distribution of all subgroups of $S_n$.
