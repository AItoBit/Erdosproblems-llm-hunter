\section{WORK: BOUNDED INCREMENTS DO NOT FORCE A LIMIT}
For $n\ge1$,
\[
0\le f(n+1)-f(n)\le1.
\]
The lower bound follows by adding an isolated vertex. For the upper bound, take an optimizing graph $G$ on $n+1$ vertices and delete any vertex, obtaining $H$. Then $\chi(G)\le\chi(H)+1$ and $\omega(G)\ge\omega(H)\ge1$, so
$\chi(G)/\omega(G)\le f(n)+1$.
Nevertheless these inequalities plus $f(n)=\Theta(n/(\log_2 n)^2)$ do not imply convergence. To see this, for fixed $a>b>0$ define for large real $x$
\[
h(x)=\frac{x}{(\log_2 x)^2}\bigl(a+b\sin(\log\log x)\bigr),
\qquad F(n)=\lfloor h(n)\rfloor.
\]
Differentiation gives $h'(x)=(a+b\sin(\log\log x))/(\log_2 x)^2+O((\log x)^{-3})$. Thus eventually $0<h'(x)<1$, and $F$ is nondecreasing with increments zero or one. Yet
\[
\liminf F(n)(\log_2n)^2/n=a-b,\qquad
\limsup F(n)(\log_2n)^2/n=a+b.
\]
Integer rounding contributes only $o(1)$, and the phases of $\log\log n$ approach the maxima and minima along suitable integer subsequences.
\section{ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
The constructed $F$ is an abstract sequence, not the graph extremal function. It disproves only a proposed inference from monotonicity, bounded jumps and order of growth. The source's inverse transformation remains valid, but transfers rather than resolves oscillation. Its inequalities $\liminf g\ge L^2$ and $\limsup g=M^2$ allow convergence even when $L<M$; a stronger graph or Ramsey argument is still needed.
