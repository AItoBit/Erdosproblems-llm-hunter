\section{WORK: A FINITE ALTERATION BOUND}
Let $S$ contain $n$ integers and have at most $\lambda$ unordered representations of any sum, with repetitions allowed. For $n\ge8\lambda$, $S$ contains a Sidon subset of size at least
\[
\frac{n^{2/3}}{4\lambda^{1/3}}.
\]
For each sum, join every two distinct unordered representations to form a forbidden configuration. Two distinct representations cannot share an element unless they are identical. Hence configurations have either four distinct elements or three distinct elements of the form $a+c=2b$.
The total number of representation pairs is at most
\[
\sum_s\binom{r(s)}2\le\frac{\lambda-1}2\sum_s r(s)
\le\frac{\lambda n^2}{2}.
\]
The three-element configurations number at most $\lambda n$, since their middle element determines a diagonal representation and there are at most $\lambda-1$ alternatives.
Select each element independently with probability $p=(8\lambda n)^{-1/3}$. Then delete one vertex from each retained forbidden configuration. The expected size left is at least
\[
pn-\frac{\lambda n^2p^4}{2}-\lambda np^3
=\frac{15}{16}pn-\frac18\ge\frac12pn
=\frac{n^{2/3}}{4\lambda^{1/3}}.
\]
The displayed inequality uses $pn\ge1$, which follows from $n\ge8\lambda$. Some realization therefore has the claimed size. Removing all configurations makes every unordered sum unique.

\section{ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Diagonal representations require three-vertex configurations and cannot be hidden in a four-uniform hypergraph. The separate $\lambda np^3$ term handles them. With $n=A(N)=N^{1/2-o(1)}$, this lemma gives only $N^{1/3-o(1)}$ points at each cutoff. Further, the extracted subsets at different cutoffs need not be nested. Thus this standard alteration argument neither constructs a single infinite dense Sidon set nor resolves the near-square-root $B_2[\lambda]$ problem. It identifies two separate missing requirements: density preservation and compatibility across scales.
