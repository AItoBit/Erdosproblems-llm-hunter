\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 73: hereditary independence and odd cycles}

\subsection{1) Statement and known status}
Suppose every finite subgraph $H$ of a graph $G$ satisfies
\[
 \alpha(H)\geq\frac{|V(H)|-k}{2},
\]
where $k$ is a fixed nonnegative integer. Must deleting a number of
vertices bounded in terms of $k$ make $G$ bipartite?
The current problem page records an affirmative theorem of Reed.
This attempt proves the zero-defect case, a necessary odd-cycle
packing bound, and the assertion for graphs whose cycles are
pairwise vertex-disjoint. It does not reprove Reed's general theorem.

\subsection{2) A defect parameter and the easy direction}
For a finite graph define
\[
 b(G)=\max_{U\subseteq V(G)}\bigl(|U|-2\alpha(G[U])\bigr).
\]
The empty set ensures $b(G)\geq0$. It suffices to test induced
subgraphs in the hypothesis: deleting edges cannot decrease an
independence number. Thus the hypothesis is exactly $b(G)\leq k$.

Let $X$ be a vertex set for which $G-X$ is bipartite. For every $U$,
the larger part of a bipartition of $G[U\setminus X]$ is independent
in $G[U]$ and has size at least $(|U|-|X|)/2$. Therefore
\[
 b(G)\leq |X|.
\]
This is the easy direction. The requested theorem reverses it up to
some function of $k$, and that reversal does not follow by rearranging
this inequality.

\subsection{3) Odd-cycle packing and the case $k=0$}
Suppose $G$ contains $t$ pairwise vertex-disjoint odd cycles of
lengths $2r_1+1,\ldots,2r_t+1$. Take the subgraph $H$ consisting
only of their edges and vertices, deleting all other edges. On each
cycle an independent set has maximum size $r_i$, so
\[
 |V(H)|-2\alpha(H)=
 \sum_{i=1}^t(2r_i+1)-2\sum_{i=1}^t r_i=t.
\]
The hypothesis consequently forces $t\leq k$.
For $k=0$ even one odd cycle is impossible. A graph without odd
cycles is bipartite: in each component choose a root and partition
vertices by parity of distance from it. An edge joining equal
parities would, together with shortest root paths, produce an odd
closed walk and hence an odd cycle. This proof also works for
infinite graphs, since each root path is finite. Thus no deletion
is needed when $k=0$.

If all cycles of $G$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint, the odd cycles
are a packing and there are at most $k$ of them. Delete one vertex
from each. Every surviving cycle was already a cycle of $G$, and
all its odd cycles were hit. The remaining graph is therefore
bipartite, proving the desired assertion for this class with bound $k$.

\subsection{4) A sharp elementary lower obstruction and the gap}
For $G=K_{k+2}$, a nonempty induced subgraph on $s$ vertices has
independence number $1$, so $b(G)=k$. A complete graph is bipartite
precisely when it has at most two vertices. Hence exactly $k$
vertices must be removed from $K_{k+2}$. Any universal deletion
bound must therefore be at least $k$.

For general graphs, a maximum packing of odd cycles has at most
$k$ members, but deleting all their vertices may cost arbitrarily
many vertices because their lengths are unbounded. No bounded
transversal has been constructed here. This is the precise remaining
gap; the packing bound alone is not treated as the requested theorem.

\textbf{Outcome of this attempt: UNRESOLVED, with the stated special
cases and necessary bounds proved.} The known general resolution
is acknowledged, and no new result is claimed.
