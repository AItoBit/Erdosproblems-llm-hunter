\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 185: avoiding collinear triples in a ternary cube}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
Let $f_3(n)$ be the largest size of a subset of $\{0,1,2\}^n$ with
no three distinct collinear points. Is $f_3(n)=o(3^n)$?
We prove the answer is yes using density Hales--Jewett as an explicit
external theorem. We also reconstruct the elementary spherical-layer
lower bound $f_3(n)\gg3^n/\sqrt n$.

\subsection{2) Deduction of the upper bound}
The density Hales--Jewett theorem for three symbols states that for
every $\epsilon>0$ there is an $N_0(\epsilon)$ such that, for all
$n\geq N_0(\epsilon)$, every subset of $\{0,1,2\}^n$ of size at least
$\epsilon3^n$ contains a combinatorial line. Its variable coordinates
form a nonempty set $S$. If $b$ has the fixed coordinates of the line
and zero in $S$, the three points are
\[
 b,\quad b+\mathbf1_S,\quad b+2\mathbf1_S.
\]
They are distinct and collinear in $\mathbb R^n$. Therefore a set with
no collinear triple has size less than $\epsilon3^n$ whenever
$n\geq N_0(\epsilon)$. Since this holds for every positive $\epsilon$,
it is exactly $f_3(n)/3^n\to0$.

\subsection{3) An explicit lower bound from a sphere}
For an integer $r$, consider
\[
 S_r=\left\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^n:
             \sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-1)^2=r\right\}.
\]
A Euclidean sphere has no three distinct collinear points: restricting
its defining squared norm to a line with nonzero direction gives a
quadratic equation with positive leading coefficient, hence at most
two roots. Thus every $S_r$ is an admissible set.

Choose $x$ uniformly from the cube and let
$X=\sum_i(x_i-1)^2$. Its summands are independent, equal to one with
probability $2/3$, so
$\mathbb EX=2n/3$ and $\operatorname{Var}X=2n/9$.
Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P(|X-2n/3|<\sqrt n)\geq7/9.
\]
The indicated interval contains at most $2\sqrt n+2$ integers. At least
one layer therefore has size at least
\[
 \frac{7\cdot3^n}{9(2\sqrt n+2)}
 \geq\frac7{36}\frac{3^n}{\sqrt n}\qquad(n\geq1).
\]
The lower and upper bounds are compatible, since the density of this
construction tends to zero. They do not determine an asymptotically
exact formula for $f_3(n)$.
