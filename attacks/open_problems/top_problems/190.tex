\section*{\texorpdfstring{Landau's Fourth Problem on Primes of the Form n\textasciicircum{}2+1}{Landau's Fourth Problem on Primes of the Form n\textasciicircum{}2+1}}\label{190}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $x\ge1$, set
\[
 Q(x)=\#\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:1\le n\le x,\ n^2+1\text{ is prime}\}.
\]
The selected conjecture is $Q(x)\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$, equivalently
$\forall B\ \exists n>B$ such that $n^2+1$ is prime. Prime means an integer greater than one having no positive divisors other than one and itself. The problem asks only infinitude, not an asymptotic formula for $Q(x)$, a positive density among integers, or primality for every $n$. In particular, parity restrictions on $n$ do not negate the infinitude formulation.
\par\smallskip\textit{Formulation and concept references:} \href{https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LandausProblems.html}{[S1]}.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there infinitely many primes that exceed a positive integer square by exactly one?
