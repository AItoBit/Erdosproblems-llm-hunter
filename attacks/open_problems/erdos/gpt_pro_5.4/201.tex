\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#201: progression-free subsets inside arbitrary $N$-sets (ROUND 2)}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE}
I again pursue path \textbf{(C)}.  This is Erd\H{o}s Problem \#201 \cite{Bloom201}.  Round 1 gave the classical comparison
\[
G_k(N)\asymp_k R_k(N)
\]
up to unspecified constants, but it still relied on the external Koml\'os--Sulyok--Szemer\'edi theorem and did not isolate what structural feature of an arbitrary set $B$ makes it hard or easy to extract a large progression-free subset.  In this round I replace that coarse comparison by a direct theorem depending on the number of progression-types already present in $B$, and then use it to rule out an entire class of candidate ``worst-case'' sets.

\subsection*{2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED}
From Round 1 I use only the notation
\[
f_k(B):=\max\{|A|: A\subseteq B\text{ contains no nontrivial }k\text{-term AP}\}
\]
and
\[
G_k(N):=\min_{|B|=N} f_k(B),
\qquad
R_k(N):=f_k(\{1,2,\dots,N\}).
\]
I also use the trivial inequality $G_k(N)\le R_k(N)$ already proved in Round 1, together with the fact that Koml\'os--Sulyok--Szemer\'edi give the complementary comparison $R_k(N)\ll_k G_k(N)$ and that Semchankau refines this on a dense subsequence \cite{KSS75,Semchankau20}.

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND 2)}
The new tool is O'Bryant's arbitrary-set version of the Behrend construction \cite{OBryant10}.  It gives lower bounds for $f_k(B)$ not merely in terms of $|B|$, but in terms of how many progression-\emph{types} are present in $B$.

For $D=1$ (ordinary arithmetic progressions), the type of a nontrivial $k$-term progression is determined by its first two terms.  So the relevant parameter is a concrete measure of how many $k$-term progression patterns $B$ actually supports.

This lets us bypass the KSS theorem entirely and prove a direct lower bound for $G_k(N)$.

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND 2)}
The Round-1 obstacle was that constant-factor compression theorems do not get close to the ratio question $R_3(N)/G_3(N)\to 1$.

The new plan is different:
\begin{enumerate}
\item specialize O'Bryant's theorem to ordinary arithmetic progressions ($D=1$);
\item use the crude but universal bound that the number of $k$-term progression-types in an $N$-element set is at most $N^2$;
\item deduce a direct lower bound for $G_k(N)$ of Behrend type, with no appeal to KSS;
\item then sharpen it for sets with very few progression-types, thereby ruling out a large structured class of candidate hard examples.
\end{enumerate}
This does not solve the ratio problem, but it produces a new structural obstruction not present in Round 1.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND 2)}
Fix $k\ge 3$.
For a finite set $B\subset \mathbf Z$, let $\mathrm{Type}_k(B)$ denote the number of types of nontrivial $k$-term arithmetic progressions contained in $B$ in the sense of O'Bryant's theorem for $D=1$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 5.1 (O'Bryant, specialized to $D=1$).}
Fix an integer $n\ge 2$ with
\[
k>2^{n-1}.
\]
Let $\Psi(N)\ge 2$ be any function.  Then every finite set $B\subset \mathbf Z$ of size $N$ satisfying
\[
\mathrm{Type}_k(B)\le N\Psi(N)
\]
contains a $k$-AP-free subset of size at least
\[
f_k(B)\gg_{k,n,\Psi} N\, (\log \Psi(N))^{1/(2n)}
\exp\!
\bigl(-C_{k,n}(\log \Psi(N))^{1/n}\bigr)
\]
for some constant $C_{k,n}>0$ depending only on $k$ and $n$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Justification.}
This is exactly O'Bryant's Theorem 1 for $D=1$ \cite{OBryant10}, rewritten with natural logarithms and with all harmless base-change constants absorbed into $C_{k,n}$ and the Vinogradov symbol.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 5.2.}
For every $N$-element set $B$,
\[
\mathrm{Type}_k(B)\le N^2.
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
A nontrivial $k$-term arithmetic progression is determined by its first two terms.  There are at most $N^2$ ordered choices for those two terms from $B$, so there are at most $N^2$ progression-types. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.3 (direct arbitrary-set lower bound).}
Let
\[
n_k:=\lceil \log_2 k\rceil.
\]
Then, for every $N\ge 2$,
\[
G_k(N)\gg_k N\, (\log N)^{1/(2n_k)}
\exp\!\bigl(-C_k(\log N)^{1/n_k}\bigr)
\]
for some constant $C_k>0$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
By definition of $n_k$, we have $k>2^{n_k-1}$, so Theorem 5.1 applies with $n=n_k$.
By Lemma 5.2 every $N$-element set $B$ satisfies
\[
\mathrm{Type}_k(B)\le N^2=N\cdot N,
\]
so we may take $\Psi(N)=N$.  Hence every such $B$ contains a $k$-AP-free subset of size at least
\[
\gg_k N\, (\log N)^{1/(2n_k)}
\exp\!\bigl(-C_k(\log N)^{1/n_k}\bigr).
\]
Minimizing over all $B$ of size $N$ gives the claimed bound for $G_k(N)$. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Special case $k=3$.}
Here $n_k=2$, so Corollary 5.3 becomes
\[
G_3(N)\gg N\, (\log N)^{1/4}\exp\!\bigl(-C\sqrt{\log N}\bigr).
\]
This is a direct arbitrary-set Behrend-type lower bound, proved without using Koml\'os--Sulyok--Szemer\'edi.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.4 (ruling out low-type-complexity families).}
Fix $A>0$.  Suppose $\mathcal B$ is a family of finite sets such that every $B\in \mathcal B$ of size $N$ satisfies
\[
\mathrm{Type}_k(B)\le N(\log N)^A.
\]
Then every such $B$ of sufficiently large size $N$ contains a $k$-AP-free subset of size at least
\[
f_k(B)\gg_{k,A} N\, (\log\log N)^{1/(2n_k)}
\exp\!\bigl(-C_{k,A}(\log\log N)^{1/n_k}\bigr).
\]
In particular, for $k=3$ one gets
\[
f_3(B)\gg_A N\, (\log\log N)^{1/4}
\exp\!\bigl(-C_A\sqrt{\log\log N}\bigr).
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Apply Theorem 5.1 with $n=n_k$ and $\Psi(N)=(\log N)^A$.
The conclusion follows immediately. \hfill$\square$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Interpretation.}
Corollary 5.4 rules out an entire class of candidate hard examples: sets whose $k$-term progression-type count is only polylogarithmic per element automatically contain progression-free subsets \emph{far denser} than the general Behrend scale.  So any family that is genuinely difficult for Problem \#201 must have substantially richer progression-type structure than that.

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
Again I try to break the new work.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Does O'Bryant's theorem really apply to ordinary arithmetic progressions?}  Yes.  It is stated for $k$-term $D$-progressions, and ordinary arithmetic progressions are exactly the case $D=1$.
\item \emph{Is $n_k=\lceil \log_2 k\rceil$ admissible?}  Yes, because then $2^{n_k-1}<k$.
\item \emph{Could the type count exceed $N^2$?}  No.  Once the first two terms are fixed, the common difference is fixed, hence the whole progression is fixed.
\item \emph{Did I accidentally claim a pointwise theorem with a constant depending on the individual set $B$?}  No.  Corollary 5.3 is a uniform class statement with $\Psi(N)=N$, and Corollary 5.4 is a uniform class statement with $\Psi(N)=(\log N)^A$.
\item \emph{Is the improvement genuinely beyond Round 1?}  Yes.  Round 1 used the external KSS comparison $R_k\ll_k G_k$; Round 2 derives a direct lower bound for $G_k$ from an arbitrary-set theorem and, more importantly, isolates a concrete structural obstruction via progression-type complexity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{7) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).}

Round 2 establishes a new direct theorem for Problem \#201:
\begin{quote}
Every $N$-element set of integers contains a $k$-AP-free subset of size at least
\[
\gg_k N\, (\log N)^{1/(2n_k)}\exp\!\bigl(-C_k(\log N)^{1/n_k}\bigr),
\qquad n_k=\lceil \log_2 k\rceil,
\]
without using the Koml\'os--Sulyok--Szemer\'edi comparison theorem.
\end{quote}
In addition, any family whose $k$-AP-type count is only $N(\log N)^A$ is ruled out as a genuinely hard family: such sets automatically contain much denser progression-free subsets than the generic Behrend scale.

The sharp ratio question
\[
\frac{R_3(N)}{G_3(N)}\to 1
\]
remains open, but the obstruction has been sharpened: any counterexample strategy must come from sets with very rich arithmetic-progression-type structure.
