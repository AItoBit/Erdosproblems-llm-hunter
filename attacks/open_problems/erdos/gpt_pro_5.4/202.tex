\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#202}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE}

\textbf{Path pursued: (C)-style obstruction sharpening / strict advance without a full resolution.}

Round~1 already identified that the main gap is the true asymptotic order of
\[
f(N):=\max\{r:\text{ there exist pairwise disjoint congruence classes }a_i \pmod{n_i},\ 2\le n_1<\cdots<n_r\le N\}.
\]
The most promising Round~2 move is therefore not to restart the global asymptotic problem, but to prove a \emph{new unconditional theorem} that is substantially stronger than the Round~1 elementary lower bound, and then place it against the current best-known asymptotic bounds from the literature.

The concrete target in this round is to improve the Round~1 elementary lower bound
\[
f(N)\ge \lfloor \log_2 N\rfloor
\]
to the polynomial lower bound
\[
f(N)\ge \lfloor \sqrt{N}\rfloor,
\]
with a completely self-contained proof.

\subsection*{2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED}

I use only the following Round~1 items.

\begin{itemize}
\item The formal definition of $f(N)$.
\item The disjointness criterion: two classes $a \pmod m$ and $b \pmod n$ are disjoint if and only if
\[
a\not\equiv b \pmod{\gcd(m,n)}.
\]
\item The Round~1 status: the full asymptotic problem remained unresolved.
\end{itemize}

I do \emph{not} reuse the Round~1 proof of the logarithmic lower bound, except as a benchmark to improve upon.

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)}

The new observation is that there is a very simple ``ladder'' construction with a \emph{common scale} $M$:
\[
1 \pmod M,\qquad 2 \pmod{2M},\qquad 3 \pmod{3M},\qquad \ldots,\qquad r \pmod{rM}.
\]
If $r\le M$, then any two residues differ by a number strictly between $0$ and $M$, while the gcd of the corresponding moduli is always a \emph{multiple} of $M$. This immediately forces disjointness.

Optimizing the choice of $M$ gives $r\asymp \sqrt{N}$.

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)}

The exact remaining gap after Round~1 was that the only fully proved lower bound in the write-up was logarithmic, while the actual problem asks for asymptotic growth much closer to linear.

In this round I will prove the following two statements.

\begin{enumerate}
\item For integers $M\ge 2$ and $1\le r\le M$, the congruence classes
\[
x\equiv j \pmod{Mj}\qquad (1\le j\le r)
\]
are pairwise disjoint.

\item As a corollary, for every $N\ge 2$,
\[
f(N)\ge \lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor.
\]
\end{enumerate}

This overcomes the specific Round~1 weakness that the only fully proved lower bound was logarithmic.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-2)}

\paragraph{Theorem 5.1.}
Let $M\ge 2$ and let $1\le r\le M$. Then the congruence classes
\[
\mathcal{C}_j:=\{x\in \mathbf{Z}: x\equiv j \pmod{Mj}\}\qquad (1\le j\le r)
\]
are pairwise disjoint.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Take $1\le i<j\le r$. By the Round~1 disjointness criterion, it is enough to show that
\[
i\not\equiv j \pmod{\gcd(Mi,Mj)}.
\]
Now
\[
\gcd(Mi,Mj)=M\gcd(i,j).
\]
So if the two classes intersected, we would have
\[
M\gcd(i,j)\mid (j-i).
\]
But since $1\le i<j\le r\le M$, we have
\[
0<j-i<M.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
M\gcd(i,j)\ge M.
\]
A positive integer strictly smaller than $M$ cannot be divisible by an integer at least $M$, so
\[
M\gcd(i,j)\nmid (j-i).
\]
Hence
\[
i\not\equiv j \pmod{\gcd(Mi,Mj)},
\]
and therefore $\mathcal{C}_i\cap \mathcal{C}_j=\emptyset$.

Since this holds for every pair $i<j$, the family $\mathcal{C}_1,\dots,\mathcal{C}_r$ is pairwise disjoint.
\hfill $\square$

\paragraph{Corollary 5.2.}
For every $N\ge 4$,
\[
f(N)\ge \lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Let
\[
M:=\lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor.
\]
Then $M\ge 2$ and $M^2\le N$. Apply Theorem~5.1 with $r=M$. The resulting moduli are
\[
M,\,2M,\,3M,\,\dots,\,M^2,
\]
which are distinct and all at most $N$. Therefore there are at least $M$ pairwise disjoint congruence classes with distinct moduli not exceeding $N$, so
\[
f(N)\ge M=\lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor.
\]
\hfill $\square$

\paragraph{Small-$N$ completion.}
For $N=2,3$ one has $f(N)=1$, so the same inequality
\[
f(N)\ge \lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor
\]
also holds there. Thus the bound is valid for every $N\ge 2$.

\paragraph{Concrete example.}
For $N=100$, taking $M=10$ yields the ten pairwise disjoint classes
\[
1 \pmod{10},\ 2 \pmod{20},\ 3 \pmod{30},\ \dots,\ 10 \pmod{100}.
\]
Hence
\[
f(100)\ge 10.
\]

\paragraph{Placement against the best current asymptotic bounds.}
The present theorem is \emph{not} competitive with the best known asymptotic lower bound. The currently recorded best bounds are
\[
\frac{N}{L(N)^{1+o(1)}}<f(N)<\frac{N}{L(N)^{\sqrt 3/2+o(1)}},
\qquad
L(N):=\exp\!\big(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}\big),
\]
due to de la Bret\`eche, Ford, and Vandehey, refining earlier work of Croot and Chen.

I am using this only as a checked external benchmark; the new theorem proved above is independent of those deep results.

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

I now try to break the new argument.

\paragraph{(i) Are the moduli distinct?}
Yes. They are $M,2M,\dots,rM$, and these are strictly increasing because $1,2,\dots,r$ are.

\paragraph{(ii) Are the residues legitimate representatives?}
Yes. For each $j\ge 1$,
\[
0<j<Mj,
\]
so $j$ is a valid residue modulo $Mj$.

\paragraph{(iii) Is the use of the disjointness criterion correct?}
Yes. The criterion is exactly the Round~1 lemma:
\[
a \pmod m \text{ and } b \pmod n \text{ intersect } \Longleftrightarrow a\equiv b \pmod{\gcd(m,n)}.
\]
I used it with $(a,m)=(i,Mi)$ and $(b,n)=(j,Mj)$.

\paragraph{(iv) Is the inequality $0<j-i<M$ really guaranteed?}
Yes, because the construction is only claimed for $r\le M$. This restriction is essential. If one tried to continue past $r=M$, the difference $j-i$ could become divisible by $M$, and the argument would fail.

\paragraph{(v) Does the corollary really keep every modulus $\le N$?}
Yes. The largest modulus is $M^2$, and $M=\lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor$ implies $M^2\le N$.

\paragraph{(vi) Does this solve the original asymptotic problem?}
No. It only gives a self-contained polynomial lower bound. The asymptotic order of $f(N)$ remains open.

No hidden quantifier issue remains in the proof above.

\subsection*{7) FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)}

\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).}

The strict advance over Round~1 is the new unconditional theorem
\[
f(N)\ge \lfloor \sqrt N\rfloor \qquad (N\ge 2),
\]
proved here from scratch. This is much stronger than the Round~1 elementary bound $f(N)\ge \lfloor \log_2 N\rfloor$.

The global asymptotic problem remains open. The currently recorded best asymptotic bounds are
\[
\frac{N}{L(N)^{1+o(1)}}<f(N)<\frac{N}{L(N)^{\sqrt 3/2+o(1)}}.
\]
