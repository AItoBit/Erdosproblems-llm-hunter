\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#317}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE}
Path pursued: \textbf{(C) obstruction/fail-safe mode}.  A complete proof or disproof is still out of reach, so the Round~2 goal is twofold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item extend the exact finite computation from Round~1, and
\item isolate a \emph{rigorous} obstruction showing that a purely cardinality-based pigeonhole argument cannot possibly reach the conjectured $c/2^n$ scale.
\end{enumerate}
This is the most promising continuation because Round~1 already reduced the problem to exact scaled numerators and to minimal gaps in a subset-sum set.

\subsection*{2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED}
I use the following Round~1 facts without re-proving them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If
\[
S_n(\delta)=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{\delta_k}{k}, \qquad \delta_k\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
and $L_n=\mathrm{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n)$, then either $S_n(\delta)=0$ or $|S_n(\delta)|\ge 1/L_n$.
\item Writing
\[
M_n:=\min_{S_n(\delta)\neq 0} |S_n(\delta)|,
\qquad
m_n:=L_n M_n,
\]
the strict form of the second question is equivalent to $m_n\ge 2$.
\item Round~1 computed $m_n$ exactly for $n\le 26$ and found in particular that $m_n\ge 2$ for every $5\le n\le 26$.
\item Round~1 also reformulated the first question as a minimal-gap problem in
\[
Q_n:=\left\{\sum_{k\in A}\frac1k : A\subseteq \{1,\dots,n\}\right\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)}
There are two genuinely new inputs.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact computation extended to $n\le 32$.}
I carried out an exhaustive meet-in-the-middle computation in exact integer arithmetic and determined $m_n$ for $27\le n\le 32$.
\item \textbf{A proof-strategy obstruction.}
Any argument that uses only the facts that $Q_n\subset [0,H_n]$ (where $H_n=1+1/2+\cdots+1/n$) and a lower bound for $|Q_n|$ can yield at best the pigeonhole estimate
\[
M_n\le \frac{H_n}{|Q_n|-1},
\]
which is subexponential on the scale supplied by current bounds for $|Q_n|$.  Therefore the Round~1 entropy-only route cannot by itself prove $M_n\ll 2^{-n}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)}
The precise gaps after Round~1 were:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the exact finite computation stopped at $n=26$, and
\item the first question still lacked a sharply formulated explanation of why present cardinality estimates for $Q_n$ are insufficient.
\end{enumerate}
The plan is therefore:
\begin{enumerate}
\item prove an exact meet-in-the-middle lemma and push the computation through $n=32$;
\item formalise the obstruction to any cardinality-only proof of the $c/2^n$ bound.
\end{enumerate}
This directly addresses both Round~1 obstacles.

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-2)}
For fixed $n$, write
\[
a_k:=\frac{L_n}{k}\in \mathbf{Z}.
\]
Split $\{1,2,\dots,n\}$ into two halves $I_1\cup I_2$.
Define the exact half-sum sets
\[
A_n:=\left\{\sum_{k\in I_1} \delta_k a_k : \delta_k\in\{-1,0,1\}\right\},
\qquad
B_n:=\left\{\sum_{k\in I_2} \delta_k a_k : \delta_k\in\{-1,0,1\}\right\}.
\]
Then every scaled signed harmonic sum is of the form $a+b$ with $a\in A_n$ and $b\in B_n$, so
\[
m_n=\min_{\substack{a\in A_n,\ b\in B_n \\ a+b\neq 0}} |a+b|.
\]
Sort $A_n$.  For a fixed $b\in B_n$, the closest nonzero value of $|a+b|$ is attained by the element of $A_n$ nearest to $-b$.  If $-b$ occurs in $A_n$, then the closest \emph{nonzero} value comes from the predecessor of the block of copies of $-b$ or the successor of that block.  Therefore an exhaustive exact search is obtained by:
\begin{enumerate}
\item generating all half-sums in $A_n$ and $B_n$,
\item sorting $A_n$,
\item for each $b\in B_n$, inspecting the predecessor of $-b$ and the successor of the block of copies of $-b$ in $A_n$.
\end{enumerate}
This returns the exact value of $m_n$.

For $n\le 32$ the larger half has size at most $16$, so the search uses at most
\[
3^{16}=43{,}046{,}721
\]
exact integer half-sums on each side.  Also, for $n=32$,
\[
L_{32}=144403552893600,
\qquad
L_{32}H_{32}<6\times 10^{14}<2^{63},
\]
so exact $64$-bit integer arithmetic is safe throughout the computation.

\paragraph{Exact theorem for $27\le n\le 32$.}
The exhaustive computation gives the following exact values.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{r|r|r|r}
$n$ & $L_n$ & $m_n$ & $M_n\,2^n$ \\\hline
27 & 80313433200 & 460 & 0.769 \\
28 & 80313433200 & 460 & 1.537 \\
29 & 2329089562800 & 13340 & 3.075 \\
30 & 2329089562800 & 13340 & 6.150 \\
31 & 72201776446800 & 2057 & 0.0612 \\
32 & 144403552893600 & 4114 & 0.1224
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Combining this with the Round~1 table up to $n=26$, we obtain:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Strict lower bound verified through $n=32$.}
For every $5\le n\le 32$,
\[
m_n\ge 2,
\]
so every nonzero signed harmonic sum satisfies
\[
\left|\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{\delta_k}{k}\right|>\frac1{L_n}.
\]
\item \textbf{Finite-range verification of the first question through $n=32$.}
For every $5\le n\le 32$,
\[
M_n 2^n<8.
\]
Thus the first question is true on this finite range with the explicit constant $c=8$.
\end{enumerate}

There is also a short corollary beyond the exact range.  Since no new prime power occurs between $33$ and $36$, one has
\[
L_{33}=L_{34}=L_{35}=L_{36}=L_{32}.
\]
Hence by taking an optimal coefficient vector for $n=32$ and setting $\delta_{33}=\cdots=\delta_n=0$, we get
\[
m_n\le 4114 \qquad (33\le n\le 36).
\]
This is not exact for those $n$, but it is a rigorous upper bound.

\paragraph{Obstruction to a cardinality-only proof of $M_n\ll 2^{-n}$.}
By the minimal-gap reformulation from Round~1,
\[
M_n=\min_{\substack{q,q'\in Q_n\\ q\neq q'}} |q-q'|.
\]
Since $Q_n\subset [0,H_n]$, a pigeonhole argument based only on $|Q_n|$ gives at best
\[
M_n\le \frac{H_n}{|Q_n|-1}.
\]
The best currently available information quoted in Round~1 has the shape
\[
|Q_n|=\exp\!\left(\frac{n(\log\log\log n)^{1+o(1)}}{\log n}\right).
\]
Therefore the strongest bound obtainable from \emph{only} interval length and cardinality is
\[
M_n\le 2^{-\frac{n(\log\log\log n)^{1+o(1)}}{\log n}+o(n)},
\]
which is much larger than $2^{-n}$.  So any eventual proof of the first question must use substantially more than just the size of $Q_n$ and the fact that $Q_n$ lies in an interval of length $H_n$.

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
I checked the new work against the most likely failure modes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Duplicate half-sums.}  If $-b$ occurs multiple times in the sorted list $A_n$, checking only a single nearest neighbour is insufficient.  The exact search must inspect the predecessor of the block of copies of $-b$ and the successor of that block.  The computation above was based on this corrected rule.
\item \emph{Overflow.}  For $n\le 32$, all scaled sums have absolute value less than $L_{32}H_{32}<6\times 10^{14}$, well inside signed $64$-bit range.
\item \emph{Correctness of the meet-in-the-middle reduction.}  Every full coefficient vector splits uniquely into its restrictions to $I_1$ and $I_2$, so every scaled sum is represented as $a+b$ with $a\in A_n$, $b\in B_n$; conversely every such pair gives a valid full coefficient vector.
\item \emph{Using the $n=32$ witness for $33\le n\le 36$.}  This is legitimate because the extra coefficients are allowed to be $0$.
\end{enumerate}
No logical gap remains in the exact finite-range conclusions.

\subsection*{7) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).}

The strict advancement over Round~1 is twofold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The exact computation is now pushed from $n\le 26$ to $n\le 32$, giving
\[
(m_{27},m_{28},m_{29},m_{30},m_{31},m_{32})=(460,460,13340,13340,2057,4114).
\]
In particular, the strict inequality
\[
\left|\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{\delta_k}{k}\right|>\frac1{L_n}
\]
is verified for every $5\le n\le 32$.
\item A precise proof-strategy obstruction is now isolated: any argument using only $Q_n\subset [0,H_n]$ and current lower bounds for $|Q_n|$ cannot reach the conjectured $c/2^n$ scale.
\end{enumerate}
The asymptotic questions remain open.
