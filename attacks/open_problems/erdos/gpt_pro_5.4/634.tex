\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 634. Find all values $N$ such that there exists a triangle which can be cut into $N$ congruent triangles.}

\subsection*{1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE}

I will show that the known set of attainable $N$ is substantially larger than the Round-1 writeup recorded, by adding (i) the triquadratic family and (ii) a recent theorem of Zhang implying that for every fixed integer $m\ge 1$, all sufficiently large numbers of the form $mn^2$ are attainable.

\subsection*{2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED}

\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-2)}
\paragraph{5.1. Classical attainable families.}
From the problem page we have
\[
n^2,\qquad 2n^2,\qquad 3n^2,\qquad 6n^2,\qquad n^2+m^2\in \mathcal N.
\]
We also know that $7,11\notin\mathcal N$, and $19$ is still open.

\paragraph{5.2. New family omitted in Round 1: triquadratic values.}
Beeson's \emph{Triangle Tiling III} gives a complete solution for the family of large triangles with angles $(2\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)$. The criterion is:
\[
N\in \mathcal N \text{ via such a triangle}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N=2K^2-M^2
\]
for some positive integers $K,M$ with $K\mid M^2$ (equivalently $K\mid N$).

This is a real enlargement of the known family list. For example,
\[
28=2\cdot 4^2-2^2
\]
with $4\mid 2^2$, so $28\in\mathcal N$.
Moreover $28$ is \emph{not} of any of the classical forms listed in \S5.1:
\begin{itemize}
\item it is not a square;
\item it is not $2n^2$, $3n^2$, or $6n^2$;
\item it is not a sum of two squares, since a direct check gives
\[
28-0^2=28,\quad 28-1^2=27,\quad 28-2^2=24,\quad 28-3^2=19,\quad 28-4^2=12,\quad 28-5^2=3,
\]
and none of these is a square.
\end{itemize}
Thus Round 1 really was incomplete: $28$ is a new explicit attainable value.

\paragraph{5.3. A strong corollary of the recent theorem on the problem page.}
The current problem page records the following theorem: for integers $a\ge b$, if
\[
n\ge 3\left\lceil \frac{a^2+b^2+ab-a-b}{ab}\right\rceil,
\]
then $n^2ab\in\mathcal N$.

Fix now any integer $m\ge 1$ and choose $(a,b)=(m,1)$. Then
\[
\frac{a^2+b^2+ab-a-b}{ab}
=
\frac{m^2+1+m-m-1}{m}=m,
\]
so the theorem yields the explicit corollary
\[
mn^2\in\mathcal N\qquad\text{for every } n\ge 3m.
\]
This is a substantial strengthening of the Round-1 discussion. In particular:
\begin{quote}
For every fixed $m\ge 1$, infinitely many multiples of $m$ belong to $\mathcal N$, and in fact every sufficiently large square-multiple $mn^2$ does.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{5.4. What remains open.}
The exact set $\mathcal N$ is still not known. The small obstructions $7$ and $11$ remain valid, and $19$ is still specifically open. The recent theorem proves large new infinite families, but not a complete characterization.

\subsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
Checks on possible weak points:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Could $28$ already belong to the old list?} No; the direct calculation in \S5.2 rules out the sum-of-two-squares possibility, and the other classical forms are obviously impossible.
\item \emph{Did the Zhang corollary use the theorem correctly?} Yes. Substituting $(a,b)=(m,1)$ gives the threshold $n\ge 3m$ exactly.
\item \emph{Did I overclaim a full classification?} No. I only enlarge the proven existence side of $\mathcal N$ and keep the exact determination open.
\end{enumerate}
