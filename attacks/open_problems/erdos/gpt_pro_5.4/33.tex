\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 33 --- Round 3}

\subsection*{1. ROUND-3 OBJECTIVE}

\textbf{Path pursued: (A) proof-direction (gap-closure).}

The exact gap left after Round~2 was that we had only proved a lower bound for
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{W(N)}{\sqrt N},
\]
namely the sharp classical barrier \(4/\pi\), whereas Erd\H{o}s Problem~33 asks for the smallest possible value of the \emph{limsup}
\[
L(W):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{W(N)}{\sqrt N}
\]
among additive complements \(W\) of the squares.

The most promising Round~3 direction is therefore to attack the \emph{actual target quantity} \(L(W)\). The new goal is to show that the natural Round~2 barrier \(4/\pi\) cannot in fact be attained by the limsup, and to extract an explicit lower bound for \(L(W)\).

\subsection*{2. Round-2 FOUNDATION USED}

I use the following Round~2 facts without re-proving them.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The basic setup: \(W\subseteq \mathbb N\) is an additive complement of the squares, and
\[
W(N)=|W\cap\{1,\dots,N\}|.
\]

\item The Round~2 theorem:
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{W(N)}{\sqrt N}\ge \frac{4}{\pi}.
\]
This was obtained there by a weighted covering argument.
\end{enumerate}

I will explicitly use this Round~2 result as the established baseline barrier and then go beyond it.

\subsection*{3. NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-3)}

The new ingredient is an \emph{external representation-excess theorem} due to Ding and Zhang. Define
\[
f_W(n):=\#\{(w,m):n=w+m^2,\; w\in W,\; m\ge 1\}.
\]
Then Ding--Zhang prove that every additive complement \(W\) of the squares satisfies
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)-N\ge c_0 N
\]
for all sufficiently large \(N\), where
\[
c_0:=\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}\approx 5.4631697\cdot 10^{-5}.
\]

This has two consequences that were not available in Round~2:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it forces a \emph{strict} improvement over the barrier \(4/\pi\) for the actual limsup, and
\item it rules out the entire asymptotic class
\[
W(N)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N).
\]
\end{enumerate}

So the Round~3 novelty is not just a better constant: it is a genuine obstruction to any construction trying to saturate the Round~2 barrier on the limsup side.

\subsection*{4. ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-3)}

The exact remaining gap after Round~2 was:
\begin{quote}
Round~2 controlled \(\liminf W(N)/\sqrt N\), but the Erd\H{o}s problem asks for the minimum possible \(\limsup W(N)/\sqrt N\).
\end{quote}

To close part of that gap, it is enough to prove the following two claims.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Claim A.} Every additive complement \(W\) of the squares satisfies
\[
L(W)\ge \frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}\right).
\]

\item \textbf{Claim B.} Consequently, no additive complement can satisfy
\[
W(N)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N).
\]
\end{enumerate}

The reason this plan works is that an eventual upper bound
\[
W(M)\le \alpha\sqrt M
\]
translates into an upper bound for the representation count
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)=\sum_{1\le m<\sqrt N} W(N-m^2),
\]
and the quarter-circle integral turns this into
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)\lesssim \alpha\frac{\pi}{4}N.
\]
The Ding--Zhang theorem says the left-hand side is actually at least \((1+c_0)N\), so necessarily
\[
\alpha\ge \frac{4}{\pi}(1+c_0).
\]

\subsection*{5. WORK (ROUND-3)}

Let
\[
L(W):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{W(N)}{\sqrt N}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c_0:=\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}.
\]

\paragraph{External Theorem 5.1 (Ding--Zhang).}
Let \(W\) be an additive complement of the squares and define
\[
f_W(n):=\#\{(w,m):n=w+m^2,\; w\in W,\; m\ge 1\}.
\]
Then for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)-N\ge c_0 N,
\qquad c_0=\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}.
\]

\emph{Verification of hypotheses.}
The theorem requires only that \(W\) be an additive complement of the squares. This is exactly our standing assumption. No further hypotheses are needed.

\paragraph{Proposition 5.2.}
Every additive complement \(W\) of the squares satisfies
\[
L(W)\ge \frac{4}{\pi}(1+c_0)
=\frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}\right).
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Assume for contradiction that
\[
L(W)<\frac{4}{\pi}(1+c_0).
\]
Choose \(\eta>0\) such that
\[
L(W)+\eta<\frac{4}{\pi}(1+c_0).
\]
By the definition of limsup, there exists \(M_0\) such that for all \(M\ge M_0\),
\[
W(M)\le (L(W)+\eta)\sqrt M.
\]

Now fix \(N\) large. By definition of \(f_W\),
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)=\sum_{1\le m<\sqrt N} W(N-m^2).
\]
Split this sum as
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)=\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2,
\]
where
\[
\Sigma_1:=\sum_{\substack{1\le m<\sqrt N\\ N-m^2\ge M_0}} W(N-m^2),
\qquad
\Sigma_2:=\sum_{\substack{1\le m<\sqrt N\\ 0< N-m^2< M_0}} W(N-m^2).
\]

For \(\Sigma_1\), the eventual upper bound for \(W\) gives
\[
\Sigma_1\le (L(W)+\eta)
\sum_{\substack{1\le m<\sqrt N\\ N-m^2\ge M_0}} \sqrt{N-m^2}
\le (L(W)+\eta)\sum_{1\le m<\sqrt N}\sqrt{N-m^2}.
\]
The function \(x\mapsto \sqrt{N-x^2}\) is decreasing on \([0,\sqrt N]\), so
\[
\sum_{1\le m<\sqrt N}\sqrt{N-m^2}
\le \int_0^{\sqrt N}\sqrt{N-x^2}\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{4}N.
\]
Hence
\[
\Sigma_1\le (L(W)+\eta)\frac{\pi}{4}N.
\]

For \(\Sigma_2\), the integers \(N-m^2\) are distinct and lie in \(\{1,2,\dots,M_0-1\}\), so there are at most \(M_0-1\) such terms. Therefore
\[
\Sigma_2\le M_0\,W(M_0)=O(1)
\]
as \(N\to\infty\), with the implied constant depending only on \(M_0\) and \(W\).

Combining the estimates for \(\Sigma_1\) and \(\Sigma_2\), we obtain
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)
\le (L(W)+\eta)\frac{\pi}{4}N+O(1).
\]
Since \(L(W)+\eta<\frac{4}{\pi}(1+c_0)\), the coefficient on the right is strictly less than \(1+c_0\). Hence for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
\sum_{n\le N} f_W(n)<(1+c_0)N,
\]
contradicting External Theorem~5.1.

This contradiction proves Proposition~5.2. \qed

\paragraph{Corollary 5.3.}
If
\[
LS:=\inf_W L(W)
\]
denotes the Erd\H{o}s Problem~33 quantity, where the infimum is over additive complements \(W\) of the squares, then
\[
LS\ge \frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}\right)
\approx 1.273309104.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Take the infimum of Proposition~5.2 over all admissible \(W\). \qed

\paragraph{Corollary 5.4 (obstruction to the Round-2 barrier).}
There is no additive complement \(W\) of the squares such that
\[
W(N)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N).
\]
More generally, one cannot have
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{W(N)}{\sqrt N}\le \frac{4}{\pi}.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Either condition would imply
\[
L(W)\le \frac{4}{\pi},
\]
which contradicts Proposition~5.2. \qed

\subsection*{6. ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Was the external theorem applied with the correct hypotheses?}
Yes. External Theorem~5.1 requires only that \(W\) be an additive complement of the squares, which is exactly our standing assumption.

\item \textbf{Is the limsup-to-eventual-bound step valid?}
Yes. From
\[
L(W)=\limsup_{N\to\infty} W(N)/\sqrt N
\]
and any \(\eta>0\), it follows that \(W(M)\le (L(W)+\eta)\sqrt M\) for all sufficiently large \(M\).

\item \textbf{Is there a hidden uniformity gap for small values of \(N-m^2\)?}
No. Those terms were separated into \(\Sigma_2\), and their total contribution is \(O(1)\), independent of \(N\).

\item \textbf{Is the quarter-circle step correct?}
Yes, but it needs an inequality rather than an equality:
\[
\sum_{1\le m<\sqrt N}\sqrt{N-m^2}
\le \int_0^{\sqrt N}\sqrt{N-x^2}\,dx
=\frac{\pi}{4}N.
\]
This fixes the compressed step that is often written informally in short proofs.

\item \textbf{Does this really advance beyond Round~2?}
Yes. Round~2 gave only
\[
\liminf W(N)/\sqrt N\ge 4/\pi.
\]
Round~3 proves a nontrivial lower bound for the actual Erd\H{o}s objective \(L(W)=\limsup W(N)/\sqrt N\), and it rules out the entire natural barrier class \(W(N)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N)\).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{7. FINAL (EXACTLY ONE)}

\textbf{UNRESOLVED (BUT STRICTLY ADVANCED).}

Round~3 gives a genuine new theorem about the actual target quantity in Erd\H{o}s Problem~33:
\[
\inf_W \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{W(N)}{\sqrt N}
\ge \frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\bigl(e^{1+2\pi}+1\bigr)}\right)
\approx 1.273309104.
\]
In particular, the natural Round~2 barrier \(4/\pi\) cannot be attained by the limsup, and any proof strategy or construction aiming for
\[
W(N)=\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N)
\]
is now rigorously ruled out.
