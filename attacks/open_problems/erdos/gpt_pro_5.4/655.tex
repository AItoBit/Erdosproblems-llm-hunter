\subsection*{Problem \#655}

\subsubsection*{1) ROUND-2 OBJECTIVE}
Path (C): turn the Round-1 disproof into the exact corrected statement. Round 1 already produced a regular $n$-gon counterexample, so the natural gap to close is the sharp lower bound that actually follows from the stated hypothesis.

\subsubsection*{2) Round-1 FOUNDATION USED}
I use the following vetted Round-1 fact.

The regular $n$-gon satisfies the hypothesis of the problem and determines exactly $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct distances. Therefore the claimed bound $(1+c)n/2$ is false for every fixed $c>0$.

I do not re-prove that counterexample here.

\subsubsection*{3) NEW INSIGHT / TOOL (ROUND-2)}
The hypothesis controls the multiplicity of \emph{each radius around a fixed point}. If one fixes a point $x_i$, then among the $n-1$ distances from $x_i$ to the other points, each distinct value can occur at most twice. Therefore $x_i$ itself determines at least $\lceil (n-1)/2\rceil$ distinct distances. Since these are all global interpoint distances, the whole configuration determines at least that many distinct distances.

Combined with the Round-1 regular $n$-gon construction, this gives the exact optimum.

\subsubsection*{4) ATTACK PLAN (ROUND-2)}
The only remaining gap after Round 1 is to identify the best possible lower bound replacing the false inequality
\[
|D|\geq (1+c)\frac n2.
\]
I will prove the sharp correction
\[
|D|\geq \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor,
\]
and then use the Round-1 regular $n$-gon to show equality is attainable for every $n$.

\subsubsection*{5) WORK (ROUND-2)}
Let $x_1,\dots,x_n$ be distinct points in the plane satisfying the hypothesis that for every $i$ and every radius $\rho>0$, the circle centred at $x_i$ with radius $\rho$ contains at most two of the other points.

Let
\[
D:=\{\,|x_i-x_j|:1\leq i<j\leq n\,\}
\]
be the global distance set.

For a fixed index $i$, define the set of distances seen from $x_i$ by
\[
D_i:=\{\,|x_i-x_j|:j\neq i\,\}.
\]
Every value in $D_i$ belongs to $D$, so
\[
|D|\geq |D_i| \qquad \text{for every } i.
\]
Now fix $i$. There are $n-1$ points other than $x_i$. By hypothesis, each radius about $x_i$ can hit at most two of them. Hence each element of $D_i$ accounts for at most two points, and therefore
\[
2|D_i|\geq n-1.
\]
So
\[
|D_i|\geq \left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil = \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]
Since $|D|\ge |D_i|$, we obtain the sharp universal lower bound
\[
|D|\geq \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]

Thus the exact corrected theorem is:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corrected theorem.}
For every $n\ge 1$, every $n$-point set in the plane satisfying the stated hypothesis determines at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct distances.

\medskip
To see that this is best possible, invoke the Round-1 construction: the $n$ vertices of a regular $n$-gon satisfy the hypothesis and determine exactly $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct distances. Therefore
\[
\min |D| = \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor
\]
over all such $n$-point configurations.

\subsubsection*{6) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Quantifiers.} The proof gives the lower bound for every $n\ge 1$, not merely for large $n$.
\item \textbf{Odd/even parity.} The identity $\lceil (n-1)/2\rceil=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ handles both parity cases exactly.
\item \textbf{Edge cases.} For $n=1$, the distance set is empty and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor=0$. For $n=2$, there is exactly one distance and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor=1$. The argument still works.
\item \textbf{No hidden assumption.} The proof uses only the stated circle-multiplicity hypothesis; it does not assume convexity, general position, or any structure beyond distinctness of the points.
\item \textbf{Sharpness.} The regular $n$-gon from Round 1 attains equality, so the correction cannot be improved.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{7) FINAL}
\textbf{FULL SOLUTION --- COUNTEREXAMPLE / DISPROOF}

The original statement is false. The exact corrected statement is:
\[
|D|\ge \left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor,
\]
and this bound is sharp for every $n$, with equality achieved by the regular $n$-gon.
