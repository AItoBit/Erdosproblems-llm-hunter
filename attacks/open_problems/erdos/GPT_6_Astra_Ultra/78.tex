\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#78: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
Find an explicit exponential lower-bound construction for diagonal Ramsey numbers: for some $C>1$ and all sufficiently large $k$, construct a graph with at least $C^k$ vertices and no clique or independent set of size $k$.

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{78.tex}.
The source correctly separates unbounded exhaustive search from efficient construction, but calling the intended problem ill-posed does not solve it. Polynomial-time output and a closed formula are not equivalent formal requirements. I improve its exhaustive search to a standard conditional-expectation algorithm, while making the remaining complexity gap explicit.
\subsection{2) WORK}
Let $n=\lfloor2^{k/3}\rfloor$, and let $X$ count homogeneous $k$-subsets of a uniformly random graph. Then
\[
\mathbb E X=2\binom nk2^{-\binom k2}\le2^{1-k^2/6+k/2}<1\qquad(k\ge6).
\]
Expose edges one at a time. Given any partial assignment, the conditional expectation is computable by summing over all $k$-subsets: a subset already containing both an edge and a nonedge contributes zero; a monochromatically assigned subset contributes the appropriate probability $2^{-u}$ for its $u$ unassigned internal edges; a completely unassigned subset contributes $2^{1-\binom k2}$. Of the two possible values of the next edge, at least one has conditional expectation at most the current one, since their average equals the current expectation. Choose that value.
At the end $X$ is a nonnegative integer below one, so $X=0$.

The naive implementation takes $O(n^2\binom nk k^2)$ rational-arithmetic operations, with $O(k^2)$-bit probability denominators (and polynomially bounded total bit lengths). Since $k=\Theta(\log n)$ this is $\exp(O((\log n)^2))$, much smaller than enumerating $2^{\binom n2}$ graphs. Also $n\ge2^{k/4}$ for $k\ge12$, so the output has the desired exponential size.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The final algorithm is deterministic and terminates, but its displayed running time is quasipolynomial, not polynomial in $n$. Choosing a low-expectation branch does not magically make the sum over all $k$-sets cheap. The construction is a standard derandomization, not a new Ramsey theorem.

An efficient explicit exponential construction in the intended strong sense remains unproved. The exact remaining algorithmic obstacle here is evaluating a suitable pessimistic estimator in polynomial time.
