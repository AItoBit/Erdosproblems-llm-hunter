\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#493: product minus sum}
\noindent Complete elementary reconstruction, 24 September 2026.

\subsection*{1) Statement}
Does a fixed positive integer $k$ exist such that every sufficiently large integer $n$ can be expressed as
\[n=\prod_{i=1}^k a_i-\sum_{i=1}^k a_i,\qquad a_i\in\mathbb Z,\quad a_i\ge2?\]
Under exactly these conditions the answer is yes, with $k=2$.

\subsection*{2) Construction and its exact range}
For every integer $n\ge0$, choose $a_1=2$, $a_2=n+2$. Both are at least two and
\[a_1a_2-a_1-a_2=2(n+2)-2-(n+2)=n.\]
Thus no sufficiently-large threshold beyond $n\ge0$ is necessary. For $n>0$ the two chosen integers are even distinct, although distinctness is not required.

More generally, every ordered solution with $k=2$ corresponds exactly to a positive divisor $d$ of $n+1$:
\[n+1=(a_1-1)(a_2-1),\qquad
a_1=d+1,\quad a_2=(n+1)/d+1.\]
This proves both that every nonnegative integer occurs and that no negative integer occurs for $k=2$. The number of ordered representations is $\tau(n+1)$. The fixed number of variables two is minimal for covering positive integers, since $k=1$ always gives $a_1-a_1=0$.
