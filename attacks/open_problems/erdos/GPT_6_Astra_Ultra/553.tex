\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 553}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Let \(R(3,3,n)\) force either a triangle in one of the first two
edge colours or a clique of order \(n\) in the third. Prove
\(R(3,3,n)/R(3,n)\to\infty\).
We reconstruct a random-permutation argument, using Kim's
triangle-free graph theorem as an explicit external input, to obtain
\[
 R(3,3,n)\gg \frac{n^3}{(\log n)^4}. \tag{1}
\]
An elementary quadratic upper bound for \(R(3,n)\) then suffices.

\paragraph{The one external graph-existence input.}
Kim's theorem implies that some absolute \(c>0\) has the following
property: for every sufficiently large integer \(f\), there is a
triangle-free graph on at least \(cf^2/\log f\) vertices with
independence number less than \(f\).
Taking an induced subgraph and decreasing \(c\) if necessary, we may
take its order to be \(q=\lfloor cf^2/\log f\rfloor\), with \(0<c\leq1\).
The proof of this deep existence theorem is not supplied here.

\paragraph{Count independent sets after replacing vertices by independent sets.}
Replace each vertex of this graph by \(r\) independent vertices,
and each edge by the complete bipartite graph between its two parts.
The resulting graph \(F\) has \(N=qr\) vertices and is triangle-free:
any triangle would have to use three different parts and project to
a triangle in the original graph.

An independent set of \(n\) vertices in \(F\) meets fewer than
\(f\) parts. Extend those part labels to any \(f\)-subset of the
\(q\) labels. Hence, writing \(I\) for the number of such independent
sets, we have the overcounting bound
\[
 I\leq\binom qf\binom{fr}{n}. \tag{2}
\]
If \(fr<n\), there are no such sets and the desired construction
already follows. We therefore use the following estimates when
\(fr\geq n\).

\paragraph{Two randomly relabelled copies.}
Let \(F'\) be the image of \(F\) under a uniformly random permutation
of its \(N\) vertices. Each fixed \(n\)-subset is independent in
\(F'\) with probability \(I/\binom Nn\).
Thus the expected number of sets independent in both graphs is
\(I^2/\binom Nn\). If this is less than one, some permutation makes
that number zero.
Colour edges of \(F\) with colour 1, edges of \(F'\) not already
coloured with colour 2, and all remaining edges with colour 3.
The first two colour classes are triangle-free. A third-colour
\(K_n\) would be independent in both graphs, a contradiction.
Therefore
\[
 I^2<\binom Nn\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(3,3,n)>N. \tag{3}
\]
Overlap between the two graphs causes no difficulty because the
second colour is only a subgraph of \(F'\).

\paragraph{Parameters and the first-moment inequality.}
For every sufficiently large \(n\), take
\[
 f=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4\log n}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 q=\left\lfloor\frac{cf^2}{\log f}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 r=\left\lfloor\frac{nq}{100f^2}\right\rfloor.
\]
These are positive and \(q\geq f\) eventually. Also
\(\log(eq/f)\leq2\log n\) eventually.
Using \(\binom ab\leq(ea/b)^b\) and
\(\binom Nn\geq(N/n)^n\), (2) gives
\[
 \frac{I^2}{\binom Nn}
 \leq\left[
 \left(\frac{eq}{f}\right)^{2f/n}
 \frac{e^2f^2r}{nq}\right]^n
 \leq\left(\frac{e^3}{100}\right)^n<1. \tag{4}
\]
Here \((2f/n)\log(eq/f)\leq1\), and the bound on \(r\)
handles the second factor. Since the quantity being rounded in
\(r\) tends to infinity, the floor loses at most a factor two for
large \(n\). Thus
\[
 N=qr\gg\frac{nq^2}{f^2}
 \gg\frac{nf^2}{(\log f)^2}
 \asymp\frac{n^3}{(\log n)^4},
\]
proving (1) for every sufficiently large \(n\), not just a subsequence.

\paragraph{An elementary denominator bound.}
The usual vertex-neighbourhood argument gives
\(R(s,t)\leq R(s-1,t)+R(s,t-1)\): at a chosen vertex, either
the first-colour neighbourhood is large enough to force the first
term, or the other neighbourhood is large enough for the second.
Induction with \(R(2,t)=t\) yields
\(R(3,n)\leq\binom{n+1}{2}\).
Consequently (1) implies
\[
 \frac{R(3,3,n)}{R(3,n)}
 \gg\frac{n}{(\log n)^4}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
