\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#740: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
For an infinite cardinal $\kappa$ and fixed $r$, must every $\kappa$-chromatic graph contain a $\kappa$-chromatic subgraph with no odd cycle of length at most $r$?

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{740.tex}.
The source proves a correct equivalence between the countably chromatic version and a finite forcing statement $A_r$, cites the known triangle-free case, and describes a consistency obstruction at $\aleph_1$. I retain those distinctions and prove a stronger conditional diagonal consequence, without treating it as unconditional.
\subsection{2) WORK}
Write $A_r$ for: for each $k$ there is a finite threshold $N_r(k)$ forcing a subgraph with chromatic number at least $k$ and no odd cycle of length at most $r$.

\paragraph{Conditional simultaneous cleanup.}
Assume $A_r$ holds for every positive integer $r$. If $\chi(G)=\aleph_0$, then $G$ contains a countable subgraph $H$ with $\chi(H)=\aleph_0$ such that, for every fixed $L$, only finitely many cycles of odd length at most $L$ occur in $H$.

Construct pairwise disjoint finite vertex sets $S_j$ with
$\chi(G[S_j])\ge N_j(j)$. Removing finitely many vertices leaves infinite chromatic number, and finite-coloring compactness supplies each next finite set. Apply $A_j$ to get a finite subgraph $H_j$ of chromatic number at least $j$ with no odd cycle of length at most $j$. Take the union of the $H_j$ and delete all edges between different blocks. Then $\chi(H)=\aleph_0$. For a fixed $L$, any prohibited cycle must lie in one of the finitely many finite blocks $H_j$ with $j<L$; there are only finitely many such cycles. Removing those blocks also leaves infinite chromatic number and odd girth greater than $L$.

This conclusion is stronger than finding a different subgraph separately for each fixed $L$, but does not make $H$ bipartite. The exceptional initial blocks depend on $L$, and their union over all $L$ can exhaust $H$. An interchange of these quantifiers would incorrectly produce a bipartite graph of infinite chromatic number.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The construction is conditional on all the finite statements, including the unresolved larger odd-cycle thresholds. Finite compactness only supplies arbitrarily large finite chromatic numbers; it cannot preserve an uncountable chromatic cardinal. Edge deletion is allowed for this problem, unlike the induced-tree question.
\subsection{4) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED.}
The statements $A_r$ for larger odd $r$ have not been proved here, and the uncountable assertion is not a consequence of finite compactness. The conditional diagonal result leaves the original question unresolved.
