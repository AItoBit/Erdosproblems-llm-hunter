\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 338: restricted additive order}
\subsection*{Statement and scope}
The restricted order of $A\subset\mathbb N$ is the least $t$ such that every sufficiently large integer is a sum of at most $t$ distinct elements. The problem asks when it exists, how it compares with ordinary order, and what robustness under finite deletions implies.

We completely characterize the family consisting of all multiples of a modulus together with finitely many exceptions, and give a density condition guaranteeing restricted order at most two. The general characterization and finite-deletion questions are unresolved here. Source: \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/338}. This retains Bateman's valid example from the earlier GPT-5.2 attempt, but replaces its inaccurate account of the remaining questions.

\subsection*{An exact modular criterion}
Let $q\ge2$, let $F$ be a finite set of positive integers none divisible by $q$, and put $A=q\mathbb N\cup F$. For each residue $r\bmod q$, define
\[
 c_r=\min\{j:r\text{ is a sum of }j\text{ residues from }F
                  \text{ with repetition}\},
\]
and
\[
 s_r=\min\{|E|:E\subset F,\ \sum_{e\in E}e\equiv r\pmod q\}.
\]
An unavailable minimum is infinite; the empty sum gives $c_0=s_0=0$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(A)=1+\max_r c_r,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{\rm restricted}(A)=1+\max_r s_r, \tag{1}
\]
with infinity interpreted as absence of a finite order.

For the upper bounds, choose for each residue a minimizing multiset or subset from $F$. For every sufficiently large $n$ in that residue, subtract its fixed sum and use the remaining positive multiple of $q$ as one additional summand. In the restricted case that extra summand is distinct from all chosen elements because none of them is divisible by $q$.

For the lower bounds, a representation with at most $t$ summands of a sufficiently large integer must use an element of $q\mathbb N$: a sum of at most $t$ members of the fixed finite set $F$ is bounded. Removing all multiples of $q$ leaves at most $t-1$ members of $F$ with the required residue, counted with or without repetition as appropriate. This proves (1).

In particular ordinary order is finite precisely when $\gcd(q,F)=1$. Indeed the nonnegative sums of residues of $F$ form the subgroup they generate in the finite cyclic group: an inverse of any residue is a nonnegative multiple of it. Restricted order is finite precisely when the subset sums of the actual finite set $F$ cover every residue modulo $q$.

\subsection*{Examples and exact comparisons}
With $F=\{1\}$ and $q=h\ge3$, we have $\max c_r=h-1$, so the ordinary order is exactly $h$. The only restricted residues are zero and one, so no restricted order exists. This is Bateman's example, with its exact ordinary order verified. For $q=2$ the same set has both orders two.

For another explicit separation, take $q=7$, $F=\{1,2,3\}$. Every residue is a sum of at most two elements of $F$ with repetition, and not all are single residues, so the ordinary order is three. Subset sums cover $0,\ldots,6$; residue six needs all three elements, while the other residues need at most two. Thus the restricted order is exactly four.

\subsection*{A sufficient density hypothesis}
Suppose $\underline d(A)>1/2$. Choose $1/2<\delta<\underline d(A)$. For all sufficiently large $n$, let $E=A\cap[1,n-1]$; then $|E|\ge\delta(n-1)$. The reflected set $n-E$ lies in the same interval and has the same size, so
\[
 |E\cap(n-E)|\ge2|E|-(n-1)\ge(2\delta-1)(n-1)>1.
\]
At most one point is $n/2$. Some other point $a$ in the intersection therefore gives $n=a+(n-a)$ with two distinct elements of $A$. Thus the restricted order is at most two. It is one exactly when $A$ contains every sufficiently large integer.

\subsection*{Remaining gap}
The modular criterion is exact for the specified class, but that class has essential finite exceptions: deleting all of $F$ leaves only multiples of $q$. It therefore does not answer the questions where every finite deletion remains a basis, with or without a uniform order. General bases outside the density hypothesis are likewise not characterized here.
