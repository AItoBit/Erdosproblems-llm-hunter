\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 501: independent sets for small-measure set mappings}
\subsection*{The two questions and their known resolution}
For every real $x$, let $A_x$ be bounded with Lebesgue outer measure $\lambda^*(A_x)<1$. Call $X$ independent if $x\notin A_y$ for all distinct $x,y\in X$. The first question asks whether every such family has an infinite independent set. The second asks for three independent points when all $A_x$ are closed.

The first assertion is independent of ZFC, in the usual relative-consistency sense; the second is a theorem, with an infinite set in place of three points. We reconstruct the CH counterexample and the closed-set theorem in detail. For the positive consistency direction we explicitly import the random-real theorem stated below. This is an exposition of known work, not a new resolution or a claim to have run Lean locally.

The construction is due to Hechler. The closed-set argument is due to Newelski, Pawlikowski and Seredy\'nski, \emph{Infinite free set for small measure set mappings}, Proc.\ Amer.\ Math.\ Soc.\ 100 (1987), 335--339, \url{https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1987-0884475-3}. We checked the proof against the author-linked exposition and formalization in \url{https://github.com/elliotglazer/erdos501/blob/main/Erdos501/Closed.lean}. Current problem statement: \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/501}.

\subsection*{A complete counterexample under CH}
Assume CH and enumerate the reals without repetition as $(x_\alpha)_{\alpha<\omega_1}$. Define
\[
 A_{x_\alpha}
 =\{x_\beta:\beta<\alpha,\ |x_\beta|\le |x_\alpha|+1\}. \tag{1}
\]
Every $\alpha<\omega_1$ is countable, so (1) is countable and has outer measure zero. It is bounded by $|x_\alpha|+1$. Thus it satisfies every hypothesis of the first question.

If $X$ were an infinite independent set, repeatedly take the least unused ordinal index in $X$, obtaining
$\alpha_0<\alpha_1<\cdots$. For $i<j$, independence says $x_{\alpha_i}\notin A_{x_{\alpha_j}}$. In view of (1),
\[
 |x_{\alpha_i}|>|x_{\alpha_j}|+1.
\]
Iteration gives $|x_{\alpha_j}|<|x_{\alpha_0}|-j$, impossible for a sufficiently large integer $j$. Hence the first assertion fails under CH. The argument uses a countable sequence extracted from the well-order; it does not assume that all of $X$ has order type $\omega$.

\subsection*{Outer-measure tools for the closed case}
We use two elementary properties of Lebesgue outer measure, including for sets that need not be measurable.

First, for increasing sets $B_j$,
$\lambda^*(\bigcup_jB_j)=\lim_j\lambda^*(B_j)$.
If the limit on the right is finite, choose measurable hulls $H_j\supset B_j$ of the same outer measure and replace $H_j$ by $\bigcap_{k\ge j}H_k$. These new hulls increase, still contain $B_j$, and have measure at most $\lambda^*(B_j)$. Continuity of measure for their union proves the assertion. If the limit is infinite, monotonicity suffices.

Second, for a fixed $B$ of finite outer measure, the assignment
\[
 \nu(D)=\lambda^*(B\cap D)
\]
is a finite measure on the Borel sets $D$. This follows from the Carath\'eodory splitting property of measurable sets, first for finite Borel partitions and then for countable partitions. Thus it has the usual continuity from above and below. Measurable hulls of sets of finite outer measure exist by the defining interval-cover approximation to Lebesgue outer measure.

\subsection*{The preservation lemma}
Assume each $A_x$ is closed and $\lambda(A_x)<1$. We claim that every $Y\subset\mathbb R$ of infinite outer measure contains an $x$ for which
\[
 C_Y(x)=\{t\in Y:t\ne x,\ t\notin A_x,\ x\notin A_t\}
\]
still has infinite outer measure.

Suppose instead that every $C_Y(x)$, $x\in Y$, has finite outer measure. Set
$B_x=\{t\in Y:x\notin A_t\}$. Since
\[
 B_x\subset C_Y(x)\cup A_x\cup\{x\},
\]
each $B_x$ has finite outer measure. The sets
$Y_M=\{x\in Y:\lambda^*(B_x)\le M\}$, $M\in\mathbb N$, increase to $Y$. By the first outer-measure fact, some $M$ has $\lambda^*(Y_M)>1$. Intersecting with a sufficiently large bounded interval gives $Y'\subset Y_M$ with
\[
 1<a:=\lambda^*(Y')<\infty.
\]
Put $\eta=(a-1)/2>0$.

For each $t\in Y$, the open set $\mathbb R\setminus A_t$ is an increasing union of finite unions of open intervals with rational endpoints. The second outer-measure fact, with $B=Y'$, therefore supplies such a finite union $U_t\subset\mathbb R\setminus A_t$ with
\[
 \lambda^*(Y'\setminus U_t)<1+\eta=a-\eta,
 \qquad \lambda^*(Y'\cap U_t)>\eta. \tag{2}
\]
There are only countably many possible finite unions $U_t$. Enumerate them and use the first outer-measure fact again to choose a finite collection $\mathcal U$ for which
\[
 Z=\{t\in Y:U_t\in\mathcal U\},
 \qquad \lambda^*(Z)>\frac{aM}{\eta}. \tag{3}
\]

Partition $\mathbb R$ into the finitely many Borel atoms $D_\sigma$ of the Boolean algebra generated by $\mathcal U$, and put
$w_\sigma=\lambda^*(Y'\cap D_\sigma)$. Their weights sum to $a$. For each positive-weight atom choose $z_\sigma\in Y'\cap D_\sigma$. Because membership in every $U\in\mathcal U$ is constant on an atom, the set
\[
 K_\sigma=\{t\in Z:D_\sigma\subset U_t\}
\]
is contained in $\{t\in Y:z_\sigma\notin A_t\}=B_{z_\sigma}$. It therefore has outer measure at most $M$. Choose a measurable hull $H_\sigma\supset K_\sigma$ of measure at most $M$ and define the measurable nonnegative function
\[
 g(t)=\sum_{w_\sigma>0}w_\sigma\,\mathbf1_{H_\sigma}(t).
\]
Its integral is at most $aM$. For each $t\in Z$, the atoms contained in $U_t$ have total weight $\lambda^*(Y'\cap U_t)>\eta$ by (2); all their positive weights contribute to $g(t)$. Thus $Z\subset\{g>\eta\}$, and Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \lambda^*(Z)\le\lambda\{g>\eta\}\le\frac{aM}{\eta},
\]
contradicting (3). This proves the preservation lemma. In particular, the proof never assumes that the two-variable relation $x\in A_y$ is measurable.

\subsection*{An infinite independent set for closed values}
Start with $Y_0=\mathbb R$. Apply the lemma to choose $x_j\in Y_j$ and define
$Y_{j+1}=C_{Y_j}(x_j)$, which has infinite outer measure. The sets $Y_j$ decrease. If $i<j$, then $x_j\in Y_{i+1}$, so $x_i\ne x_j$, $x_j\notin A_{x_i}$, and $x_i\notin A_{x_j}$. Consequently $\{x_j:j\ge0\}$ is an infinite independent set.

This proves the second question and more. Boundedness of the individual closed sets was not needed; their uniformly bounded measures were enough.

\subsection*{The explicit external input for relative independence}
For the remaining direction we use Theorem 1.1 of Glazer and Sol, \emph{Erd\H{o}s Problem 501 after adding $\omega_2$ random reals} (2026), available in the primary author's repository:
\url{https://github.com/elliotglazer/erdos501/blob/main/docs/paper/erdos501_random_profiles_rev10.pdf}.
Its precise statement is: over any ground model of ZFC+CH, forcing with the measure algebra adding its $\omega_2$ random reals produces a model in which every family $(A_y)_{y\in\mathbb R}$ with $\lambda^*(A_y)<1$ has an infinite independent set. The theorem even omits boundedness. This forcing theorem is an imported dependency; its countable-support, homogeneous-reading and fresh-coordinate arguments are not reproved here.

The standard constructible-universe and forcing relative-consistency theorems now give the two conclusions. If ZFC is consistent, then ZFC+CH is consistent, and (1) gives the negative answer there. Starting from a CH model and applying the cited random-real theorem gives the consistency of the positive answer. Thus, conditional only on consistency of ZFC, neither the assertion nor its negation is provable in ZFC. No large-cardinal assumption is imported.

\subsection*{Scope and correction of the earlier example}
The CH construction and the closed-set theorem are proved above. The full independence conclusion is complete relative to the expressly named random-real and standard relative-consistency inputs; no unconditional ZFC counterexample to the first assertion is claimed.

For the family $A_x=[x,x+1/2]$, an integer-spaced sequence is independent. An arbitrary decreasing sequence need not be: $x_j=1/j$ has $x_2=1/2\in A_{x_3}=[1/3,5/6]$. This corrects the earlier GPT-5.2 sanity example while preserving its original source file offline and in its original model folder.
