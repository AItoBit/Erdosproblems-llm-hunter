\subsection*{1) OBJECTIVE AND FINITE CHECK}
A weak $(4,5)$ set of real numbers has at least five distinct positive distances on every four-element subset. The source reduces its strong $(3,3)$ subsets to sets containing no three-term arithmetic progression, and supplies
\[
A=\{0,136,200,243,246,249,272,286,298,323,400,528,596,1056\}.
\]
I checked all $\binom{14}{4}=1001$ four-element subsets using integer arithmetic. Every one has at least five distances. There are exactly twelve arithmetic-progression triples. Enumerating all $2^{14}$ subsets gives maximum progression-free size eight. These checks are reproduced in \texttt{verification/root\_checks.py}.
\subsection*{2) REPLICATION WITHOUT CREATING EXTRA RELATIONS}
Ordinary translated copies fail: equal internal distances in two copies violate the four-point requirement. Instead fix a positive transcendental real $t$ and form
\[
B_k=\bigcup_{i=1}^k\{t^{2i}+a t^{2i+1}:a\in A\}.
\]
Different listed points are distinct, because a nonzero integer polynomial cannot vanish at $t$.
Any equality of positive distances can be written $x-y=\pm(z-w)$. For four distinct endpoints in more than one block, comparison of the coefficients of each $t^{2i}$ forces each occupied block to occur equally often on the two sides. The only mixed possibility is two blocks each containing one point on each side; comparison of $t^{2i+1}$ then forces those points to coincide, a contradiction. For three distinct endpoints a repeated distance means $2x=y+z$. Its constant-block coefficients force all three into the same block.
Therefore all nontrivial distance repetitions are internal. Four points in one block inherit the property of $A$; three in one block and one outside have at most the one distance repetition possible on three real points; every other mixed four-set has all six distances distinct. Thus $B_k$ is weak $(4,5)$.
\subsection*{3) EXACT INDEPENDENCE RATIO}
The same coefficient argument shows that every arithmetic progression in $B_k$ lies in a single block. A progression-free subset has at most eight points in each block, and independent choices of eight-point progression-free subsets attain this bound. Consequently
\[
|B_k|=14k,\qquad \alpha_{\rm AP}(B_k)=8k.
\]
The finite $4/7$ obstruction therefore persists at arbitrarily large sizes, rather than being an isolated fourteen-point effect.
