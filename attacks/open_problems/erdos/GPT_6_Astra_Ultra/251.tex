\subsection*{2) WORK}
Define $f(x)=\tfrac12(x+1000)\log(x+1000)$, $b_n=\lfloor f(n)\rfloor$, and, for $n\ge1$,
\[
c_n=2(2b_{n-1}-b_n)+1.
\]
These are positive odd integers, strictly increasing, and $c_n\sim n\log n$. Here are direct verifications. Let $g(x)=2f(x-1)-f(x)$. For $x\ge1$,
\[
g'(x)=2f'(x-1)-f'(x)
\ge\tfrac12(\log1000+1)-\frac1{2000}>3.
\]
This uses $f''(t)=1/(2(t+1000))$. Since $2b_{n-1}-b_n-g(n)$ lies in $(-2,1)$, consecutive values of $2b_{n-1}-b_n$ differ by more than zero. Positivity follows already at $n=1$ from $g(1)>2$ and then monotonicity. Finally $g(n)=f(n)-2(f(n)-f(n-1))\sim\tfrac12 n\log n$, proving the asymptotic.

Nevertheless the series is an integer. For every $N$,
\[
\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{2b_{n-1}-b_n}{2^n}=b_0-\frac{b_N}{2^N}.
\]
Letting $N\to\infty$ yields
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c_n}{2^n}=2b_0+1\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus increasing odd integer coefficients with precisely the prime first-order scale $n\log n$ can satisfy all the tail lattice relations and still give a rational sum. A successful proof must use additional arithmetic of the primes.
