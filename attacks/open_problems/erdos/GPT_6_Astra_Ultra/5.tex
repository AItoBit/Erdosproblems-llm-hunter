\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#5: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
For primes $p_n$, is the set $S$ of finite limit points of $(p_{n+1}-p_n)/\log p_n$ all of $[0,\infty)$?

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{5.tex}.
The source constructs infinitely many complementary holes satisfying the four-point difference property $(P_4)$. Its particular model actually satisfies the stronger three-point property. This improves the logical audit, not the prime-gap theorem.
\subsection{2) WORK}
Fix $\epsilon>0$, $M>\epsilon$ and $R>2+2\epsilon/M$. Put
\[
B=\bigcup_{j\ge0}(MR^j,MR^j+\epsilon),\qquad A=[0,\infty)\setminus B.
\]
I claim $B$ is sum-free: $(B+B)\cap B=\varnothing$. If $x,y\in B$, and $a=MR^j$ is the larger of their two hole origins, then
\[
a+\epsilon<a+M<x+y<2(a+\epsilon)<Ra.
\]
There are no holes between $a+\epsilon$ and $Ra$, which proves the claim. Given $\beta_1\le\beta_2\le\beta_3$, if both consecutive differences are in $B$, their sum is in $A$; if one is outside $B$ there is already a difference in $A$. Hence $A$ has $(P_3)$, and therefore $(P_k)$ for every $k\ge3$.

In particular the supplied choice $R=4$, $M>3\epsilon$ has this stronger property. Nevertheless $A$ is closed and omits every nonempty open interval $(MR^j,MR^j+\epsilon)$. It is not dense. Its complement has infinite measure and only $O(\log T)$ measure below $T$, by counting the geometrically spaced origins. Thus even adding $(P_3)$ to the listed geometric hypotheses cannot force density for arbitrary model sets.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The argument never asserts that the actual prime-gap set satisfies $(P_3)$. A model satisfying a stronger property still refutes the corresponding abstract implication, but cannot refute a conjecture about primes. All inequalities are strict because hole endpoints belong to $A$.
