\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
For the largest $f(N)$ of a subset of $[1,N]$ with no relation $1/a=\sum_i1/b_i$ among distinct selected integers, determine its asymptotic size; the target is the half-density bound.

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{301.tex}.
The supplied disjoint-block proof yields $f(N)\le(53/60+o(1))N$. I enlarge the block and obtain the rigorously reproducible local improvement $349/403$. This is an improvement over the supplied argument, not a claim of a new literature record.
\subsection{2) WORK}
Take
\[
T=\{2^i3^j:0\le i<5,\ 0\le j<3\}
=\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12,16,18,24,36,48,72,144\}.
\]
For its increasing prefixes the minimum numbers of omissions needed to avoid all reciprocal relations are
\[
0,0,0,0,1,1,1,2,2,3,3,4,5,6,7. \tag{1}
\]
I verified (1) by exact exhaustive enumeration of the at most $2^{15}$ subsets. The saved script enumerates every potential smallest denominator $a$, sums every subset of larger reciprocals using \texttt{Fraction}, records each equality as a forbidden bit mask, then maximizes cardinality among masks containing no forbidden mask. Positivity guarantees that the right-hand denominators must all exceed $a$, so this enumerates every relation. This finite computation is part of the proof and is fully reproducible.

Choose block origins $d$ with $v_2(d)\equiv0\pmod5$ and $v_3(d)\equiv0\pmod3$. Their density is
\[
\delta={1/3\over(1-2^{-5})(1-3^{-3})}={144\over403}.
\]
This follows by writing $d=32^u27^v w$ with $(w,6)=1$ and summing the convergent geometric densities; truncation leaves $o(X)$ error in the counting function. The sets $dT$ are disjoint: the valuations modulo $(5,3)$ determine the multiplier in $T$, and then determine $d$.

By (1), an additional omission is forced at each cutoff multiplier $6,12,18,36,48,72,144$. Summing distinct omissions gives
\[
N-f(N)\ge\left({144\over403}\left({1\over6}+{1\over12}+{1\over18}+{1\over36}+{1\over48}+{1\over72}+{1\over144}\right)+o(1)\right)N
=\left({54\over403}+o(1)\right)N.
\]
Hence $f(N)\le(349/403+o(1))N$, strictly below $53/60$.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The proof relies on all exact local relations, including those with more than two right-hand terms. It is not based on floating point equality or random search. Blocks may be truncated at $N$; this is exactly why every prefix omission number is computed. Relations between different blocks can only further restrict admissibility, so ignoring them is safe for an upper bound.
