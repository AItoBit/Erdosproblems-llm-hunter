\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 907: continuous differences}

\paragraph{Statement and approach.}
If \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) has continuous differences
\(\Delta_t f(x)=f(x+t)-f(x)\) for every \(t>0\), then
\(f=g+H\), where \(g\) is continuous and \(H\) is additive.
We prove the reduction to a precise measurable-cocycle
theorem and then supply the regularity argument.
The cocycle theorem is an explicit external dependency.

\paragraph{From separate continuity to a measurable defect.}
Negative shifts cause no difficulty, since
\(\Delta_{-t}f(x)=-\Delta_t f(x-t)\).
Define
\[
 D(x,y)=f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y).
\]
For fixed \(y\), this is continuous in \(x\); symmetry
gives continuity in \(y\) for fixed \(x\).
To check joint measurability without assuming \(f\)
itself measurable, put
\[
 D_j(x,y)=D(2^{-j}\lfloor2^jx\rfloor,y).
\]
Each \(D_j\) is Borel measurable: it is defined on
countably many Borel vertical strips by continuous
functions of \(y\). Separate continuity gives
\(D_j(x,y)\to D(x,y)\) at every point. Hence \(D\)
is jointly Borel, in particular Lebesgue measurable.

\paragraph{The precise external theorem.}
Theorem 5 of M. Laczkovich,
\emph{Functions with measurable differences},
Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 35 (1980), 217--235,
states that if \(f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)\) is jointly
Lebesgue measurable, then \(f=u+H\), with \(u\)
Lebesgue measurable and \(H\) additive.
We apply exactly this theorem. It makes no preliminary
measurability assumption on \(f\).
Its proof, which builds on the weak difference-property
theorem, is not assumed to have been reproduced here.
Every difference of \(u=f-H\) is continuous, because
\(\Delta_tu=\Delta_tf-H(t)\).

\paragraph{A measurable function with continuous differences is continuous.}
We prove the needed regularity assertion.
Let \(x_j\to0\) be any sequence, and set
\[
 c_j=u(x_j)-u(0),\qquad
 E_j(t)=u(t+x_j)-u(t)-c_j.
\]
For fixed \(t\),
\[
 E_j(t)=\Delta_tu(x_j)-\Delta_tu(0)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The measurable functions \(E_j\) therefore converge
to zero in measure on \([0,1]\).
Also translation continuity in measure gives
\[
 u(t+x_j)-u(t)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in measure on }[0,1].
\tag{1}
\]
It follows that the constant functions \(c_j\) tend to
zero in measure, and hence \(c_j\to0\) as real numbers.
Thus \(u\) is continuous at zero.
For every fixed \(a\),
\[
 u(a+x_j)-u(a)
 =u(x_j)-u(0)+\Delta_au(x_j)-\Delta_au(0)\longrightarrow0,
\]
so \(u\) is continuous everywhere. Taking \(g=u\)
proves the desired decomposition.

Here (1) requires only that \(u\) be finite and
Lebesgue measurable. One direct proof is to approximate
its restriction to \([-1,2]\), outside a set of arbitrarily
small measure, by a continuous function, using Lusin's
theorem and extension from a compact set.
For small \(x_j\), both \(t\) and \(t+x_j\) lie in that
interval. The exceptional sets have arbitrarily small
total measure, and the continuous approximation has
uniformly small translation differences.

\paragraph{The ambiguity is exactly linear.}
If \(f=g_1+H_1=g_2+H_2\) are two such decompositions,
then \(H_1-H_2=g_2-g_1\) is both continuous and additive.
Its value on rational \(q\) is \(q(H_1-H_2)(1)\);
density of the rationals gives the same formula
for all reals. Thus the only ambiguity is moving a
linear function between the two summands.
