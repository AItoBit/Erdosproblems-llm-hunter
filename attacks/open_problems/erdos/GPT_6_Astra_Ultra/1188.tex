\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1188 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
A distinct covering system consists of residue classes $a_i\pmod{n_i}$ with distinct integers $1<n_1<\cdots<n_k$, whose union covers $\mathbb Z$. It is minimal when deleting any nonempty collection of its classes destroys that covering property. Let $F(x)$ count these minimal systems whose moduli are at most $x$. Determine the growth of $F(x)$.

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

Systems include the chosen residue classes; this differs from counting only sets of moduli. Residues are understood modulo their moduli.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
For integral $X\ge2$, each modulus $m\in[2,X]$ is absent or has one of its $m$
residues selected. Ignoring coverage and minimality gives the explicit upper bound
\[F(X)\le\prod_{m=2}^X(m+1)=\frac{(X+1)!}{2}.\tag{1}\]
There is a finite exact minimality certificate. Work modulo the least common
multiple $Q$ of the chosen moduli. A covering is minimal if and only if every
class has a residue of $\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z$ covered by that class alone.
Necessity follows by deleting that class if it has no such witness; sufficiency
follows because deleting any class loses its witness.

For example the five classes
\[0\pmod2,\quad0\pmod3,\quad1\pmod4,\quad5\pmod6,\quad7\pmod{12}\]
cover all residues modulo 12. Private witnesses, in the listed order, are
$2,3,1,11,7$. This proves minimality directly. Translating all classes by each
of the twelve residues gives twelve different minimal systems, since the
modulus-12 residue distinguishes them. Thus $F(12)\ge12$ under the source's
convention of counting residue choices, without identifying translations.

This certificate tests minimality of a particular assignment. It must not be
confused with irreducibility of its moduli, which also rules out different
assignments to proper subsets and is addressed separately in problem 1189.

\subsection*{FINITE COMPUTATIONAL CHECK}
Direct enumeration of the twelve residue positions verifies coverage, each of the five private witnesses, and distinctness of the twelve translated systems.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Estimate the total number on a sharp scale as $X$ grows. The factorial
upper bound and the explicit twelve examples are elementary benchmarks and
do not recover the stronger growth bounds cited on the official page.
