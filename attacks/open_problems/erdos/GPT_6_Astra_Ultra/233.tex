\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#233: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
For $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$, prove $\sum_{n\le N}d_n^2\ll N(\log N)^2$.

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{233.tex}.
The source changes between prime-index and prime-size formulations and imports a mean-square upper bound. I isolate precisely the tail information needed by the conjecture; no improvement in the arithmetic exponent is claimed.
\subsection{2) WORK}
For $N\ge3$ put $u_n=d_n/\log N$ and $A_j=\#\{n\le N:u_n>2^j\}$ for $j\ge0$. Pointwise geometric summation gives
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}4^j\mathbf1_{u>2^j}\le\tfrac43u^2,
\qquad
u^2\le1+4\sum_{j\ge0}4^j\mathbf1_{u>2^j}\quad(u\ge0).
\]
For the second inequality, if $u>1$ let $j$ be the last threshold strictly below $u$; then $u\le2^{j+1}$, including powers of two by taking the preceding index. Hence
\[
\sum_{n\le N}d_n^2\ll N(\log N)^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{j\ge0}4^j A_j\ll N.
\]
This is an exact dyadic-tail reformulation up to absolute constants.

For example, the uniform estimates
\[
A_j\le {CN\over4^j(1+j)^{1+\epsilon}}\quad(j\ge0)
\]
would suffice. The superficially similar bound $A_j\le CN/4^j$ does not suffice through this argument, since each occupied scale contributes order $N$. Thus even the correct quadratic tail exponent must be accompanied by summability across scales. This locates a separate issue from merely bounding the largest prime gap.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The sums over $j$ are finite at every fixed $N$, and strict threshold conventions handle equality correctly. The sufficient tail bound is not proved for prime gaps. A nonsummable majorant is not a counterexample to the conjecture.
