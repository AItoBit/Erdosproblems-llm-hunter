\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#302: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 23 September 2026. Source versions read in full: \texttt{302.tex} in \texttt{gpt\_pro\_5.4}.

\subsection*{1) OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE AUDIT}
Let $f(N)$ maximize the size of a subset of $[1,N]$ containing no three
distinct denominators with $1/a=1/b+1/c$. The source supplies density
$5/8$ and optimizes a single-residue low block plus a high interval.
I extend its obstruction to several residue classes simultaneously and
show why this broader version still cannot beat $5/8$.

\subsection*{2) WORK: A MULTI-RESIDUE BARRIER}
Let $R$ be a set of units modulo $m\ge2$ such that
\[
 \gcd(r-s,m)>1\quad\hbox{for all }r,s\in R. \tag{1}
\]
Take
\[
 A=\{n\le N/4:n\bmod m\in R\}\ \cup\ [\lceil N/2\rceil,N].
\]
This is solution-free. In any proposed solution the smallest denominator
is $a=bc/(b+c)<N/2$, so it lies in the low block. If $b$ also lies there,
the equation becomes $(b-a)c=ab$. Since $a,b$ are units modulo $m$, this
congruence has a solution in $c$ only if $\gcd(b-a,m)=1$, contrary to
(1). The same argument excludes $c$ from the low block. If both $b,c$
are high, then $1/a=1/b+1/c<4/N$, hence $a>N/4$, another contradiction.
Consequently
\[
 |A|=\left(\frac12+\frac{|R|}{4m}\right)N+O_m(1).
\]

But $R$ and $R+1$ are disjoint in $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$: an intersection
would give two members differing by one, whose difference is coprime to
$m$. Thus $2|R|\le m$, and this entire compatible multi-residue template
has density at most $5/8$. The parity choice $m=2,R=\{1\}$ attains it.

\subsection*{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
Condition (1) is sufficient to forbid a low--low pair for every third
residue. It is not necessary for every possible interval construction.
Accordingly the upper bound just proved is a bound on this template,
not on $f(N)$. It does not rule out interdependent choices of several
scale intervals or more selective choices of the third denominator.
No assertion that the optimal asymptotic constant equals $5/8$ is made.
