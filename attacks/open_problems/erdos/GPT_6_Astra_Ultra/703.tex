\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 703: a forbidden intersection size}

\paragraph{Statement and claim.}
Let \(T(n,r)\) be the largest size of a family
\(\mathcal F\subseteq2^{[n]}\) for which \(|A\cap B|\ne r\).
We give the known exact answer for fixed \(r\geq2\) and
sufficiently large \(n\), and the exponential upper bound
when \(r\) is bounded away from \(0\) and \(n/2\).
The upper bounds are established external theorems of
Frankl--F\"uredi and Frankl--R\"odl. We reconstruct the matching
families, check parity and quantifiers, and derive their
asymptotic consequences. No new solution is claimed.
See \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/703}.

\paragraph{The external upper-bound inputs.}
For every fixed \(r\), Frankl--F\"uredi determine the maximum
for all \(n\geq n_0(r)\). Their theorem is stated, together
with the extremal families, on page 261 of
P. Frankl and V. R\"odl, \emph{Forbidden intersections},
Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 300 (1987), 259--286:
\url{https://users.renyi.hu/~pfrankl/1987-3.pdf}.
Theorem 1.1 of that same primary paper gives: for every
\(0<\varepsilon<1/4\) there exists \(\delta>0\), depending
only on \(\varepsilon\), such that
\[
 \varepsilon n<r<(1/2-\varepsilon)n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T(n,r)<(2-\delta)^n.                                          \tag{1}
\]
These structural upper bounds are used as external inputs,
not reproved below. The fixed-\(r\) threshold \(n_0(r)\) does
not itself imply the uniform linear-range statement (1).
For \(\varepsilon\geq1/4\) the requested interval is empty.

\paragraph{The exact fixed-\(r\) answer and its constructions.}
For \(n\geq n_0(r)\), the cited fixed-\(r\) theorem gives
\[
 T(n,r)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\binom nj+
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\sum_{j=(n+r+1)/2}^{n}\binom nj,&n+r\text{ odd},\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle2\sum_{j=(n+r)/2}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}j,&n+r\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{2}
\]
Here is a direct verification of the matching lower bounds.
Include every set of size less than \(r\).
Such a set has intersection less than \(r\) with every set,
so these members cause no obstruction.
If \(n+r\) is odd, also include every set of size at least
\((n+r+1)/2\). Any two of these have intersection at least
\[
 2\cdot\frac{n+r+1}{2}-n=r+1.
\]
This proves the first case in (2).

If \(n+r\) is even, fix a point \(x\) and also include every
set \(A\) with \(|A\setminus\{x\}|\geq(n+r)/2\).
Two such sets have intersection outside \(x\) of size at least
\[
 2\cdot\frac{n+r}{2}-(n-1)=r+1.
\]
Their number is twice the indicated upper binomial tail
on \(n-1\) points, because membership of \(x\) is free.
For sufficiently large \(n\), these upper families are
disjoint from the sets of size less than \(r\), which
justifies adding the two counts.

The published extremal theorems constrain distinct members.
If the displayed problem is read as also allowing \(A=B\),
its upper bound still follows by restriction, and the
constructions above still work: none of their sets has
size \(r\). Thus (2) is valid under either convention
in this fixed-\(r\), large-\(n\) range.

\paragraph{Consequences of the formula.}
For fixed \(r\), the lower tail in (2) is \(O_r(n^{r-1})\).
By symmetry, each upper tail is half the full binomial sum,
apart from a bounded number of central terms. Each such term
is \(O(2^n/\sqrt n)\), by the central binomial estimate.
Therefore
\[
 T(n,r)=\left(\frac12+O_r(n^{-1/2})\right)2^n
 \qquad(r\text{ fixed},\ n\longrightarrow\infty).               \tag{3}
\]
This does not conflict with (1), where \(r\) grows linearly
with \(n\).

\paragraph{A quantitative lower bound in the linear range.}
For any \(r\), the union of all sets of size less than \(r\)
and all sets of size greater than \((n+r)/2\) avoids
intersection \(r\), by the same two intersection estimates.
For fixed \(0<\rho<1/2\) and \(r=\lfloor\rho n\rfloor\),
Stirling's formula applied to single layers of this family gives
\[
 \log_2 T(n,r)\geq
 n\max\left\{H_2(\rho),H_2\left(\frac{1+\rho}{2}\right)\right\}
 +o(n),
\]
where \(H_2(t)=-t\log_2t-(1-t)\log_2(1-t)\).
The upper bound (1), with any
\(\varepsilon<\min(\rho,1/2-\rho)\), gives
\(\log_2 T(n,r)<n\log_2(2-\delta)\).
These bounds establish exponential behavior strictly below
\(2^n\) throughout the requested interior range.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The explicit exponential question is answered completely
relative to Frankl--R\"odl's theorem. Formula (2) also supplies
the exact fixed-\(r\) answer, relative to Frankl--F\"uredi's
theorem. An exact value for every pair \(n,r\) is not claimed.
