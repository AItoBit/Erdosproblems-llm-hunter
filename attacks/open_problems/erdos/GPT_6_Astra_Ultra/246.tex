\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 246: completeness of two coprime multiplicative generators}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
Let $a,b>1$ be coprime integers. Every sufficiently large integer
is a sum of distinct elements of
$G=\{a^u b^v:u,v\geq0\}$. We reconstruct Birch's proof through
bounded gaps, disjoint signed identities, and residue adjustment.
The intended nontrivial-base assumption matters: if one base is
one, the set consists of powers of the other base and is complete
only when that other base is two. For a base $c\geq3$, numbers
$2c^m$ cannot be sums of distinct powers of $c$.

\subsection{2) Bounded gaps between subset sums}
The ratio $\log b/\log a$ is irrational, since a rational ratio
would give $a^r=b^s$ for some positive $r,s$, contrary to
coprimality. Density of an irrational rotation implies that,
for every sufficiently large real $X$, some $g\in G$ satisfies
\[
 \frac23X<g\leq X.
\]
For completeness, take finitely many nonnegative integers $v$
whose residues $v\log b$ modulo $\log a$ meet every circular
interval of length $\log(3/2)$. For large $\log X$, one can
choose such a $v$ and a nonnegative integer $u$ with
$\log X-\log(3/2)<u\log a+v\log b\leq\log X$.

Choose an integer $H$ past this threshold. Given any integer
$N>H$, subtract such a $g$ and repeat on the remainder until
the remainder $y$ lies in $[0,H]$. Each remainder is less than
one third of its predecessor, so the selected terms are strictly
decreasing and distinct. If $N\leq H$, select no terms.
We have proved that for every integer $N\geq0$,
\[
 N-y=\Phi(E),\qquad 0\leq y\leq H,\quad
 E\subseteq\mathbb N_0^2\text{ finite},
 \qquad \Phi(E)=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}a^u b^v.
\]

\subsection{3) Many disjoint signed identities for one}
We also use $\Phi$ on finite subsets of $\mathbb Z^2$, where
its values are positive rationals. Fix positive integers $L,M$.
The grid
$\{0,L,\ldots,rL\}\times\{0,M,\ldots,rM\}$
has $2^{(r+1)^2}$ subsets, but its subset sums are integers
between zero and $(r+1)^2a^{rL}b^{rM}$.
For large $r$, two distinct subsets have the same sum.
Cancel their intersection to obtain disjoint nonempty sets
$F,G$ with $\Phi(F)=\Phi(G)$.

Choose $w\in F\cup G$ with maximal coordinate sum, and interchange
$F,G$ if necessary so that $w\in G$. Translating all exponent
pairs by $-w$ gives
\[
 1=\Phi(U)-\Phi(V),\qquad
 U=F-w,\quad V=(G\setminus\{w\})-w.
\]
The sets $U,V$ are disjoint, avoid $(0,0)$, lie in
$L\mathbb Z\times M\mathbb Z$, and all their points have
coordinate sum at most zero. They are therefore disjoint
from the nonnegative quadrant.

Repeat this construction $H$ times. At each stage choose $L,M$
larger than the absolute values of all coordinates previously
used. Every new nonzero grid point then differs from all
previous points. We obtain identities
\[
 1=\Phi(U_i)-\Phi(V_i)\quad(1\leq i\leq H)
\]
whose $2H$ supports are pairwise disjoint and all avoid
$\mathbb N_0^2$.

\subsection{4) A representable arithmetic progression}
Put $r_0=\sum_{i=1}^H\Phi(V_i)$. If $N-y=\Phi(E)$ as above,
then
\[
 N+r_0=\Phi(E)+\sum_{i\leq y}\Phi(U_i)
                       +\sum_{i>y}\Phi(V_i).
\]
All supports on the right are disjoint. Choose integers $A,B\geq0$
so large that translation by $(A,B)$ puts every $U_i,V_i$
inside the nonnegative quadrant. Multiplying by $D=a^A b^B$
shows that every integer
\[
 R+DN,\qquad N\geq0,\qquad R=Dr_0,
\]
is a sum of distinct members of $G$. The number $R$ is a
nonnegative integer. Distinct exponent pairs give distinct
values because $a,b$ are coprime.

\subsection{5) Filling every residue class}
Multiply these representations by $ab$. Their terms now have
both exponents positive. Write
\[
 M_0=abD=a^{A+1}b^{B+1},\quad
 P=a^{(A+1)\phi(b^{B+1})},\quad
 Q=b^{(B+1)\phi(a^{A+1})}.
\]
Euler's theorem gives
\[
 P\equiv(0,1),\qquad Q\equiv(1,0)
       \pmod{(a^{A+1},b^{B+1})}.
\]
The same congruences hold for every positive power of $P$ or $Q$.
Thus, by choosing between zero and $b^{B+1}-1$ distinct positive
powers of $P$, and between zero and $a^{A+1}-1$ distinct positive
powers of $Q$, we obtain a fixed finite sum $E_c$ for every
residue class $c$ modulo $M_0$.
All these terms are on the two coordinate axes; they are
distinct from each other and from the terms in the representations
of $abR+M_0N$.

For a sufficiently large integer $n$, choose
$c\equiv n-abR\pmod{M_0}$. Then
$n-E_c-abR$ is a nonnegative multiple of $M_0$.
Combining its interior representation with the axis terms of
$E_c$ represents $n$ as required. There are only finitely many
$E_c$, so one common lower threshold works for every residue.
