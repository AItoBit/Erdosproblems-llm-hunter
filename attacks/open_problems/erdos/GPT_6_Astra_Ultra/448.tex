\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 448}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Let \(\tau^+(n)\) count the occupied dyadic divisor bins
\([2^k,2^{k+1})\), and let \(\tau(n)\) count all divisors.
Must \(\tau^+(n)<\epsilon\tau(n)\) hold for almost all \(n\),
for every \(\epsilon>0\)?
No. We deduce the negative answer from the quantitative
Erd\H{o}s--Tenenbaum theorem, and derive the companion mean-order
estimate from Ford's interval-divisor bound. The two analytic
theorems are explicit external inputs.

\paragraph{The external small-ratio estimate and its quantifiers.}
Theorem 1 of Erd\H{o}s and Tenenbaum (1981) states that, for every
\(\eta>0\), some constant \(C_\eta\) bounds the upper natural density
of
\[
 \{n:\tau^+(n)<a\tau(n)\}
\]
by \(C_\eta a^{1-\eta}\), for \(0<a<1\).
If a half-open endpoint convention different from the one above is
used, the same conclusion holds after changing the constant: each
bin in one dyadic partition meets at most two bins in the other,
so their occupied-bin counts differ by at most a factor of two.

Take \(\eta=1/2\), and then choose a fixed \(a>0\) small enough that
\(C_{1/2}\sqrt a<1/2\). The complementary set
\[
 \{n:\tau^+(n)\geq a\tau(n)\}
 \tag{1}
\]
has lower natural density at least \(1/2\).
Thus the proposed assertion fails for the single fixed choice
\(\epsilon=a\). This checks the necessary positive-density conclusion;
isolated examples such as primes would not suffice.

More generally, if a set \(\mathcal A\) satisfies
\(\tau^+(n)/\tau(n)\to0\) as \(n\to\infty\) within \(\mathcal A\),
then, for every \(a>0\), its tail is contained in the preceding
small-ratio set. Hence its upper density is at most \(C_{1/2}\sqrt a\).
Letting \(a\downarrow0\) proves that \(\mathcal A\) has density zero.

\paragraph{The companion average: the required external interval estimate.}
Write \(H(x,y,2y)\) for the number of integers at most \(x\)
having a divisor in \((y,2y]\).
Ford's Corollary 2 gives, uniformly for \(1\leq y\leq x/2\),
\[
 H(x,y,2y)\asymp
 \frac{x}{(\log Y)^\alpha(\log\log Y)^{3/2}},
 \qquad Y=\min(y,x/y)+3,
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(\alpha=1-(1+\log\log2)/\log2\), so \(0<\alpha<1\).
The additive three makes the denominator harmless at small \(y\).

Let \(\widetilde\tau^+(n)\) count occupied bins \((2^k,2^{k+1}]\),
allowing \(k=-1\) to include the divisor \(1\).
Finite double counting gives
\[
 \sum_{n\leq x}\widetilde\tau^+(n)
 =\sum_{k\geq-1}H(x,2^k,2^{k+1}).
 \tag{3}
\]
The two endpoint bins contribute \(O(x)\).
Put \(L=\log_2 x\). For \(L/4\leq k\leq3L/4\), (2)
has \(\log Y\asymp\log x\) and
\(\log\log Y\asymp\log\log x\).
There are \(\asymp\log x\) such terms, giving the lower bound
\[
 \sum_{n\leq x}\widetilde\tau^+(n)
 \gg \frac{x(\log x)^{1-\alpha}}{(\log\log x)^{3/2}}.
\]
For the upper bound, (2) reduces the sum to a constant multiple of
\[
 x\sum_{0\leq j\leq L}
 \frac1{(j+1)^\alpha(\log(j+2))^{3/2}}.
\]
The part \(j\leq\sqrt L\) is \(O(L^{(1-\alpha)/2})\).
For larger \(j\), \(\log(j+2)\asymp\log L\) and
\(\sum_{j\leq L}(j+1)^{-\alpha}=O(L^{1-\alpha})\).
This proves the matching upper bound.

Changing the endpoint assignment can only move divisors which are
powers of two. Thus
\[
 |\tau^+(n)-\widetilde\tau^+(n)|\leq v_2(n)+1,\qquad
 \sum_{n\leq x}(v_2(n)+1)\leq2x.
\]
This error is smaller than the established main order, proving
\[
 \sum_{n\leq x}\tau^+(n)\asymp
 \frac{x(\log x)^{1-\alpha}}{(\log\log x)^{3/2}}.
 \tag{4}
\]
