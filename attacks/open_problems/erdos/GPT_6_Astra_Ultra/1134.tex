\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1134: a collision gives density zero}

\paragraph{Statement and scope.}
Let \(A\) be the least set of positive integers containing \(1\) and closed under \(x\mapsto2x+1,3x+1,6x+1\). Does \(A\) have positive lower density? No: we prove the quantitative bound \(A(X)=O(X^{99/100})\). This is a reconstruction of the known negative resolution, not a claim to improve the published exponent. See \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/1134}.

\paragraph{A shortening identity.}
Write a word in the alphabet \(\{2,3,6\}\) in the order in which its maps \(F_m(x)=mx+1\) are applied. Then
\[
 F_2(F_2(F_3(x)))=12x+7=F_6(F_2(x)).
\]
Consequently the consecutive subword \(322\) may be replaced by \(26\) without changing the represented integer or the product of its letters. Every value in \(A\) therefore has a representing word with no occurrence of \(322\): repeated replacements terminate because they shorten the word. The empty word represents \(1\).

\paragraph{A convergent word sum.}
Put \(\sigma=99/100\) and \(S=2^{-\sigma}+3^{-\sigma}+6^{-\sigma}\). A word avoiding \(322\) avoids it in particular in each of its disjoint blocks of three letters. If \(P(w)\) is the product of the letters, this gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{w:\,322\text{ absent}}}P(w)^{-\sigma}
 \leq (1+S+S^2)\sum_{j\geq0}(S^3-12^{-\sigma})^j.
\]
The right side is finite. Indeed
\[
 S\leq6^{1/100}(1/2+1/3+1/6)=6^{1/100},
 \qquad 6^{3/100}<13/12,\qquad 12^{-\sigma}>1/12.
\]
The middle strict inequality is equivalently the exact integer inequality
\(216\cdot12^{100}<13^{100}\). Thus \(S^3-12^{-\sigma}<1\).

\paragraph{Counting distinct integers.}
Starting from \(1\), each operation increases the value at least by its multiplicative factor, so the value represented by \(w\) is at least \(P(w)\).
Choose one shortened word for each member of \(A\cap[1,X]\). These words are distinct and satisfy \(P(w)\leq X\). Therefore, writing \(C\) for the finite word sum above,
\[
 A(X)\leq\sum_{\substack{w:\,322\text{ absent}\\P(w)\leq X}}1
 \leq X^\sigma\sum_{w:\,322\text{ absent}}P(w)^{-\sigma}
 \leq CX^\sigma.
\]
In particular the natural density exists and is zero.

\paragraph{Attribution and dependencies.}
The problem page reports the Crampin--Hilton resolution and a sharper exponent, also discussed in Lagarias's account. This proof uses the same basic phenomenon of coincident affine words, with an elementary block estimate; it needs no external counting theorem.

\paragraph{Completion estimate.}
The requested positive-density assertion is completely disproved.
