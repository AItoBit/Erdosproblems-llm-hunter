\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 682: rough integers in prime gaps}

\paragraph{Statement and claim.}
Let \(p_n<p_{n+1}\) be consecutive primes. For almost all indices
\(n\), there is an integer \(m\) strictly between them whose least
prime factor is at least \(p_{n+1}-p_n\).
The main analytic input is the established theorem of Gafni and Tao,
\emph{Rough numbers between consecutive primes}, Theorem 1.1:
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06463}.
Below we explain its precise counting parameter and derive the
assertion, together with an elementary obstruction for other gaps.
See \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/682}.

\paragraph{External theorem and change of parameter.}
The cited theorem bounds the number of exceptional gaps with
\emph{prime value} \(p_n\in[X,2X]\) by
\[
 O\left(\frac X{(\log X)^2}\right).                             \tag{1}
\]
This is an external analytic theorem; its sieve argument is not
reproved here. It is important that \(X\) in (1) is not an index.
Let \(E(Y)\) count all exceptional gaps starting at a prime at
most \(Y\). Split \((\sqrt Y,Y]\) into dyadic intervals. On these
intervals the logarithms are at least a constant times \(\log Y\),
and their lengths sum to at most \(Y\). The remaining gaps
starting below \(\sqrt Y\) number at most \(\sqrt Y\). Hence
\[
 E(Y)\ll \frac Y{(\log Y)^2}+\sqrt Y
       \ll\frac Y{(\log Y)^2}.                                  \tag{2}
\]
By the prime number theorem, \(p_N\sim N\log N\) and
\(\log p_N\sim\log N\). Consequently the number of exceptional
indices \(n\leq N\) is
\[
 E(p_N)\ll \frac N{\log N}=o(N).                                \tag{3}
\]
This proves the required almost-all conclusion.
An \(O(N/(\log N)^2)\) bound with \(N\) an index does not follow
from (1), and is not claimed in this reconstruction.

\paragraph{An explicit local obstruction.}
Suppose \(p>3\) and both \(p,p+4\) are prime.
Necessarily \(p\equiv1\pmod6\): the other possible odd prime
residue, \(5\pmod6\), would make \(p+4\) divisible by \(3\).
The three integers inside this gap then satisfy
\[
 2\mid p+1,\qquad 3\mid p+2,\qquad 2\mid p+3.
\]
Thus \(p,p+4\) are consecutive primes, and every interior
integer has least prime factor strictly smaller than the
gap length \(4\). Every such cousin-prime gap is exceptional;
for example the gap from \(7\) to \(11\) is exceptional.
This does not prove that infinitely many exceptions exist,
because infinitude of these prime pairs is an additional
unproved input and is not being assumed.
By contrast, every twin-prime gap has its even midpoint as
a witness with least prime factor \(2\), exactly its gap length.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The density-one assertion is fully proved relative to the cited
Gafni--Tao theorem and the prime number theorem. The parameter
conversion and the local gap obstruction are established above.
No assertion about the infinitude of exceptional gaps is needed.
