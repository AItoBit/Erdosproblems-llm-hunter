\section{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
\textbf{Erd\H{o}s \#300; the sharp lower construction and a density-threshold deduction, 2026-09-24.}
Let $A(N)$ be the largest size of a set $E\subseteq[1,N]$ containing no subset $S$ with $\sum_{n\in S}1/n=1$. Estimate $A(N)$.

\section{QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK}
Liu and Sawhney prove $A(N)=(1-1/e+o(1))N$. This disproves the earlier expectation that $A(N)\sim N$. Below we prove a finite lower bound by a terminal interval and deduce the matching upper asymptotic from their precise density-threshold theorem, explicitly imported as the difficult input.

\section{OBJECTIVE AND ATTACK PLAN}
An interval whose entire reciprocal sum is below one automatically avoids a unit-sum subset. The harmonic integral identifies its optimal terminal-interval scale. The external theorem shows that no arbitrary set can asymptotically do better.

\section{WORK}
Set $m=\lceil N/e\rceil$ and take
\[
E_N=\{m+1,m+2,\ldots,N\}.
\]
If this interval is nonempty, strict decrease of $1/x$ gives
\[
\sum_{n=m+1}^N\frac1n
<\int_m^N\frac{dx}{x}
=\log(N/m)\leq1.
\tag{1}
\]
The strict inequality follows term by term from $1/n<\int_{n-1}^n dx/x$. If the interval is empty its reciprocal sum is zero. Every subset has reciprocal sum at most that of $E_N$, so no subset sums to one. Consequently, for every $N\geq1$,
\[
A(N)\geq N-\lceil N/e\rceil,
\qquad
\liminf_{N\to\infty}A(N)/N\geq1-1/e.
\tag{2}
\]

The same constant can be anticipated from a general comparison. Among all $h$-element subsets of $[1,N]$, the terminal interval $\{N-h+1,\ldots,N\}$ has the least reciprocal sum. Therefore a set with $h=(\alpha+o(1))N$ elements has reciprocal sum at least $-\log(1-\alpha)+o(1)$ for $0<\alpha<1$. This exceeds one when $\alpha>1-1/e$. A total sum exceeding one does not by itself imply an exact unit-sum subset; the next input is what closes that logical gap.

\textbf{Declared external theorem.} Liu and Sawhney's Theorem 1.3 states: for every fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$, all sufficiently large $N$, and every $E\subseteq[1,N]$ with
\[
|E|\geq(1-1/e+\varepsilon)N,
\]
there is a subset $S\subseteq E$ whose reciprocal sum equals $1$. Its Fourier-analytic proof and denominator-clearing construction are external dependencies, not derived from the harmonic comparison above.

Fix any such $\varepsilon$. An extremal avoiding set for $A(N)$ cannot reach that threshold once $N$ is sufficiently large, so
\[
A(N)<(1-1/e+\varepsilon)N.
\]
Taking the limit superior and then letting the fixed positive $\varepsilon$ decrease to zero gives
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}A(N)/N\leq1-1/e.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (2) and (3) proves
\[
A(N)=(1-1/e+o(1))N.
\]

\section{VERIFICATION AND SCOPE}
The lower construction has reciprocal sum strictly below one, even when an integral bound reaches one exactly. The upper deduction uses a theorem for arbitrary subsets, not only intervals. Its quantifier is ``each fixed $\varepsilon$ and sufficiently large $N$'', which is exactly what the limit argument requires. The complete asymptotic deduction is provided; the deep upper-bound theorem is explicitly imported. No new result is claimed.
