\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1152 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
For each $n\geq1$, choose $n$ distinct interpolation nodes $x_{1n},\ldots,x_{nn}$ in $[-1,1]$, and let $\epsilon(n)\to0$. Must there be a continuous $f:[-1,1]\to\mathbb R$ such that every polynomial sequence satisfying
\[
\deg p_n<(1+\epsilon(n))n,\qquad p_n(x_{kn})=f(x_{kn})\quad(1\leq k\leq n)
\]
fails to converge to $f(x)$ for almost every $x\in[-1,1]$?

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
There is an exact algebraic description of the extra freedom. Set
$w_n(x)=\prod_{j=1}^n(x-x_{jn})$ and let $L_nf$ be the degree-at-most-$n-1$
interpolant. Every interpolating polynomial has the unique form
\[p_n=L_nf+w_nq_n.\tag{1}\]
Indeed its difference from $L_nf$ vanishes at $n$ different points and is therefore
divisible by $w_n$; polynomial division gives uniqueness.
For $q_n\ne0$, the two summands in (1) have different degree ranges, so there is
no leading-term cancellation and
\[\deg p_n=n+\deg q_n<(1+\epsilon(n))n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \deg q_n<\epsilon(n)n.\tag{2}\]
For nonnegative $\epsilon(n)$ this leaves $\lceil\epsilon(n)n\rceil=o(n)$ free
coefficients, with zero free coefficients when $\epsilon(n)=0$.
If a negative degree allowance excludes $L_nf$ itself, existence of admissible
interpolants must first be checked; a vacuous universal statement proves no
interpolation phenomenon.

The desired result therefore requires one $f$ whose interpolation errors cannot
be repaired almost everywhere by any of the low-degree multipliers in (1).
Simply showing divergence of the unique degree-$n-1$ interpolants only handles
$q_n=0$, and does not address the quantifier over all correction sequences.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Construct one continuous function defeating every permitted multiplier
sequence. The dimension count $o(n)$ is not a measure-theoretic obstruction by
itself and supplies no almost-everywhere divergence conclusion.
