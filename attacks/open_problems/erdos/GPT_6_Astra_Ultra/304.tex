\subsection{2) WORK}
For reduced $u/v>0$, a representation $u/v=1/x+1/y$ with $2\le x<y$ corresponds exactly to divisors
\[
d\mid v^2,\quad 0<d<v,\quad u\mid v+d,\quad x=(v+d)/u\ge2,
\]
with $y=(v+v^2/d)/u$. Indeed $(ux-v)(uy-v)=v^2$, and $x<y$ gives $d<v$. Conversely the congruence on $d$ implies that on $v^2/d$ because $(u,d)=1$, and $d<v$ gives strict ordering. The source omitted this ordering restriction in its displayed construction.

For a general search with residual fraction $r>0$ and $k>1$ remaining terms whose denominators begin at $s$, every first denominator satisfies
\[
\max\{s,\lfloor1/r\rfloor+1\}\le x\le\lfloor k/r\rfloor.
\]
The lower inequality leaves a strictly positive residual; the upper follows from $r\le k/x$. Recursing with exact fractions and increasing denominators is therefore complete. For $k=1$, a representation exists precisely when $1/r$ is an integer at least $s$.

The saved implementation verifies $N(78,79)=5$ and $N(77,79)=6$, finding respectively
\[
(2,3,7,90,24885),\qquad(2,3,8,62,4524,7386184)
\]
as denominator lists, and exhaustively rejecting all shorter lengths. Thus the largest numerator need not be hardest even at one fixed denominator. No claim that 77 maximizes over all numerators is needed for this counterexample to the boundary-only strategy.
