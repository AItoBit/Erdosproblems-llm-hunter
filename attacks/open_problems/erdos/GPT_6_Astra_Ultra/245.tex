\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 245: doubling of an infinite set of density zero}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
For an infinite set $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ with $A(N)=o(N)$,
must
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{(A+A)(N)}{A(N)}\geq3?
\]
Here $S(N)=|S\cap[1,N]|$. Freiman proved the affirmative answer.
This attempt establishes the sharpness of the constant by a
fully specified example with limsup equal to three. The universal
lower bound is unresolved in this attempt.

\subsection{2) The block construction and zero density}
For $j\geq1$, put
\[
 x_j=2^{4^j},\qquad \ell_j=\sqrt{x_j},\qquad
 I_j=[x_j,x_j+\ell_j]\cap\mathbb Z,\qquad A=\bigcup_{j\geq1}I_j.
\]
Let $B_j=\bigcup_{i<j}I_i$, $b_j=|B_j|$, and
$M_j=\max B_j$ for $j\geq2$. Since
$x_j=x_{j-1}^4$ and $\ell_j=x_{j-1}^2$,
\[
 b_j=O(\sqrt{x_{j-1}}),\qquad
 M_j=O(x_{j-1}),\qquad
 b_j^2+M_j=o(\ell_j),\qquad b_j=o(\ell_j).
\]
For $x_j\leq N<x_{j+1}$ we have
$A(N)\leq b_j+\ell_j+1=O(\sqrt{x_j})$ and $N\geq x_j$.
Therefore $A(N)/N\to0$.

\subsection{3) Bounding the limsup from above}
First let $x_j+\ell_j\leq N<x_{j+1}$.
Only the first $j$ blocks can contribute to $(A+A)\cap[1,N]$.
Old--old sums have at most $b_j^2$ possible values.
New--old sums lie in an interval of length at most
$\ell_j+M_j+1$. New--new sums lie in
$[2x_j,2x_j+2\ell_j]$, of size $2\ell_j+1$.
Consequently
\[
 (A+A)(N)\leq 3\ell_j+b_j^2+M_j+2
             =3\ell_j+o(\ell_j),
 \qquad A(N)=b_j+\ell_j+1.
\]
The ratio is at most $3+o(1)$ throughout this range.

Next let $N=x_j+t$, where $0\leq t\leq\ell_j$.
No new--new sum is at most $N$. All old--old sums are already
below $x_j$, while the new--old sums at most $N$ lie in an
interval of size at most $t+1$. At $x_j-1$, the preceding
completed-block bound gives
$|B_j+B_j|\leq(3+\varepsilon_j)b_j$, with $\varepsilon_j\to0$.
Thus
\[
 \frac{(A+A)(x_j+t)}{A(x_j+t)}
 \leq\frac{(3+\varepsilon_j)b_j+t+1}{b_j+t+1}
 \leq3+|\varepsilon_j|.
\]
This also covers the partially filled blocks, so the global
limsup is at most three.

\subsection{4) Attaining three}
Fix one $a_*\in I_1$. For large $j$, at
$N_j=2x_j+2\ell_j<x_{j+1}$, both intervals
\[
 a_*+I_j,\qquad I_j+I_j=[2x_j,2x_j+2\ell_j]\cap\mathbb Z
\]
are present and disjoint. Their total size is $3\ell_j+2$.
Since $A(N_j)=b_j+\ell_j+1=(1+o(1))\ell_j$,
the ratio has limsup at least three, and hence exactly three.

For comparison, a much weaker universal bound follows from
$a_*+A\subseteq A+A$:
$(A+A)(N)\geq A(N-a_*)\geq A(N)-a_*$, so the liminf is at
least one. This elementary observation does not establish
Freiman's lower bound of three.

\subsection{5) Attribution and remaining work}

Freiman's 1973 resolution. The separated-block construction is
an exposition of the sharpness mechanism, not a claimed new
resolution. What is missing is a proof that every infinite
zero-density set has limsup at least three.
