\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 76: packing monochromatic triangles}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
Must every red--blue coloring of $K_n$ have a family of edge-disjoint
monochromatic triangles of size $(1+o(1))n^2/12$?
Gruslys and Letzter proved the affirmative answer. This attempt gives
a self-contained quadratic lower bound with a smaller leading
constant and proves that the requested constant $1/12$ is optimal.
It does not reproduce their sharp packing theorem.

\subsection{2) A maximal packing leaves a $K_6$-free graph}
Take a maximal family of edge-disjoint monochromatic triangles,
and let its size be $t$. Remove their $3t$ edges, retaining the
original colors on the remaining graph $H$. There is no monochromatic
triangle in $H$, by maximality.

Every red--blue $K_6$ contains a monochromatic triangle. Indeed,
some vertex has three incident edges of one color, say red, to
$x,y,z$. If any edge within $\{x,y,z\}$ is red, it completes a red
triangle with that vertex; otherwise those three edges are all blue.
Consequently the uncolored graph $H$ contains no $K_6$.

Here is an elementary density estimate sufficient for our purpose.
In a $K_6$-free graph on $v$ vertices, choose a maximum clique of
size $s\leq5$. Every vertex outside it has at most $s-1$ neighbors
in it. Hence the sum of degrees of clique vertices is at most
$(s-1)v$, and one of them has degree at most
$(s-1)v/s\leq4v/5$. This includes $s=1$, when the graph has no
edges. Repeated vertex deletion, applied to the remaining induced
graphs, now gives
\[
 e(H)\leq\frac45\sum_{v=1}^n v=\frac25 n(n+1).
\]
Since $e(H)=\binom n2-3t$, we conclude
\[
 t\geq\frac{n^2}{30}-\frac{3n}{10}.
\]
For small $n$ a negative right side is simply a vacuous lower bound.
This argument uses maximality, not an unproved claim that a greedy
packing is maximum.

\subsection{3) A sharp obstruction to improving the target constant}
For even $n=2m$, split the vertices into two sets of size $m$.
Color edges within each part blue and edges between the parts red.
There are no red triangles, and every blue triangle lies inside a
part. Thus any permitted packing has at most
\[
 \frac{2\binom m2}{3}
 =\frac{n^2}{12}-\frac n6
\]
triangles. In particular no universal leading constant greater than
$1/12$ is possible.

For the infinite sequence $m=3^r$, this upper bound is achieved
exactly. Identify each part with the vector space $\mathbb F_3^r$.
An affine line has three points. Each pair of distinct points lies
on exactly one such line, since its direction is their nonzero
difference and the only nonzero scalars in $\mathbb F_3$ are
$1,-1$. The triangles formed by all affine lines therefore partition
the edges of each complete graph on a part. Using them in both
parts gives exactly the displayed number of triangles.

\subsection{4) Gap and sources}
\textbf{Outcome of this attempt: UNRESOLVED.} The lower constant
proved here is $1/30$, whereas the problem requires $1/12$.
The upper example establishes optimality but does not improve the
universal lower bound. The needed additional packing structure is
the substantive missing part of this reconstruction. The batch check
enumerates all colorings on six vertices for the Ramsey step and
verifies the affine-line decompositions for small dimensions.
