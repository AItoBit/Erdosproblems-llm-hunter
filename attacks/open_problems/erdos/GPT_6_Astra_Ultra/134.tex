\section{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
\textbf{Erd\H{o}s \#134; reconstruction, 2026-09-23.}
Given $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, is it true that, for all sufficiently large
$n$, every triangle-free $n$-vertex graph with maximum degree below
$n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ can be extended on the same vertex set to a
triangle-free graph of diameter $2$ by adding at most $\delta n^2$ edges?

\section{QUICK LITERATURE AND CONTEXT CHECK}
The current page records a positive answer. Alon's primary note proves
it by a bounded-degree random extension followed by maximal completion.
We reconstruct that proof with $\eta=\min(\varepsilon,1/12)$, sufficient
for the question, and supply the relevant probability comparison.
No new bound or novelty is claimed.

\section{WORK}
Set $c=n^{-\eta}$, $D=\lceil2c\sqrt n\rceil$, and
$m=\lfloor c^2 n^{3/2}\rfloor$. Starting from the given graph, make $m$
random additions. At each step choose uniformly from the pairs of
nonadjacent vertices that both have degree below $D$ and have no common
neighbor. Adding such an edge preserves triangle-freeness and the degree
bound $D$.

At any stage at most $2cn$ vertices have reached degree $D$: each such
vertex has acquired at least $c\sqrt n$ new incidences, while there
are at most $2m$ new incidences in total. The number of unordered pairs
having a common neighbor is at most
\[
 \sum_v\binom{d(v)}2\le nD^2/2=(2+o(1))c^2n^2.          \tag{1}
\]
There are at least $\binom{n-2cn}{2}$ pairs of unsaturated vertices,
and at most $nD/2=O(cn^{3/2})$ existing edges. These estimates ensure an
eligible pair always exists for large $n$, so the process can perform
all $m$ steps.

Fix a set $U$ of $u=\lceil5cn\rceil$ vertices. Conditional on $U$ still
being independent, at least $3cn$ of its vertices are unsaturated.
By (1), its eligible internal pairs number at least
\[
 \binom{3cn}{2}-(2+o(1))c^2n^2\ge2c^2n^2
\]
for large $n$. There are at most $n^2/2$ eligible pairs altogether.
Thus, at each step before $U$ ceases to be independent, the chance
of selecting an edge inside it is at least $4c^2$, and in particular
at least $3c^2$. Iterated conditional probabilities give
\[
 \Pr(U\hbox{ remains independent})\le(1-3c^2)^m
 \le\exp(-3c^2m).
\]
The union bound over all $U$ gives probability at most
\[
 \exp\left(u\log(en/u)-3c^2m\right).                     \tag{2}
\]
The positive term is $O(cn\log n)$, whereas the magnitude of the
negative term is $(3+o(1))c^4n^{3/2}$. Their ratio tends to infinity,
since
\[
 \frac{c^3\sqrt n}{\log n}
 =\frac{n^{1/2-3\eta}}{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Therefore (2) is less than one for large $n$. There is an extension
$H$ with independence number $\alpha(H)<u$.

Now add edges to $H$ as long as this preserves triangle-freeness,
obtaining a maximal triangle-free graph $J$. Every neighborhood in $J$
is independent in $J$, and hence independent in $H$. Thus
\[
 \Delta(J)<u,\qquad e(J)\le n(u-1)/2=O(cn^2)=o(n^2).
\]
Every nonadjacent pair of vertices in $J$ has a common neighbor;
otherwise their edge could still be added. Consequently $J$ is
connected and has diameter at most $2$. For $n\ge3$ a triangle-free
graph cannot be complete, so its diameter is exactly $2$.
The number of edges added to the original graph is at most
$e(J)=o(n^2)$, and is therefore at most $\delta n^2$ for all sufficiently
large $n$.\hfill$\square$

\section{VERIFICATION}
The estimate is uniform in the initial graph; all probabilities are
conditional on its history. Independence can only be lost during the
process, which justifies the survival bound. The final maximal extension
need not retain the intermediate degree cap $D$; its degree is instead
controlled by the already established independence bound. These are
separate stages of the argument.

\section{FINAL STATUS}
\textbf{SOLVED (known positive answer reconstructed).}
The construction adds $O(n^{2-\min(\varepsilon,1/12)})$ edges. The proof
uses only elementary counting and the probabilistic method.
