\section{WORK: COUNTING THE REQUIRED OBSTRUCTIONS}
Let $T$ count nontrivial three-term progressions of $B$ as unordered triples, and write $r=f_3(B)$. Independently retain each element with probability $p$. Deleting at most one point from each surviving progression leaves a progression-free set, so
\[
r\ge pN-p^3T\qquad(0\le p\le1).
\]
If $T<N/3$, taking $p=1$ gives $r>N(2/3)$. Therefore whenever $r\le2N/3$ we have $T\ge N/3$ and may put $p=\sqrt{N/(3T)}\le1$. This yields
\[
r\ge\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}\frac{N^{3/2}}{\sqrt T},
\qquad
T\ge\frac{4N^3}{27r^2}.
\]
More generally, for $k$-term progressions, optimizing $pN-p^kT_k$ gives
\[
f_k(B)\ge\frac{k-1}{k}N\left(\frac{N}{kT_k}\right)^{1/(k-1)}
\quad\text{when }T_k\ge N/k.
\]
Thus a proposed family with unusually small progression-free subsets must support quantitatively many actual progressions. This is the usual alteration argument, not a novel extremal theorem.
