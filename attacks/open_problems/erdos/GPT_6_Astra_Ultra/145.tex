\subsection*{2) WORK}
The following elementary jump lemma applies to any positive increasing integer sequence $s_k$ with $s_{k+1}/s_k\to1$. Suppose, for a fixed $\gamma\ge0$,
\[
F_\gamma(y):=\sum_{s_{k+1}\le y}g_k^\gamma=B_\gamma y+o(y).
\]
At $y=s_{k+1}$ the jump of $F_\gamma$ is exactly $g_k^\gamma$. Apply the asymptotic both at $s_{k+1}$ and at $s_{k+1}-1/2$. Subtracting gives
\[
g_k^\gamma=B_\gamma/2+o(s_{k+1})=o(s_{k+1}).
\]
If $s_k\le x<s_{k+1}$, the left-endpoint and right-endpoint sums differ by precisely this one jump. As $s_{k+1}/x\to1$, their normalized difference is $o(1)$. Therefore the same asymptotic, with the same constant, holds with cutoff $s_k\le x$.

For squarefree integers, their positive asymptotic density $6/\pi^2$ gives $s_{k+1}/s_k\to1$: a gap from $s_k$ to at least $(1+\delta)s_k$ would contradict the difference between the counting asymptotics at these two arguments. Thus any established right-endpoint moment asymptotic transfers automatically. In particular Chan's checked theorem for $0\le\gamma<3.75$ transfers throughout that same interval, without importing a particular pointwise squarefree-gap exponent.

For higher moments the original obstruction persists: existence of all fixed-gap frequencies $p_m$ does not bound
\[
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac1x\sum_{s_k\le x,\ g_k>Y}g_k^\alpha.
\]
The jump lemma controls only the final gap, not this whole tail.
