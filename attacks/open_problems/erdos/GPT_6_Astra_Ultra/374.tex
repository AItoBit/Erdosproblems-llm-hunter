\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#374: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
Let $F(m)$ be the least number of distinct factorials, including $m!$, whose product is a square, and $D_k=\{m:F(m)=k\}$. Determine their growth for $3\le k\le6$, especially whether $D_6(X)\gg X$.

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{374.tex}.
The source enlarges fixed-prime semiprime families and obtains an unbounded multiple of $X/\log X$. I show why even complete success on every semiprime cannot yield the requested positive density.
\subsection{2) WORK}
\paragraph{An elementary density barrier.}
Let $\mathcal P_y$ be the primes at most $y$, and write $w_y(n)=\#\{p\in\mathcal P_y:p\mid n\}$. Every semiprime $n=pq$ has $w_y(n)\le2$. Pointwise,
\[
\mathbf1_{w_y(n)\le2}\le e^2e^{-w_y(n)}.
\]
The right-hand side is periodic modulo $\prod_{p\le y}p$. By CRT its mean is
\[
e^2\prod_{p\le y}\left(1-{1-e^{-1}\over p}\right).
\]
As $y\to\infty$ this tends to zero, using the divergence of $\sum_p1/p$ and $1-u\le e^{-u}$. For every fixed $y$ it bounds the upper density of all semiprimes. Therefore the semiprimes have natural density zero.

The same proof works for integers with at most any fixed number $r$ of distinct prime factors, replacing $e^2$ by $e^r$. Thus no construction restricted to a fixed bound on the number of prime factors can establish $D_6(X)\gg X$, even if it settles every integer in its selected class. This is stronger than simply observing that one fixed semiprime family has size about $X/\log X$.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The auxiliary parameter $y$ is fixed before taking the density limit; only afterward is it sent to infinity. This avoids any unjustified uniform error estimate. The result is a barrier for restricted constructions, not an upper bound on $D_6$ itself. The square-product identity only proves $F(m)\le6$; excluding smaller representations is a separate requirement.
\subsection{4) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED.}
Positive-density membership in $D_6$, and the requested orders for the other $D_k$, remain unproved. One needs families with unbounded prime-factor complexity together with control of minimal representation length.
