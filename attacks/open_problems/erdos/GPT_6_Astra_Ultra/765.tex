\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#765: the asymptotic \(C_4\) extremal number}
\subsection*{Statement and outcome}
Determine the asymptotic value of \(\operatorname{ex}(n,C_4)\), the maximum edge count in a simple \(n\)-vertex graph containing no four-cycle. We reconstruct the classical answer
\[
\operatorname{ex}(n,C_4)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)n^{3/2}.
\]
Attempt status: solved. The lower-order refinements mentioned in the editorial are separate questions.

\subsection*{Counting common neighbours}
In a \(C_4\)-free graph, two distinct vertices have at most one common neighbour. Counting unordered pairs of neighbours of each vertex therefore gives
\[
\sum_{v}\binom{d(v)}2\leq\binom n2.
\]
If \(e\) is the number of edges, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\frac{4e^2}{n}-2e\leq n(n-1).
\]
Solving the quadratic,
\[
e\leq\frac n4\left(1+\sqrt{4n-3}\right)
=\frac12n^{3/2}+\frac n4+O(\sqrt n). \tag{1}
\]

\subsection*{An explicit polarity construction}
Let \(q\) be an odd prime, and let the vertices be the one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^3\), of which there are \(v=q^2+q+1\). For nonzero representatives define
\[
B((x,y,z),(x',y',z'))=xz'+zx'-2yy'.
\]
Join two \emph{distinct} projective points exactly when \(B=0\). This is well-defined under scaling, symmetric, and nondegenerate, since its matrix has nonzero determinant in odd characteristic.

For two distinct projective points \(u,v\), the equations \(B(u,w)=B(v,w)=0\) are independent linear equations: otherwise nondegeneracy would force \(u,v\) proportional. Their joint solution space has dimension one. Thus the two vertices have at most one common neighbour, and the graph has no \(C_4\).

The orthogonal plane to any vertex contains \(q+1\) projective points. The graph degree is \(q+1\), except that a self-orthogonal point loses its loop and has degree \(q\). Self-orthogonality is \(xz=y^2\). Its projective solutions are
\[
[t^2:t:1]\quad(t\in\mathbb F_q),\qquad [1:0:0],
\]
so there are exactly \(q+1\) of them. Therefore
\[
e=\frac12\big((q^2+q+1)(q+1)-(q+1)\big)
=\frac12q(q+1)^2. \tag{2}
\]
This includes the loop correction and establishes the sharp leading coefficient on these orders.

\subsection*{Passing to every large order}
We use the classical prime number theorem only in the following explicit form: if \(x\to\infty\), the largest prime \(q\leq x\) satisfies \(q/x\to1\). For any fixed \(\eta>0\), this follows from
\(\pi(x)-\pi((1-\eta)x)\sim\eta x/\log x>0\).

Take \(x=(\sqrt{4n-3}-1)/2\) and the largest odd prime \(q\leq x\). Then \(q^2+q+1\leq n\) and \(q=(1-o(1))\sqrt n\). Add isolated vertices to (2) to obtain exactly \(n\) vertices. Its edge count is \((1/2-o(1))n^{3/2}\), which together with (1) proves the formula.

\subsection*{Attribution and scope}
The upper bound is Reiman's common-neighbour argument, and the finite-field lower construction is the Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi/Brown polarity construction. The proof is given above, with the prime number theorem explicitly external.
