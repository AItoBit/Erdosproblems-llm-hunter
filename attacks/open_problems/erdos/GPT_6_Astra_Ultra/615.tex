\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 615: dense $K_4$-free counterexamples}
\subsection*{Statement and scope}
Does some constant $c>0$ guarantee a $K_4$ in every sufficiently large $n$-vertex graph with at least $(1/8-c)n^2$ edges and independence number less than $n/\log n$? The answer is no. We reconstruct the quantitative Bollob\'as--Erd\H{o}s construction as developed by Fox--Loh--Zhao. Two precise geometric existence/measure estimates are used as external inputs; the graph construction, forbidden-subgraph proof and parameter choice are supplied below.

\subsection*{Geometric inputs}
Write $S^{h-1}$ for the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^h$ with normalized surface measure $\lambda$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is an absolute $C$ such that, for $0<\mu<1$ and even $n\ge(C/\mu)^h$, the sphere can be partitioned into $n/2$ measurable pieces of equal measure and diameter at most $\mu/4$. This is the Feige--Schechtman partition result in the form used in Section 8 of Fox--Loh--Zhao.
\item A measurable subset of $S^{h-1}$ of diameter at most $2-\delta$, where $0<\delta<1$, has measure at most $2e^{-\delta h/2}$. This is their Corollary 8.7, from spherical isodiametry and cap concentration.
\end{enumerate}
These are the only substantial external inputs. In particular we do not assume the Ramsey--Tur\'an conclusion being proved.

\subsection*{Construction and exclusion of $K_4$}
Choose $0<\varepsilon<1/100$, an integer $h\ge16$, and put $\mu=\varepsilon/\sqrt h$. Take a partition $D_1,\ldots,D_{n/2}$ from the first input and points $x_i,y_i\in D_i$. Regard $X=\{x_i\}$ and $Y=\{y_i\}$ as disjoint labelled vertex sets, even if some coordinates coincide. Join two vertices in the same part when their Euclidean distance exceeds $2-\mu$, and join $x\in X$ to $y\in Y$ when $|x-y|<\sqrt2-\mu$.

Three unit vectors $x,x',x''$ in one part cannot form a triangle, since that would give
\[
 0\le|x+x'+x''|^2
 =9-|x-x'|^2-|x-x''|^2-|x'-x''|^2
 <9-3(2-\mu)^2<0.
\]
A $K_4$ must therefore use two vertices from each part. Such vertices would satisfy
\begin{align*}
0&\le|x+x'-y-y'|^2\\
 &=|x-y|^2+|x-y'|^2+|x'-y|^2+|x'-y'|^2
   -|x-x'|^2-|y-y'|^2\\
 &<4(\sqrt2-\mu)^2-2(2-\mu)^2
 =\mu(2\mu+8-8\sqrt2)<0.
\end{align*}
Thus the graph is $K_4$-free.

\subsection*{Independence and density estimates}
If $I\subseteq X$ is independent, its representative points have diameter at most $2-\mu$. The union of the corresponding partition pieces has diameter at most $2-\mu/2$. The second input yields
\[
 \frac{|I|}{n/2}\le2e^{-\mu h/4}.
\]
Apply this also to $Y$ to obtain
\[
 \alpha(G)\le2n e^{-\varepsilon\sqrt h/4}. \tag{1}
\]

For completeness, a rough cap estimate suffices for the density. The first coordinate of a uniform point of $S^{h-1}$ has density proportional to $(1-t^2)^{(h-3)/2}$ on $[-1,1]$. For $h\ge16$ its normalizing integral is at least
\[
 \int_{-1/\sqrt h}^{1/\sqrt h}(1-t^2)^{(h-3)/2}\,dt
 \ge\frac{2e^{-1}}{\sqrt h}>rac{1}{2\sqrt h}.
\]
Here $\log(1-u)\ge-2u$ for $0\le u\le1/2$ justifies the lower bound. Thus the normalized density is at most $2\sqrt h$.
For a fixed unit vector $x$, the cap $|x-y|\le\sqrt2-2\mu$ corresponds, after rotation, to
\[
 y_1\ge 2\sqrt2\mu-2\mu^2\le3\varepsilon/\sqrt h.
\]
Symmetry and the density bound show that this cap has measure at least $1/2-6\varepsilon$. It meets at least $(1/2-6\varepsilon)n/2$ partition pieces. The chosen representative of every piece it meets lies within $\mu/4$ of a cap point, hence has distance strictly less than $\sqrt2-\mu$ from $x$. Every vertex therefore has at least $(1/4-3\varepsilon)n$ neighbours in the opposite part, and
\[
 e(G)\ge(1/8-3\varepsilon/2)n^2. \tag{2}
\]

\subsection*{Parameters and the quantifiers}
Given any proposed $c>0$, choose a fixed $\varepsilon<\min\{c/3,1/100\}$. Along sufficiently large even integers $n$, put
\[
 h=\left\lfloor\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right\rfloor.
\]
The partition hypothesis holds because
\[
 \log (C\sqrt h/\varepsilon)^h
 =h\bigl(\tfrac12\log h+\log(C/\varepsilon)\bigr)
 =(1/2+o(1))\log n<\log n.
\]
Also $\sqrt h/\log\log n\to\infty$, so (1) is $o(n/\log n)$, in particular strictly smaller than $n/\log n$. Formula (2) exceeds $(1/8-c)n^2$. These arbitrarily large even orders already refute the proposed universal eventual bound. No parity extension is needed for that conclusion.
