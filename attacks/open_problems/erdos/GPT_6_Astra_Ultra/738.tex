\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#738: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
Does every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number contain every finite tree as an induced subgraph?

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{738.tex}.
The source transfers known finite theorems about radius-two trees, subdivided stars, induced subdivisions and path-induced copies. Those are literature consequences, not a solution for all trees. I isolate a sufficient cycle exclusion that makes an elementary embedding argument work, and explain precisely why triangle-freeness alone does not supply it.
\subsection{2) WORK}
\paragraph{A direct high-girth case.}
Let $T$ have $t\ge2$ vertices and diameter $d$. Every finite graph $G$ with $\chi(G)\ge t$ and girth greater than $d+1$ contains an induced copy of $T$.

First, $G$ has a nonempty subgraph of minimum degree at least $t-1$. Otherwise successively deleting a vertex of degree at most $t-2$ and coloring in reverse order would give a $(t-1)$-coloring. Root $T$ and embed it greedily in this subgraph, parents before children. When inserting a child, at most $t-2$ already used vertices other than its parent can block neighbors of the parent. Minimum degree $t-1$ therefore leaves an unused neighbor. This produces an ordinary copy of $T$.

Any additional ambient edge between two nonadjacent vertices of this copy, at tree distance $s$, would create a cycle of length $s+1\le d+1$. The girth hypothesis excludes every such edge, so the copy is induced. By the finite-coloring compactness theorem the same conclusion holds when $G$ has infinite chromatic number and that girth condition.

More precisely it suffices to exclude the lengths $\{d_T(u,v)+1:u,v\text{ nonadjacent in }T\}$, rather than all short cycles. Triangle-freeness excludes only the distance-two chords. Chords between vertices at distance three create four-cycles, which are entirely compatible with triangle-freeness. Removing ambient edges to obtain a high-girth subgraph does not repair this, since inducedness must be measured in the original graph.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The greedy proof is for a finite tree and an ordinary subgraph, followed by a separate ambient-chord check. It is not valid to confuse a subdivision with the original tree or to transfer inducedness from an arbitrary edge-deleted subgraph. The additional girth assumption is explicitly stronger than the target.
\subsection{4) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED.}
An argument ruling out the longer-distance chords using only triangle-freeness and large chromatic number is still missing. This is a verified special case and an identified obstruction, not an extension of the known general tree families.
