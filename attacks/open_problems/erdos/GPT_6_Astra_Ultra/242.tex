\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#242: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 23 September 2026. Source versions read in full: \texttt{242.tex} in \texttt{gpt\_pro\_5.4}.

\subsection*{1) OBJECTIVE AND SOURCE AUDIT}
The target is $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ with $x<y<z$ positive integers, for every
$n>2$. The supplied congruence identities are correct; the surviving
$n\equiv1\pmod{24}$ condition does not itself generate denominators.
I replace further ad hoc congruence guessing by a complete divisor test
for each fixed $n$, and apply it to survivors of the supplied tests.

\subsection*{2) WORK: AN EXACT DIVISOR CRITERION}
There is a solution if and only if there is an integer
\[
 n/4<x<3n/4
\]
and positive divisors $d<e$ of $N^2$, where $N=nx$ and $m=4x-n$, such that
\[
 de=N^2,\qquad d\equiv e\equiv-N\pmod m,\qquad (d+N)/m>x.
\]
The corresponding denominators are $y=(d+N)/m$, $z=(e+N)/m$.
Indeed, after removing $1/x$ the equation becomes
$m/N=1/y+1/z$, which is equivalent to
\[
 (my-N)(mz-N)=N^2.
\]
Each factor is positive because $m/N>1/y$ and $m/N>1/z$.
The congruences make $y,z$ integral, $d<e$ makes them distinct, and the
last inequality enforces the order. Conversely the factor identity
expands to exactly the required unit-fraction equation.
Finally, $4/n>1/x$ and $4/n<3/x$ give the strict range for $x$.

Exact arithmetic gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
n&x&y&z\\\hline
73&20&210&30660\\
97&25&810&392850\\
193&50&1380&1331700
\end{array}
\]
For example, for $n=73,x=20$, $m=7,N=1460$ and the factors are
$d=10$, $e=213160$. All three displayed rows were independently checked
as rational identities. Each $n$ is $1\pmod{24}$, and respectively
$n+1=2\cdot37,2\cdot7^2,2\cdot97$; the middle example is already handled
by the source's divisor criterion, while the first and third are not.

\subsection*{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The factor test is finite for a fixed $n$ but its search range grows with
$n$. Exhibiting solutions for these values proves no uniform coverage of
the surviving residue class. The factorization identity is standard algebra,
not a claimed new resolution of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture.
The companion exact checks are in \texttt{verification/root\_checks.py}.
