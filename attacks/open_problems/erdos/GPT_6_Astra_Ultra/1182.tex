\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1182 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
Let $R(H,G)$ denote the two-colour Ramsey number. For each $n$, define $f(n)$ as the greatest edge count of a connected $n$-vertex graph $G$ satisfying $R(K_3,G)=2n-1$. Define $F(n)$ as the greatest threshold such that every connected $n$-vertex graph with at most $F(n)$ edges satisfies this equality. Determine the asymptotic sizes of $f(n)$ and $F(n)$. The original questions include whether $F(n)/n\to\infty$.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
Every connected $n$-vertex graph has Ramsey number at least $2n-1$ against a
triangle. On $2n-2$ vertices, make the red graph complete bipartite with two
parts of size $n-1$. It has no red triangle, while the blue graph consists of
two disjoint cliques, neither large enough to contain a connected $n$-vertex graph.

For every tree $T$ on $n$ vertices the lower bound is attained. Consider any
coloring on $2n-1$ vertices with no red triangle. If a vertex has at least $n$
red neighbors, those neighbors form a blue clique, which contains $T$.
Otherwise every vertex has red degree at most $n-1$, hence blue degree at least
$n-1$. A graph of minimum degree at least $n-1$ contains every $n$-vertex tree:
root the tree and embed vertices in parent-before-child order; whenever a new
child is needed, its already embedded parent has at most $n-2$ other occupied
vertices to avoid and at least $n-1$ neighbors.
Thus
\[R(K_3,T)=2n-1.\tag{1}\]

Every connected graph with $n-1$ edges is a tree, so (1) gives the baseline
$F(n)\ge n-1$ and $f(n)\ge n-1$. These are independently proved elementary
benchmarks, not a claim of a new tree Ramsey theorem. The source's cited
linear upper bound for $F$ answers its superlinear subquestion negatively;
that external upper-bound proof is not reconstructed here.
