\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 248: uniformly few prime factors in all forward shifts}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
Must infinitely many integers $n$ satisfy
$\omega(n+k)\leq Ck$ for every $k\geq1$, with one absolute
constant $C$? Tao and Ter\"av\"ainen proved the stronger
statement with $\Omega$, which counts prime factors with
multiplicity. This attempt proves the truncation and moment
reductions below, but does not construct the necessary sieve
measure. The full problem is unresolved within this attempt.

\subsection{2) Only a logarithmic window needs attention}
The elementary inequality $2^{\Omega(m)}\leq m$ gives
$\Omega(m)\leq\log_2m$, where $\log_2$ here means logarithm
to base two. Let $x$ be large, $x\leq n\leq2x$, and put
$L=\lceil\log_2(3x)\rceil$.
For $L\leq k\leq x$ we have
$\Omega(n+k)\leq\log_2(3x)\leq k$.
For $k>x$, we have $n+k<3k$, and
$\log_2(3k)\leq k$ for $k\geq4$.
Thus all $k\geq L$ are automatic. The challenge is to control
$1\leq k<L$ with an absolute constant as $x$ grows.

\subsection{3) Separating primes from higher prime powers}
Put $R=x^{1/100}$ and, for $m\leq3x$, define
\[
 Y(m)=\sum_{p\leq R}1_{p\mid m},\qquad
 T(m)=\sum_{\substack{p^j\leq R\\j\geq2}}1_{p^j\mid m}.
\]
The prime factors greater than $R$, counted with multiplicity,
contribute at most $\log(3x)/\log R\leq200$ for large $x$.
For $p\leq R$, put $r_p=\lfloor\log R/\log p\rfloor\geq1$.
The contribution of $p$ to $Y+T$ is $\min(\nu_p(m),r_p)$.
If $\nu_p(m)>r_p$, then
\[
 \frac{\nu_p(m)}{r_p}
 \leq\frac{\log(3x)/\log p}
           {\tfrac12\log R/\log p}\leq400.
\]
The same comparison is immediate if $\nu_p(m)\leq r_p$.
Therefore the deterministic bound
\[
 \Omega(m)\leq400\bigl(Y(m)+T(m)+1\bigr)
\]
holds. This accounts for multiplicities, rather than silently
replacing $\Omega$ by $\omega$.

\subsection{4) A sufficient pair of moment estimates}
Here is a precise probabilistic input that would finish the proof.
Suppose there are absolute constants $A,B$ such that for every
sufficiently large $x$ there is a probability measure on integers
$n\in[x,2x]$ satisfying, for $1\leq k<L$,
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl(Y(n+k)-Ak\bigr)_+^4\leq Bk^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E T(n+k)^2\leq Bk^{1/2}.
\]
The subscript $+$ denotes the positive part.
Choose a fixed $d>0$ so large that
$2B/d^4+3B/d^2<1$.
Markov's inequality and the union bound show that the probability
of any violation of
$Y(n+k)\leq(A+d)k$ or $T(n+k)\leq dk$ is at most
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{L-1}
   \left(\frac{B}{d^4k^2}+\frac{B}{d^2k^{3/2}}\right)
 \leq\frac{2B}{d^4}+\frac{3B}{d^2}<1.
\]
We used the integral bounds $\sum k^{-2}\leq2$ and
$\sum k^{-3/2}\leq3$. Some integer in $[x,2x]$ therefore
satisfies
$\Omega(n+k)\leq400(A+2d+1)k$ throughout the finite window.
The preceding truncation handles every larger $k$.
The constants are independent of $x$; letting $x\to\infty$
then gives infinitely many such integers.

\subsection{5) Remaining gap and attribution}
The moment hypotheses are sufficient conditions proved sufficient
here, not estimates established in this attempt. Constructing
one measure that supplies them uniformly over the growing shift
window is the essential missing step. A different measure for
each $k$, or constants growing with $x$, would not suffice.
The published solution uses a slowly growing-dimensional
Maynard-type sieve and suitable second and fourth moments.
We have not independently reconstructed its correlation estimates
or claimed that our simplified moment hypotheses are its
verbatim statement.

Sources: \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/248};
T.~Tao and J.~Ter\"av\"ainen,
\emph{Quantitative correlations and some problems on prime
factors of consecutive integers}, Theorem 1.1 and Section 2,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01739}.
The known resolution belongs to those authors; this is a partial
reconstruction of its probabilistic strategy.
