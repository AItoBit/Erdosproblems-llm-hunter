\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 1160 --- GPT 6 Astra Ultra partial attempt}


\subsection*{FORMAL RESTATEMENT}
Let $g(n)$ count the isomorphism classes of groups of order $n$. Must
\[
g(n)\leq g(2^m)
\]
hold for every positive integer $n\leq2^m$?

\subsection*{CONTEXT AND SCOPE}
This replaces the earlier statement-only record with an elementary mathematical attempt. The arguments below establish only their stated partial results; no novelty or full solution is claimed.

\subsection*{ATTACK PLAN AND WORK}
A useful comparison is available at odd orders. If $n$ is odd, the assignment
$G\mapsto G\times C_2$ embeds isomorphism classes of groups of order $n$ into
those of order $2n$. The subgroup of all odd-order elements in this direct
product is exactly $G\times\{1\}$: the second coordinate is nontrivial precisely
when the element has even order. It is characteristic, so an isomorphism of
the products recovers an isomorphism of the original groups. Thus
\[g(n)\le g(2n)\qquad(n\text{ odd}).\tag{1}\]
This does not compare $n$ with a nearby power of two.

One can also exhibit many different groups at the target order without invoking
classification. For $0\le j\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, the groups
$C_{2^{m-j}}\times C_{2^j}$ have order $2^m$ and distinct exponents
$2^{m-j}$, hence
\[g(2^m)\ge\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1.\tag{2}\]
In particular the desired comparison holds for $n$ prime, where a nonidentity
element generates the whole group and $g(n)=1$. It also holds for prime-square
orders when $m\ge2$: a group of order $p^2$ is abelian (its nontrivial center and
the fact that a cyclic central quotient forces commutativity prove this), and
the only two possibilities are $C_{p^2}$ and $C_p\times C_p$.

\subsection*{VERIFICATION AND REMAINING GAP}
Control the number of groups at arbitrary mixed-prime orders below $2^m$.
The recoverable-product injection changes the order in the wrong way for the
full comparison, and the explicit abelian family is only a lower bound.
