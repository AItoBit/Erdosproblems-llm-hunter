\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 466}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Let \(N(X,\delta)\) be the largest size of a set in a disc of radius
\(X\) whose pairwise distances stay at least \(\delta\) from every
integer. Does \(N(X,\delta)\to\infty\) for some fixed \(\delta>0\)?
Yes. The following explicit parabola construction proves this for
\(\delta=2/5\), with a logarithmic lower bound.

\paragraph{The points and the distance calculation.}
Take
\[
 P_j=(100^j,10^j),\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,L.
\]
For \(i<j\), put \(s=10^j+10^i\), \(e=10^j-10^i\).
The horizontal difference \(D=es=100^j-100^i\) is an integer, and
\[
 |P_j-P_i|=e\sqrt{s^2+1}=D+\eta,\qquad
 \eta=\frac{e}{\sqrt{s^2+1}+s}.
 \tag{1}
\]
We have \(s\geq11\), \(e/s\geq9/11\), and \(e<s\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{9}{\sqrt{122}+11}
 \leq\eta<\frac12.
 \tag{2}
\]
The left side exceeds \(2/5\), since
\(45>2\sqrt{122}+22\) is equivalent to \(529>488\).
Since \(D\) is an integer, (1)--(2) show
\[
 \bigl\||P_j-P_i|\bigr\|=\eta>\frac25
\]
for every pair.

\paragraph{Fit the points into the required disc.}
Their norms satisfy
\[
 |P_j|=\sqrt{100^{2j}+100^j}\leq\sqrt2\,100^j.
\]
For \(X\geq2\), choose
\(L=\lfloor\log(X/2)/\log100\rfloor\).
All \(L+1\) points lie in the disc of radius \(X\), and
\[
 N(X,2/5)\geq L+1>
 \frac{\log(X/2)}{\log100}.
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular this tends to infinity. For \(X\geq4\), (3) also gives
\(N(X,2/5)>\log X/(2\log100)>\log X/10\).
Monotonicity in \(\delta\) gives the same lower bound at \(\delta=1/10\).

An explicit family with a fixed distance margin and unbounded size is proved.
