\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 798: covering a grid by determined lines}

\paragraph{Statement and claim.}
For the grid \([n]^2\), let \(t(n)\) be the least number of
selected grid points whose pairwise determined lines cover
the whole grid. We reconstruct Alon's bounds
\[
 c n^{2/3}\leq t(n)\leq C n^{2/3}\log n
 \quad(n\geq2),
\]
so in particular \(t(n)=o(n)\).
Source: N. Alon, \emph{Economical coverings of sets of lattice
points}, \url{https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/lattice.pdf}.
See \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/798}.
The planar argument below includes the approximation and
probabilistic steps explicitly.

\paragraph{Lower bound by separating directions.}
Take \(t\) selected points. Write a lattice-line direction
as a primitive integer vector \((u,v)\), identified with its
negative, and put \(q=\max(|u|,|v|)\).
There are at most \(4q\) directions with this value of \(q\).
For each direction, at most \(t/2\) distinct parallel lines
can contain two selected points, since they use disjoint
pairs of selected points.
Thus the number \(f_q\) of determined lines of this type
satisfies
\[
 f_q\leq2tq,\qquad \sum_qf_q\leq\binom t2.
\]
A determined line has \(q\leq n-1\), and contains at most
\(\lfloor(n-1)/q\rfloor+1\leq2n/q\) grid points.
For any integer \(Q\geq1\), the number of covered points is
at most
\[
 \sum_{q\leq Q}2tq\frac{2n}{q}
 +\sum_{q>Q}f_q\frac{2n}{Q}
 \leq4ntQ+\frac{nt^2}{Q}.
\]
Taking \(Q=\lceil\sqrt t\rceil\) gives \(O(nt^{3/2})\).
Covering all \(n^2\) grid points therefore requires
\(t\gg n^{2/3}\).

\paragraph{An approximation lemma proved by pigeonhole.}
Set \(Q=\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil\).
For any two grid points \(x,a\), there are integers \(p_1,p_2\)
and a real \(z\) such that
\[
 1\leq q:=\max(|p_1|,|p_2|)\leq Q,\qquad
 |x_i-a_i-p_i z|\leq Q^2/q,                                   \tag{1}
\]
and for one coordinate \(j\), \(p_j=q\) and
\(x_j-a_j-p_jz=0\).
If \(x=a\), choose \((p_1,p_2)=(1,0)\), \(z=0\).
Otherwise choose \(j\) with maximal nonzero \(|x_j-a_j|\);
let \(\alpha\) be the ratio of the other coordinate difference
to this one, so \(|\alpha|\leq1\).
Among the \(Q+1\) fractional parts of \(0,\alpha,\ldots,Q\alpha\),
two fall in the same one of \(Q\) equal intervals.
Their difference gives \(1\leq q\leq Q\) and an integer \(p\)
with \(|q\alpha-p|\leq1/Q\), \(|p|\leq q\).
Take \(p_j=q\), the other component \(p\), and
\(z=(x_j-a_j)/q\). The nonzero error is at most
\(n/(qQ)\leq Q^2/q\), proving (1).

\paragraph{Many centres admit a small-error line.}
Fix a grid point \(x\), and fix \(C_0=1024\).
The number of centres \(a\) admitting data in (1) with
\(q<Q/C_0\) is at most \(n^2/4\), for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Indeed, for each \(q\) there are two choices of \(j\), at
most \(n\) choices of \(a_j\), at most \(3q\) choices of the
other integer component of \(p\), and at most
\(3Q^2/q\) choices of the remaining coordinate of \(a\).
The total is at most
\[
 (Q/C_0)\,18nQ^2
 \leq(144/C_0)n^2<n^2/4,
\]
using \(Q^3\leq8n\).
Exclude also centres within \((C_0+2)Q+1\) of the boundary;
they number \(O(nQ)=o(n^2)\), and at most \(n^2/4\) for
sufficiently large \(n\).
At least half the grid points remain as interior centres
for which the approximation has \(q\geq Q/C_0\).
For these centres, (1) gives
\[
 |x_i-a_i-p_i z|\leq C_0Q,\qquad |p_i|\leq Q.                  \tag{2}
\]

\paragraph{A small square whose pairs cover the fixed point.}
For an interior centre \(a\), let \(B(a)\) contain all grid
points with \(|y_i-a_i|\leq(C_0+2)Q\).
It has at most \((2(C_0+2)Q+1)^2=O(Q^2)\) points.
Under (2), the line
\[
 y_i=x_i-p_iw
\]
passes through \(x\) at \(w=0\), and at \(w=z\) lies within
\(C_0Q\) of \(a\) in each coordinate.
Its two points at integer parameters
\(\lceil z\rceil,\lceil z\rceil+1\) both lie in \(B(a)\),
because each parameter differs from \(z\) by at most two.
They are distinct since \(p\ne0\).
Thus a line determined by two members of \(B(a)\) covers \(x\).

\paragraph{Random centres cover every point simultaneously.}
Choose \(r=\lceil3\log_2 n\rceil+1\) independent uniformly
random interior centres.
For each fixed \(x\), at least half of all interior centres
work, by the preceding count; its probability of being
uncovered is at most \(2^{-r}<n^{-3}\).
The union bound over \(n^2\) grid points gives a probability
less than one of any uncovered point.
Some choice therefore covers the entire grid using the
selected set \(\bigcup_{j=1}^rB(a_j)\), whose size is
\[
 O(rQ^2)=O(n^{2/3}\log n).
\]
Enlarging the absolute constant handles the finitely many
smaller \(n\geq2\). Since \(n^{-1/3}\log n\to0\), the
requested sublinear conclusion follows.

\paragraph{Completion estimate.}
\textbf{COMPLETION: 100\%}
