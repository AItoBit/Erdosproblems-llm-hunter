\section{WORK: A GREEDY BELOW-ONE CERTIFICATE}
For $N\ge2$, there exists $A\subseteq\{2,\ldots,N\}$ with
\[
0<1-\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\le\frac1N.
\]
Start with residual $r_1=1$. At step $n\ge2$, include $n$ exactly when $1/n<r_{n-1}$; subtract $1/n$ if included. The residual always stays strictly positive. Inductively $r_{n-1}\le1/(n-1)$. If $n$ is skipped, $r_n\le1/n$. If included,
\[
r_n\le\frac1{n-1}-\frac1n=\frac1{n(n-1)}\le\frac1n.
\]
This proves the certificate. Since its total sum is below one, none of its subsets can sum to one.
Now any inadmissible set in the source's old formulation contains a proper subset summing to one, so its positive excess is at least $1/N$. The greedy admissible certificate has distance at most $1/N$. Thus if a minimizer of the unrestricted problem is inadmissible, its value is at least that of an admissible set; otherwise it already qualifies. This proves equality of the two minima for all $N\ge2$, and $N=1$ follows directly, allowing the empty set.
