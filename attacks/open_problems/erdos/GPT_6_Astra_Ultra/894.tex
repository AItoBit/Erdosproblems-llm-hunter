\section*{Erd\H{o}s Problem 894: colouring lacunary differences}

\paragraph{Statement and result.}
Suppose \(n_{j+1}\geq(1+\epsilon)n_j\), where \(\epsilon>0\).
There is a finite colouring of the positive integers such that
no two equally coloured integers have difference \(n_j\).
We give the circle-colouring deduction explicitly, including its
quantitative bound. The Diophantine input is external.

\paragraph{The precise input.}
Peres and Schlag, Theorem 3.1 of
\emph{Two Erd\H{o}s problems on lacunary sequences: chromatic number
and Diophantine approximation},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0223}, prove that an increasing
integer sequence satisfying
\[
 n_{j+M}>2n_j,\qquad M\geq4,
\]
admits \(\theta\in[0,1)\) with
\(\|n_j\theta\|\geq\delta=c/(M\log M)>0\) for every \(j\);
here \(c>0\) is an absolute constant, decreased if necessary.
The local-lemma proof of this theorem is not repeated here.
Taking
\[
 M=\max\left(4,1+\left\lceil
                  \frac{\log2}{\log(1+\epsilon)}\right\rceil\right)
\]
verifies its strict doubling hypothesis.

\paragraph{Construction and verification of the colouring.}
Choose an integer \(q>1/\delta\), and colour \(a\in\mathbb N\)
by the unique \(i\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) such that
\[
 \{a\theta\}\in[i/q,(i+1)/q).
\]
These are fractional parts, rather than distances to the nearest
integer: the latter would identify opposite points of the circle.
If \(a,b\) have the same colour, their fractional parts differ
by less than \(1/q\). Consequently
\[
 \|(a-b)\theta\|<1/q<\delta.
\]
Thus \(|a-b|\) cannot equal any \(n_j\).
This verifies every forbidden difference simultaneously and
does not require \(\theta\) to be irrational.
For \(0<\epsilon\leq1/2\), we obtain
\[
 q=O\!\left(\epsilon^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon)\right).
\]
For larger \(\epsilon\), a fixed absolute number of colours
follows from the same formula for \(M\).

\paragraph{Why dependence on lacunarity is necessary.}
For \(0<\epsilon\leq1/2\), put \(K=\lfloor1/\epsilon\rfloor\).
The initial segment \(1,2,\ldots,K\) satisfies the required
successive-ratio bound. Extend it indefinitely by choosing each
next integer at least \(1+\epsilon\) times its predecessor.
The vertices \(1,2,\ldots,K+1\) form a clique in its difference
graph, because all their positive pairwise differences belong
to the initial segment. Hence some such sequences require
at least \(K+1\) colours. The upper bound therefore has the
correct reciprocal dependence up to a logarithmic factor.

\paragraph{Attribution and assessment.}
The circle-colouring argument is the standard deduction
associated with Katznelson's formulation; see
\url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/894}, checked 24 September 2026.
This is a complete deduction from the stated Peres--Schlag
theorem, not a new-discovery claim or a local formal verification.
