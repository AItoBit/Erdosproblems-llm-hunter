\subsection*{2) WORK}
Let $t=\max_i v_2(n_i)$. If $t\ge1$, multiply the identity $\sum_i1/n_i=1$ by the least common multiple $L$. Modulo two, precisely the terms of valuation $t$ contribute one. Their number must therefore be even and at least two. Any two such denominators are $2^t$ times distinct odd integers, so they differ by at least $2^{t+1}$. Consequently
\[
2^{v_2(n_i)}\le W/2\qquad\text{for every even }n_i.
\]
Combining this with the supplied prime-power bound gives the stronger necessary divisibility
\[
n_i\mid 2^{\max\{0,\lfloor\log_2W\rfloor-1\}}
\prod_{\substack{p\le W\\p\text{ odd prime}}}p^{\lfloor\log_pW\rfloor}
\qquad(W\ge2).
\]
The greatest allowed power of two has been reduced by a factor two.

For an odd prime $p$, let $t$ be the maximum positive valuation and suppose exactly two denominators have valuation $t$, say $p^tu$ and $p^tv$, where $p\nmid uv$. Reduction of the same cleared identity modulo $p$, followed by cancellation of the common unit $L/p^t$, gives
\[
u^{-1}+v^{-1}\equiv0\pmod p,
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad u+v\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
In particular, if $p^t>W/2$, at most two multiples of $p^t$ fit in the interval. Both must occur and they are consecutive multiples: $v=u+1$. Thus
\[
2u+1\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
This is a residue restriction beyond merely demanding a repeated maximum valuation.

No inference from these necessary conditions to impossibility of dense configurations has been justified.
