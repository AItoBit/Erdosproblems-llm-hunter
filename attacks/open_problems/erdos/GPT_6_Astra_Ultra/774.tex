\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#774: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
If every finite subset $B$ of $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ contains a dissociated subset of size at least $\delta|B|$, must $A$ be a finite union of dissociated sets?

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{774.tex}.
The source correctly cites counterexamples to the analogous fixed-$h$-free assertion, where one side of a subset-sum relation has size at most $h$. Its broad claim that every proof using a fixed level is ruled out needs a qualification: a proof may still use additional consequences of the full dissociated-subset hypothesis. I prove the precise compactness condition that a positive argument would need.
\subsection{2) WORK}
\paragraph{A uniform-color compactness lemma.}
For a countable set $A$, the following are equivalent for a fixed integer $m\ge1$:
\[
A\text{ is a union of }m\text{ dissociated sets};
\qquad
\text{for every }h,\ A\text{ is a union of }m\ h\text{-free sets}. \tag{1}
\]
The forward implication is immediate. For the reverse, enumerate $A=\{a_1,a_2,\ldots\}$. Form a rooted tree whose level-$n$ nodes are $m$-colorings of $\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$ with each color class dissociated; edges are restriction. The assumption with $h=n$ supplies at least one such coloring, since every subset-sum equality on these $n$ elements has both sides of size at most $n$. Each node has at most $m$ children. A finitely branching tree with nodes at all depths has an infinite path: recursively select a child with descendants at unbounded depths. Along that path, every finite monochromatic subset-sum relation is excluded at some finite level. The limiting color classes are therefore dissociated.

Thus the exact missing uniformity is the number of colors $m$, independent of $h$. Merely knowing that each truncation has some finite cover $m(h)$ is insufficient for this argument. Equivalently, if $A$ is not a union of $m$ dissociated sets, some finite subset $F$ already cannot be $m$-colored into dissociated sets, and taking $h=|F|$ detects the obstruction at a finite level.

The published fixed-$h$ counterexample does not have the full proportional dissociated-subset property by hypothesis. Consequently it cannot by itself refute an argument using that stronger premise to obtain the uniform $m$ in (1).
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
The compactness proof fixes $m$ before taking the limit; allowing a fresh unbounded color palette at each level would invalidate finite branching. The $h$-free convention permits the other side of the relation to be arbitrarily large, as in the source paper. All constructions here are countable because $A\subseteq\mathbb N$.
\subsection{4) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED.}
No bound $m=m(\delta)$ uniform in the truncation depth is established. The lemma makes that gap exact and corrects an overbroad proof-obstruction claim, but does not settle the Pisier question.
