\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem \#236: independent continuation}
\noindent Date: 2026-09-23. Research attempt; no claim of a new published result.
\subsection{1) TARGET AND SOURCE AUDIT}
For $f(n)=\#\{k\ge0:n-2^k\text{ is prime}\}$, is $f(n)=o(\log n)$ uniformly as $n\to\infty$?

All supplied versions considered: \texttt{236.tex}.
The source obtains a second-moment bound, which permits sparse exceptions. I instead examine the pointwise congruence obstruction and its exact scope.
\subsection{2) WORK}
If $n$ is even, then for $k\ge1$, $n-2^k$ is even and can be prime only when it equals two. There is at most one such exponent. The case $k=0$ contributes at most one more, so $f(n)\le2$ for even $n$.

Now let $\ell$ be any odd prime and let $r$ be the multiplicative order of $2$ modulo $\ell$. If $n\bmod\ell$ belongs to the subgroup generated by $2$, there is exactly one exponent class $k\equiv k_0\pmod r$ for which $\ell\mid n-2^k$. Among these exponents at most one can contribute a prime, namely the exponent (if any) satisfying $2^k=n-\ell$. Writing $K=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$, we obtain
\[
f(n)\le (1-1/r)(K+1)+2.
\]
In particular for odd $n$ not divisible by three, $r=2$ gives $f(n)\le\tfrac12\log_2 n+O(1)$.

For several primes these forbidden exponent classes can be combined exactly modulo the least common multiple of their orders. If their union covers a proportion $\theta$ of exponent classes, then $f(n)\le(1-\theta)(K+1)+O(\operatorname{lcm}(r_1,\ldots,r_s)+s)$. This follows by counting complete periods and allowing one exceptional prime equality for each modulus.
\subsection{3) ADVERSARIAL VERIFICATION}
If $\ell\mid n$, then $n$ is outside the nonzero subgroup and this prime removes no exponent. In particular an odd $n$ divisible by many small primes defeats any fixed list of these tests. The modulus-dependent remainder prevents unrestricted growth of the list without further work.
\subsection{4) FINAL}
\textbf{UNRESOLVED.}
These pointwise bounds settle even $n$ and constrain many odd residue classes, but leave odd integers with strong divisibility by small primes. Neither the congruence argument nor the supplied moment bound excludes every exceptional sequence.
