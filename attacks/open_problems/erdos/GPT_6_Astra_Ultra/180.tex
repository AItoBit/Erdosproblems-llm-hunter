\section{Erd\H{o}s Problem 180: finite-family extremal compactness}

\subsection{1) Statement and scope}
For a finite family $\mathcal F$ of graphs, must there be some
$F\in\mathcal F$ for which
$\operatorname{ex}(n;\mathcal F)\gg\operatorname{ex}(n;F)$?
Here forbidden subgraphs are ordinary, not necessarily induced,
subgraphs. In its literal form the answer is no. We give the elementary
counterexample recorded on the current problem page, with exact
extremal counts. The more restrictive variants excluding these small
obstructions are separate questions.

\subsection{2) Two forbidden graphs whose combination is much stronger}
Take
\[
 \mathcal F=\{P_3,2K_2\},
\]
where $P_3$ has two adjacent edges and $2K_2$ has two disjoint edges.
Any graph with two distinct edges contains one of these: the edges
either share a vertex or they do not. Conversely a graph with just
one edge avoids both. Therefore, for $n\geq2$,
\[
 \operatorname{ex}(n;\mathcal F)=1.                    \tag{1}
\]
A graph is $P_3$-free exactly when its maximum degree is at most one.
It is therefore a union of isolated vertices and disjoint edges, and
\[
 \operatorname{ex}(n;P_3)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor.           \tag{2}
\]

\subsection{3) The other individual extremal number}
In a $2K_2$-free graph, every pair of edges intersects. If all edges
share one vertex, there are at most $n-1$ edges, attained by a star.
Otherwise, choose two edges $ab,ac$ and an edge not containing $a$.
To meet both chosen edges the latter edge must be $bc$.
Any further edge meeting all three of $ab,ac,bc$ must have both endpoints
in $\{a,b,c\}$: an edge containing an outside vertex misses the
opposite edge of the triangle. Thus in this second case there are at
most three edges, attained by a triangle together with isolated vertices.
It follows that, for $n\geq3$,
\[
 \operatorname{ex}(n;2K_2)=\max(n-1,3).                \tag{3}
\]
In particular this equals $n-1$ for $n\geq4$; at $n=4$ the star and
triangle constructions tie.

Combining (1)--(3), the ratio of the family extremal number to either
individual extremal number tends to zero. Since these are the only
members of $\mathcal F$, no member has the asserted comparison with
a positive constant independent of $n$.
